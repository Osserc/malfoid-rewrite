# Divergence Ledger

Every departure from canon. Anything not listed here follows the original books.

Entry format: **Status** · **Canon** (what the original does, in our own words) · **Ours** · **Why** (if it isn't obvious) · **Home** (where it's written out in full) · **Ripples** (affected files, open questions).

**Retired, never reuse:** DIV-033, DIV-034, DIV-036, DIV-042 — the trio split across three Houses and its knock-ons (whole-year classes, Sirius's second break-in, the Boggart lesson House by House), reverted 2026-10-27. Canon applies; the unity theme moved to DIV-055. The entries are in the git history.

### DIV-009 · The first meeting at Madam Malkin's

- **Status:** settled
- **Canon:** Draco brags about brooms and houses, asks about Harry's family and sneers at Hagrid. Harry gets out before giving his surname, disliking him.
- **Ours:** Sunlight on her hair reminds Harry of Disney's *Sleeping Beauty*, which he once watched in secret from behind the living-room door. When she asks who he is, he blurts out whether she's Sleeping Beauty. Then he notices she doesn't have the princess's purple eyes, and tells her hers are pretty too. She's had plenty of compliments, but never one this sincere and awestruck. It sours when she learns it's a Muggle story: flustered, she changes the subject by sneering at Hagrid. Harry leaves thinking the "princess" is cruel, and regrets being nice. Neither learns the other's name.

### DIV-011 · Dracaena is gifted and brave

- **Status:** settled
- **Canon:** Draco is a capable student who leans on his father's name and folds under real danger.
- **Ours:** An excellent potioneer, cunning, and a voracious reader on whatever grips her: poisons, the strange boy's stories. Competent and no coward, as befits wizard royalty.
  - **Potions:** the best potioneer in her year, and possibly in the castle, thanks to a six-year head start with Snape as her master (DIV-031). Hermione comes second. At least in Potions, she isn't the brightest witch of her generation.
  - **B1, the first Potions lesson:** Snape has her answer the questions Harry can't, to show her off. Hermione blurts out the answer to the third and loses points: right by the book, imprecise in practice. "Anything to add, Miss Malfoy?" Dracaena corrects her from years of experience and wins points for Slytherin. Hermione's hatred of her starts here.
  - **B6, the first Potions lesson:** Harry beats her at the Draught of Living Death using the Prince's notes. The sixth-year Prince's tricks are experimental and less elegant than the method the adult Snape taught her, and she has no drive left to one-up Harry: her priorities are elsewhere (DIV-015). She and Hermione both notice how much his new tricks resemble her technique. In the same lesson she knows the Amortentia and keeps her hand down (DIV-045).
- **Why:** her skill needs a source, and Hermione needs taking down a peg.

### DIV-012 · Muggle Studies: Burbage's best student

- **Status:** settled. Hermione dropping it is draft.
- **Canon:** Draco never takes Muggle Studies. Hermione takes it in B3 and drops it.

A timetable clash gives her the excuse. Dracaena frames it as Hermione conceding defeat.

### DIV-015 · Between B5 and B6: the death of faith

- **Status:** settled
- **Canon:** Draco takes the Dark Mark and the mission to kill Dumbledore, as punishment for Lucius's failure. He's proud of it until it crushes him.
- **Ours:** She is Marked and co-opted (canon default), but she's never proud of it: she's broken from the outset, ashamed of the Mark and defiled by it, a constant reminder of the chains that hold her. That summer she meets Voldemort for the first time, and her Occlumency (DIV-020) is no match for him: he shatters her walls with ease. He doesn't care that she "lied". He wants obedience and people broken into submission, and her love for Potter is only more suffering to inflict. She realises her prince can't possibly win, because Voldemort is too powerful. The dream was never real. She sinks into despair and panic and goes along with the assassination plot, to at least save her parents. She's back in the cage, and the promise she just made is broken. Back at school she doesn't end it at once: for a few weeks she's distant and hollow, only physical with Harry, and she quits the Quidditch team. When Flitwick reopens the Duelling Club, Harry offers it as a date. She refuses, and their fight ends in the breakup, brutal enough to lead in the end to Sectumsempra. B6 is the nightmare.

### DIV-017 · B3: her play-acting, and what it costs

- **Status:** settled
- **Canon:** Draco milks his hippogriff injury for weeks. Slytherin uses it to postpone their match against Gryffindor, and he makes Harry and Ron prepare his Potions ingredients. He also joins the fake-Dementor prank at the Ravenclaw match.
- **Ours:**
  - **The injury:** she milks it for weeks too. She's not above subterfuge and pettiness when it comes to throwing darts at her target.
  - **The match:** she uses the injury to delay the match against Harry, because after losing in B2 she knows she needs more practice.
  - **Potions:** she badgers Harry into preparing her ingredients and turns him into her servant. When his poor technique spoils the potion she steps in, strangely sweet under the barbs. In her head they're a married couple cooking together, until she realises she's daydreaming in front of the whole class and scrambles to save face. Harry is weirded out.
  - **The lesson:** later she realises her play-acting gave Lucius the fuel to have Buckbeak executed. That lesson in consequences is what pushes her to save him.
  - **The prank:** she takes no part in the fake-Dementor prank at the Ravenclaw match. Crabbe and Goyle pull it on their own initiative, thinking she'll approve, and are confused when she greatly disapproves. Kicking him while he's down isn't her game ([lap-pillow](../scenes/b3/lap-pillow.md)).
  - **The Boggart:** with her wand arm in a sling, she sits out her own class's Boggart lesson. Harry never sees it: canon's lesson is Gryffindor's, and Slytherin isn't in the room. Lupin later goads her into a remedial lesson: she'd have faced a Boggart when Harry never has. She freezes in front of her father's disgust, alone with Lupin, and tells no one until Harry.

### DIV-018 · B3 climax: the swarm, the confession, the flight to Sirius

- **Status:** settled
- **Canon:** The Dementors converge on the lake, drawn by Black. Harry is saved by a Patronus his time-travelling self casts. Then he and Hermione fly Buckbeak to free Sirius.
- **Ours:**
  - **The lead:** to make room to free Buckbeak, she plants a fake lead with the Ministry that draws the Dementors to the lake (how: Q-025).
  - **The lake:** she can't control Buckbeak and ends up at the lake herself. She knows the Patronus Charm, having watched Harry attempt it and studied it for him, but she tries and can't cast it (DIV-027).
  - **The Patronus:** Harry re-evaluates her. She stole Buckbeak to save him, the lap pillow and her Patronus study weren't jokes, and now she's trying to drive the Dementors off herself: there's something more to this girl. His Patronus drives off the swarm and saves everyone, powered by the memory of the lap-pillow scene. As he casts there's a first flicker of resonance between them (DIV-021): love, however vague, is already there. How it works is open (Q-028).
  - **The confession:** under the Dementors' influence she breaks down sobbing and confesses, unprompted, that the swarm is her fault.
  - **Hermione's idea:** ask her to fly Buckbeak to Sirius.
  - **Why she agrees:** she owes Harry, and knows it. And she's just been thrust into the fairy tale: her prince drove off a horde of demons, for her.
  - **Sirius:** he recognises Narcissa's daughter, and they talk briefly. Maybe the Black family made something good after all, if Narcissa gave birth to a girl this brave.

### DIV-020 · Occlumency: the Malfoys, and Snape's lessons

- **Status:** settled
- **Canon:** Draco is an Occlumens, taught by his aunt Bellatrix (mentioned in B6). Lucius isn't shown to be one.
- **Ours:**
  - **The family:** no one is born an Occlumens, but the discipline the Malfoys instil lends itself to learning it.
  - **Lucius** is a skilled Occlumens. He can't lie to Voldemort's face as Snape does, but in their regular B5 meetings he tweaks what Voldemort perceives through passive Legilimency. He tells Voldemort he has planted his daughter as a spy on Potter (DIV-013). Voldemort never comes to the Manor before B6.
  - **Dracaena** learns Occlumency from Snape in the summer between B4 and B5 (the author has the hook). Snape's cover holds: he's a fine Occlumens, and he has plausible deniability with Lucius, since he trained her as a general precaution for someone working under cover, not to deceive Voldemort.
  - **It isn't enough.** In summer 1996 Voldemort shatters her walls with ease (DIV-015). In the final duel she uses it another way (DIV-021).
- **Why:** Legilimency isn't mind reading, and Voldemort is very arrogant. That leaves Lucius room to tweak and Snape room to hide.

### DIV-021 · Resonance

- **Status:** settled. The link rule in its home is draft.
- **Canon:** Love works as magic through sacrifice: Lily's death protects Harry, and his in B7 protects the defenders. In B5 Voldemort can't stay in Harry's mind once it fills with love. Wands bond strangely through twin cores (Priori Incantatem, B4).
- **Ours:** A wand resonates when two people wield it in total sync, and their magic becomes exponentially stronger. It's literally the power of love: friendship isn't enough, or it would be more common. It's obscure, extremely rare and extremely hard to pull off.
  - **Sync:** both draw the same radicals (DIV-007) at the same instant, with no leader. Harry and Dracaena can because of years of feuding and duelling (DIV-028).
  - **Volatile:** no instant win. It shows cracks, but it holds.
  - **Occlumency:** in resonance Dracaena wraps Harry's mind in hers. Voldemort's Legilimency finds nothing but her love for Harry.
  - **The stained soul:** until the forest, Harry's Horcrux blocks it. In near-misses she's hurt by the stained, mutilated soul she senses in him, without either of them knowing what it is.
  - **How it builds:** a first flicker at the B3 lake (DIV-018). Beyond that it's kept vague until a dedicated session (Q-028).
  - **Nobody knows:** in school no one knows what resonance is. Dracaena finds the rule by research and tells no one.
- **Note:** builds on 🔒 DIV-006 and 🔒 DIV-007 without changing them.
- **Home:** [setting/magic.md](../setting/magic.md)
- **Ripples:** Q-012 (answered), Q-014, Q-024, Q-028 · DIV-018, DIV-023 · [threads/harry-dracaena.md](../threads/harry-dracaena.md)

### DIV-023 · The final sequence

- **Status:** settled. Harry's broken wand is draft.
- **Canon:** Narcissa declares Harry dead and Hagrid carries his body to the castle. Neville defies Voldemort, who sets the Sorting Hat burning on his head. Neville pulls the sword from it and kills Nagini, and the battle resumes into the Great Hall, where Voldemort duels McGonagall, Slughorn and Shacklebolt at once. He throws everything at them, Legilimency included, along with the risen dead and the Dementors, which Neville and the DA hold off (DIV-025). He ends up cornered. His Killing Curse meets their Expelliarmus and rebounds. No wand technicalities decide it (DIV-024): together, they are simply stronger.

### DIV-024 · No wand allegiance

- **Status:** settled. The Elder Wand's own lore is open (Q-011).
- **Canon:** A wand's allegiance passes to whoever defeats its owner, by disarming or overpowering them, and a won wand works better for its new owner. The Elder Wand's chain (Dumbledore → Draco → Harry) decides the final duel.
- **Ours:** Purged. Wands have no allegiance that passes by conquest. The Elder Wand is the exception, with lore of its own: it passes by blood, through killing. So Voldemort killing Snape is the correct move, and pointless (DIV-023). Only the idea is set.
- **Home:** [setting/magic.md](../setting/magic.md)
- **Ripples:** Q-011, Q-027 · DIV-023

### DIV-025 · Neville holds the line

- **Status:** settled
- **Canon:** After killing Nagini, Neville disappears into the general fighting. When Harry and Voldemort face off in the Great Hall, everyone falls silent to watch, and nothing threatens the duel.
- **Ours:** Voldemort can't get past the resonance, so he turns the battlefield against them. With the Elder Wand at full power he raises every body in the Great Hall as an Inferius, friend and foe alike: he sees only bodies, and any will do. He calls back the Dementors Dracaena drove off earlier (DIV-027) and sets the whole horde on the duellists, who are locked on him and can't defend themselves. Neville stands against it with his own wand and the sword of Gryffindor, which burns through the Inferi, and keeps fighting as his injuries mount. Surrounded and down on one knee, he hears Godric Gryffindor tell him to stand his ground as Gryffindor's heir. He gets up and rallies the DA, who raise walls of flame against the Inferi, and together they shield Harry and Dracaena long enough for them to win. On the way he sees Bellatrix and lets her go, because he's needed to protect his friends: he too chooses love and justice over vengeance. He survives.

### DIV-035 · A small, shrinking people

- **Status:** draft
- **Canon:** The numbers contradict each other. Harry's year is about forty pupils, and every subject has a single teacher, which only works for a school of a few hundred. Rowling has also said Hogwarts holds about a thousand, and the castle is built on that scale. How the wizarding economy works is never explained.
- **Ours:** Wizarding Britain is small (roughly ten thousand people, as an illustration) and shrinking. Canon's single institutions are what a community that size looks like.
  - **The long decline:** since the Statute of Secrecy, people have married out and drifted into the Muggle world, and the pure-blood lines have thinned. The castle was built for a far larger magical population.
  - **The war dip:** the first war hollowed out the cohorts born in it, so everyone at Hogwarts during the series is war-born, and those years are proportionally more Muggle-born. A post-war boom follows.
  - **The economy:** magic multiplies labour, the Ministry is sized to police the border with the Muggles, and the economy lives off the Muggle one.
- **Why:** the canon problem needed an answer ([problems/scale.md](../problems/scale.md)). It gives DIV-008 hard numbers: wizarding Britain really is disappearing.

### DIV-066 · The Snitch carries the score

- **Status:** settled
- **Canon:** The Snitch is worth 150 points and ends the match. One catch decides almost every game, so the Seeker is the only player who matters and the other six play for pride. The Quidditch Cup goes on points scored across the season, and Quidditch feeds the House Cup.
- **Ours:** The Snitch is worth 30, still ends the match, and **carries the score**: every point either side puts up weighs it down, so it begins the match untouchable and ends it catchable. The Seeker stops waiting on luck and starts deciding when the match ends, with an opponent trying to decide otherwise.
  - **The burden is the combined score,** which makes every goal double-edged: scoring is how you come back, and scoring is what arms the other Seeker to end the match on you.
  - **Nobody can sit on a lead,** because only goals bring the Snitch into reach.
  - **The closing move.** Both Seekers want it when the margin is under 30; only the leader wants it when the margin is over. Catching it from behind is a concession, to stop the bleeding and save the aggregate.
  - **What a Seeker does:** no bat, and no touching the Quaffle. Disruption is bluff, not force, and leaving your opposite number costs you the seconds she's unmarked, so the two of them glue themselves together without any rule saying so.
  - **The numbers.** Goals stay at 10 and ordinary match totals stay modest, so 30 is three goals: a real swing, never a game in itself. A decider runs longer and higher, because the Cup margin forces the leader to keep attacking: the B3 final ends 140–50 (DIV-069).
  - **The Quidditch Cup** goes on aggregate, as in canon. **The House Cup** takes a flat prize per match won, never the match score, so the change doesn't touch it.
  - **The deadlock.** A scoreless match never weighs the Snitch down and never ends, which is where the legendary months-long games came from.
- **Why:** at 150 the Seeker is a lottery ticket. At 30 the job is a decision with an opponent attached, which is what the rivalry needs, and the other six players finally have a reason to exist: Chasers run the clock, not just the score.

### DIV-068 · Brooms amplify the rider

- **Status:** settled
- **Canon:** Brooms are a ladder. A better broom is faster and better in every way, the Slytherin team's Nimbus 2001s are bought advantage, and a Firebolt outclasses everything on the pitch.
- **Ours:** Power and obedience come together, so a broom transmits its rider rather than carrying them. Cheap brooms are forgiving, stable and slow; top brooms are responsive to the point of wildness and do exactly what they're asked, including what the rider didn't mean to ask. Money buys amplification, not performance, and the gap between two top brooms shows only at the very edge of what either can do.
  - **Temperament decides fit.** A wild broom suits Harry, who flies the way he duels. It's the wrong instrument for Dracaena, who is precise and controlled, so a Firebolt would make her slower (DIV-069).
  - **Assistance, not just obedience.** The two top lines differ in kind, not rank. The Nimbus line's flying charm is *opinionated*: it manages the small corrections — angling into a turn, holding a line — so the rider can spend attention on scouting and duelling, and it quietly declines an instruction it reads as a mistake. A Firebolt does nothing for you and refuses nothing. It obeys, including stray thoughts, so riding one is a constant tax on attention, and the reward is that nothing is off the table.
  - **So the better broom costs more to fly.** Harry spends the whole B3 final minding his Firebolt and is nearly spent by the end; Dracaena never once fights hers. He wins on the one move where he stops minding it and simply trusts it (DIV-069).
  - **It explains canon.** Lucius buys seven top brooms and his team loses on older ones anyway, Dracaena included in B2.
- **Why:** without it, Quidditch is decided by whose father spends the most, which is a poor foundation for a rivalry and worse for the B3 final, where she has to lose fairly. This fixes it without regulating equipment, so canon's Firebolt subplot survives untouched.

She baits Hermione deliberately and doesn't flinch when the slap lands: she thinks she has it coming. Hermione sees only the jeer; **Harry sees her face**, and isn't sure what he saw. A faint alarm bell.
  - **Flint's strategy.** Slytherin foul their way through the final as in canon, and here it is a plan rather than spite. Flint knows his Chasers are the weaker side, so he stops playing for the match and plays for the aggregate: batter the Gryffindor Chasers, hold the margin under the Cup line, and have his Seeker end the game early. It costs him the match and would have won him the Cup.
  - **The clean player.** She is the only Slytherin who doesn't foul, and Harry gets two different versions of it. In private, mid-air, she tells him she doesn't want to win like this and that some of them have dignity — sincerity only he hears, and he has no reason to credit it. In public she finally snaps and flies at her own Beater, and what the stadium sees is not principle but a tantrum about her match being taken from her. He has both halves and can't reconcile them, which is the point (Q-035).
  - **Two moments neither of them takes.** At the most catchable the Snitch has been all match, she leaves her post to scream at her Beater and he leaves his to check on his Chaser. Neither notices. It is the first time either has chosen something over the Snitch, and it is the behaviour she gets accused of in B5 (DIV-067).
  - **The margin, not the lead.** Gryffindor are ahead almost throughout and it never helps them: they need to be 60 clear before Harry may take the Snitch, or Slytherin keep the Cup on aggregate. Every Slytherin goal pushes that line back by a goal. Harry's job is therefore the same all match — stop her ending it — and the phases belong to her, not to him.
  - **Her phases.** For most of the match she hunts freely: catching the Snitch caps Gryffindor's margin and saves the Cup, which means losing the match on purpose, and she does it willingly and hates it. Once Gryffindor reach the margin her job inverts — she can no longer take her eyes off him and has to mark instead of hunt. Her rage is at being contained, not at being confused.
  - **The fake-out.** The margin comes within reach, Harry breaks for the Snitch, she goes with him — and her own Chasers score, which pushes the finish line back a goal and forces him to break off. She is saved by her team without their knowing it. He turns and gives her a smug look: *I could end this whenever I want.*
  - **The real duel** is over in a moment. She sticks far closer this time, having seen his acceleration, and it isn't enough.
  - **Why she loses.** Two things, and neither is equipment. **She is legible to him**, because she has watched him fly for two years and he has learned what the watching looks like from the inside: he baits the trick she is waiting for. And **she will not be reckless.** The winning move is an improvisation she finds distasteful, not one she lacks the skill for (DIV-070). At the lake she says so herself, unprompted: she would never have done it, it's uncouth.
  - **The cost is his.** He pays all match, minding a broom that obeys every stray thought, and is nearly spent by the end; she never fights hers (DIV-068). He wins on the one move where he stops minding it.
  - **Nothing to blame.** A better broom wouldn't have helped her, and the reader has watched the better broom cost Harry all afternoon. She never reaches for the excuse: what she names at the lake is taste, not equipment, which is the more damning answer because it is about who she is. Lucius doesn't offer her a Firebolt either, before or after — he celebrates achievement, he doesn't console failure.
  - **The gloves.** They come onto the pitch wearing each other's — she in a Seeker's glove she won off him at the Duelling Club, he in one of hers with her initial stitched into the palm (DIV-059). It is the first time the trophies are visible to anyone else, it is played as a joke by both, and neither mentions them again. He catches the Snitch with her glove on his hand.
  - **The hair tie.** It comes loose mid-duel and he takes it out of the air without deciding to — his broom giving him the reach because it obeys what he hasn't said (DIV-068) — and pockets it. At the lake he gives it back, and she turns round and starts to ask him to tie it for her before Madam Hooch arrives and cuts her off. Being interrupted is what lets her pretend tomorrow that she never asked.
  - **The crack opens.** The school already jokes about them; this is where her own house stops laughing. Mid-match she answers a jibe of his with a Muggle cheer — Muggle Studies showing in the worst possible place (DIV-012) — and the Slytherin stands go quiet and scowl. She calls her own Beater inbred in front of witnesses (DIV-070), abandons her post to do it, and ends the day taking Harry's hand on the pitch. It is written home about. B5's accusation lands on ground this prepares (Q-081).
  - **Alone on the pitch.** Her team desert her. She sits there staring at her hands and cries: she played it straight, spent everything she had, and lost anyway.
  - **The hand.** Harry, hauled off by his own side, looks back, sees her alone, and goes to her. He crouches and holds out a hand: he had a good time flying against her, and can they do it again? It mirrors the train, where she offered hers and he didn't take it (DIV-009). She wants to believe he's being snide and can't, and takes it.
  - **The race.** He's off his broom and on it again, challenging her to a race, and she chases him. They both get detention.
  - **The mask goes back on** the next morning, colder than ever, which confuses Harry badly. The same reflex as the Potions daydream (DIV-017).
- **Why:** it answers Q-035 with the two strongest supporting scenes in B3, and it gives her the second half of her Buckbeak motive: the guilt makes her want to act, and this makes her believe acting straight will work (DIV-003, [buckbeak-rescue](../scenes/b3/buckbeak-rescue.md)).

### DIV-070 · Dracaena's objections are aesthetic

- **Status:** draft
- **Canon:** Draco's prejudice is plain bigotry with no reasoning under it, and his snobbery is a character flaw and nothing more.
- **Ours:** her taste is her moral channel. Things are wrong to her because they are *ugly*, beneath her, unbecoming of what she thinks she is, and that single principle runs most of what she does.
  - **How it shows.** Slytherin's fouling disgusts her because it is squalid, not because it is unfair. Buckbeak's execution offends her because butchering a beast on a grudge does not befit nobility. Her insult of choice, under real anger, is *inbred* (B3 final), and *uncouth* is what she calls the move that beats her.
  - **It is a way out of the ideology, not a rejection of it.** She does not decide blood purity is wrong. She accepts the premise and notices her own class failing it: the lines have thinned, the specimens in front of her at her own table are not impressive, and she can see it (DIV-008, DIV-035). Crabbe and Goyle are her standing private evidence; she keeps them because they are useful and because it is expected.
  - **The same move as Muggle Studies.** Cold evaluation on merit rather than loyalty, applied to her own side instead of the other one (DIV-012). Two exits from the same door, both running on her snobbery rather than against it.
  - **It is also her ceiling.** She loses the B3 final because winning required an improvisation she finds distasteful, and she says so at the lake. Her taste is her compass and the thing that stops her: in B5 she loses again to something her code does not price (enjoying herself), and in B6 Harry grabs a spell he doesn't know — the same recklessness she called uncouth — and it nearly kills her (DIV-032, DIV-043).
- **Why:** it makes her bigotry arguable rather than inert, gives her a route out that doesn't require a conversion scene, and turns the one arena she can never win into a fact about her character instead of a fact about Harry's broom (DIV-069).

### DIV-071 · Her cruelty is real, and Harry sees it

- **Status:** draft
- **Canon:** Draco is nasty to everyone more or less equally, and the reader is never invited to distinguish between kinds of nastiness.
- **Ours:** what she aims at Harry is a game. What she aims at anyone else is not, and the difference is visible.
  - **The B3 final.** She turns on her own Beater and Harry, a few feet away, watches her face come apart with it. He has seen her angry at him a hundred times and this is nothing like it: she looks as though she would strangle the man. It is ugly, and it frightens him a little.
  - **What it tells him, and what he does with it.** Nothing, yet. It sits alongside everything else he can't place about her that year.
  - **Why it matters later.** Her becoming *dere* for Harry does not remove the darkness; it only points it elsewhere. It is still there in B6 when she goes after Hermione to make him hate her, and it is the reason that relapse reads as her rather than as a plot requirement ([sleeping-beauty](../threads/sleeping-beauty.md)).
  - **The exclusive game, from the other side.** It is the companion to DIV-029: their feud is a private arrangement, and what she gives everyone else is the real thing.
- **Why:** without it the softening across B3–B5 turns her into a sweetheart who was only ever pretending, which the style guide warns against, and the B6 relapse has nothing to stand on.

### DIV-072 · Painted life: the Dessendre gift

- **Status:** settled
- **Canon:** Wizarding paintings all move and talk, and nobody distinguishes between kinds. Portraits are furniture and occasionally useful.
- **Ours:** canon's portraits are unchanged — echoes with manners, not people. Against them stands one exception: the Dessendres, an extinct French family whose ancestral gift made paintings that were actually alive. Theirs is the only real painted life there has ever been.
  - **Fenced by its own nature.** Ancestral and blood-bound, so there is nothing to learn; not spellcasting, so radicals don't touch it (DIV-007); extinct for about a century before B1, so there is nobody to ask. A canvas is also trivially destroyed from outside, which is why almost none of it survived. Nobody hides from a war in a painted world because the people who could make one are dead.
  - **The story.** The son died; the mother painted her family back and went in after them; the father went in after *her* and lost years to it; the younger daughter, Alicia, followed him in to help and hadn't the skill to get out again. The elder, Clea, never went in — she acted from outside to end it, failed, left them to it, and regretted it for twenty years. The end came from outside and had nothing to do with any of it — the manor burned, and the line ended there. How Verso died is never stated or asked. How it went for the people inside stays vague on purpose, under two constraints: no creature turning on its maker, and the fire is an ordinary accident rather than a verdict (Q-086).
  - **Being inside.** It wastes a living body by degrees, over years; the painted themselves are untouched by it. **You can leave whenever you like** — a canvas is not a trap, so Aline stayed because she wanted to and Renoir stayed because she wouldn't come. The exception is skill: getting out is the painter's craft, and Alicia hadn't got it. This makes the Dessendres a story about choosing rather than about being caught, which is the same story as B6.
  - **It lives in memory, not in a library.** The true version was never written down where anyone could find it; it is a story the old houses tell because the Dessendres were their sort. Hermione's books have the magic and the fire and no people at all. That is DIV-008's cleanest demonstration — what her class has is unbroken memory, not better scholarship — and it licenses the vagueness, because a told story has gaps nobody can check.
  - **The portraits despise it.** They will not discuss painted life, because a portrait that admits it exists is admitting what it is. It is why the one surviving canvas has hung unmentioned for a century, and why the ghosts, not the paintings, are who you ask.
  - **Renoir is Harry's parallel, and the more dangerous one.** Aline retreated into the dream, which is Dracaena. Renoir went in for the best reason there is, to bring someone he loved out, and the painting took him anyway. Harry daydreams too, but forward (DIV-043); Renoir is the warning that forward-facing and selfless is not enough on its own. B6 and B7 put Harry in his exact position, and Harry does what he could not: he doesn't follow her under, he gets her out (DIV-049, DIV-052).
  - **Dracaena has the true version** and takes the opposite lesson from Harry's: not a warning, a failed experiment. A technique that nearly worked, used badly. *Why not paintings?* is a question the girl who answers everything with a method has genuinely asked (DIV-011).
  - **One object.** Clea's landscape hangs unregarded in the fifth-floor corridor to the prefects' bathroom: creatures she painted as a girl, before any of the tragedy, still waiting for a mistress who grew up and stopped coming. The one who speaks is a headless clay figure grinding boulders in his own chest to finish a castle for her, and she never gave him a name. It survives only because it was out of the house when the manor burned. The staff don't know what it is, the portraits won't say, and the ghosts will if asked properly.
  - **No Dessendre ever appears on the page**, in any book. History and one painting, not a cast.
- **Why:** canon asserts the Mirror's lesson and never demonstrates it. This demonstrates it at the scale the series needs — not a boy who stared too long but a family who could actually build the dream and were destroyed anyway — and gives the escapism theme a case outside Dracaena's head that Harry carries from B1. It also feeds the decline (DIV-008, DIV-035): one more thing the wizarding world used to be able to do.

### DIV-073 · B1: the Dessendres, and the corridor

- **Status:** settled
- **Canon:** Dumbledore tells Harry that men have wasted away before the Mirror, not knowing whether what it showed was real or possible. The warning is an assertion, and Harry takes it on trust. Nothing follows from it.
- **Ours:** he gives it a name — the Dessendres were nearly ruined by a magic like this one — and Harry goes and looks.
  - **Why he reaches for a hundred-year-old French family.** Not because it is famous; by B1 the public barely has the story (DIV-072). Because it is what *he* thinks about. He is half telling on himself, to a boy who has just seen his dead parents, and it reads differently on a reread after B7.
  - **Hermione delivers the public version**, with relish, and the public version is thin: the magic and the fire, with no people in it. Harry handed her the name to get rid of her; she was already digging into magic like the Mirror on his behalf, and comes back with a lead he never asked for.
  - **Dracaena is in it, as the guide.** She has the true version from family memory and nobody else in the castle does, which is what she is doing in the scene. She is not a dreamer here and does not go back to the painting — the compulsive returning still belongs to B6. What she brings is knowledge, snobbery and a verdict.
  - **The hunt.** The portraits are useless, because they despise painted life and won't say so. Dracaena asks a ghost instead, charms Sir Nicholas blind inside a minute, and has the location. First demonstration that she can work any room she likes — and cannot say a civil word to the boy she wants ([dracaena-malfoy](../characters/dracaena-malfoy.md)).
  - **The slur.** Hermione's delight at a marvellous extinct magic is, to her, a stranger poking at her family's dead. See DIV-074.
  - **The argument.** Dracaena tells the true story; Hermione takes Renoir's side; Dracaena answers that Aline got what she was after and merely had to ration her time better. Harry hears both, wants Dracaena's to be true, and does not settle it — the creature does (DIV-072, Q-085).
  - **The painting.** Clea's landscape, fifth-floor corridor to the prefects' bathroom. The creature talks about his mistress in the present tense, describes a routine, and has no idea how long ago it stopped.
  - **The offer, and the refusal.** Harry offers to come back and visit. He is refused — *mistress will come* — because accepting him would mean admitting she won't. What puts him beyond help is his own hope, and it is Dracaena who tells Harry to stop trying. In B6 she is the one refusing the help.
  - **Her crack.** She ends it sad and glassy-eyed and walks off unsteadily, and Harry has no idea what he saw. First deposit in the account Q-035 draws on, and it must stay deniable — no visible crying.
  - **What Harry takes from it.** Two things. The reason he never again wants to live in a dream, demonstrated rather than asserted. And the harder one: magic like this reaches past the person who works it, and some things can't be taken back.
  - **The flaw.** He is shown it at eleven and still isn't careful. It comes due at Sectumsempra — an unknown spell, cast in anger, permanent damage (DIV-032). Never joined up in the text; the reader does it or doesn't.
- **Why:** it turns a B1 one-liner into the spine of the escapism theme and plants B5, B6 and B7 at once. Putting her in it costs nothing and buys three things: the warning reaches Harry from the girl who will make the same mistake, the reader meets her competence before her cruelty has an explanation, and the hand that shuts down his rescue in B1 is the one that refuses rescue in B6.

### DIV-074 · B1: the first Mudblood, and Harry doesn't know the word

- **Status:** draft
- **Canon:** Draco calls Hermione a Mudblood in B2, at the Quidditch pitch. Ron's fury and his backfiring curse are what teach Harry, and the reader, how bad the word is.
- **Ours:** it lands a year earlier, in the Dessendre corridor, and it has an argument inside it.
  - **The provocation.** Hermione, thrilled, calls the Dessendres interesting and says she would have loved to talk to them. To Dracaena that is a stranger treating her people's dead as a curio to be poked at and forgotten, and it is the one thing she cannot answer politely.
  - **What she says.** That this is why Potter should choose his friends more carefully; that these Mudbloods have no respect for traditions and accomplishments that are not theirs. The grievance is real and is aimed at Hermione's *method*, not her blood — which does not make it less of a slur, and is exactly why it isn't a cheap one. A live demonstration of DIV-008 out of an eleven-year-old's mouth.
  - **Nobody defends her.** Hermione goes still and digs her nails into Harry's arm. Harry feels it, gives her a worried look, files the whole thing under Malfoy being arrogant, and moves on — **because he does not know what the word means.** Canon has him learn it in B2 from Ron; here he simply hasn't got it yet, so the slur lands, hurts, and passes unanswered in front of him.
  - **The aftermath.** He finds out later, off the page or on it, and is furious with her — which to Dracaena is Potter turning on her for calling a spade a spade, the day after she thought they had got on. Another clumsy approach punished, and more fuel for the grudge. Who tells him and when: Q-092.
  - **Hermione says nothing about it.** She does not bring her own injury to the boy she has appointed herself to look after. That silence is the big-sister engine, not forbearance ([hermione-granger](../characters/hermione-granger.md)).
- **Why:** the slur stops being a label and becomes an argument, which is what DIV-008 needs, and Harry's ignorance lets it land on the reader without being resolved on the page. It also sets up B4, where he will not tolerate a second one (DIV-038), and B6, where she goes back to it on purpose to make him hate her (DIV-015).
