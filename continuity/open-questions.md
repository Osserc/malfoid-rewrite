### Q-011 · The Elder Wand and the Hallows (DIV-006, DIV-024)

In canon the curse rebounds because of the Elder Wand's allegiance. If love is the mechanism, what's left of the allegiance chain and of the Hallows plot?

*Partly answered:* allegiance is purged (DIV-024). The Elder Wand keeps lore of its own and passes by blood, through killing, so Voldemort killing Snape is the correct move, and pointless. Only the idea is set.

**Inherited from DIV-103:** canon starts the hunt because Harry's wand beats Lucius's borrowed one in the Seven Potters chase — and we no longer have that beat, because Moody's stand is what gets Harry out and the allegiance logic behind it is gone anyway. **So the hunt needs its own reason to start.** The author's standing lean: *Voldemort wanted the thing already*, and this is the year he finally moves on it — an ambition acted on rather than a reaction to a defective wand. To be settled with the rest of the wand lore.

Still open:
- **The canon chain breaks.** Grindelwald stole the wand from Gregorovitch without killing him, and Dumbledore beat Grindelwald without killing him. Options floated:
  - A general "violence" rule, so stealing counts. Risk: disarming is violence too, which brings the disarming chain back, starting with Dracaena disarming Dumbledore on the tower.
  - Retcon: Grindelwald kills Gregorovitch. Doesn't fix Dumbledore, and changes canon's B7 visions of Voldemort hunting Gregorovitch.
  - Claude's proposal: the wand serves at full strength only for someone who killed its previous owner, and in anyone else's hand it's just a very good wand. Grindelwald and Dumbledore held it asleep (Dumbledore by choice: he refused to kill Grindelwald). Snape's kill wakes it, and it underperforms for Voldemort, as in canon, until he kills Snape. No retcons needed. The author isn't convinced yet.
- **Who masters it after the final duel.** Proposal: no one, since Voldemort dies by his own rebounded curse.
- **What's left of the Hallows plot.** *Author's direction:* the Hallows are a red herring, and their moral, accepting death, runs against ours: living for love (DIV-048). They're Dumbledore's story, not ours ([themes](../core/themes.md)).

### Q-099 · Who is Hogwarts's household? (DIV-075, DIV-077)

### Q-100 · Hermione's method (DIV-078, DIV-075)

**The big one, and still open.** A way for a bound elf to recover its natural state over time, inside a household that has decided never to use what it holds. Not freeing, which kills them. No mechanism is settled; the author is thinking about it. What follows is what is fixed while that happens.

**What she has that nobody has ever had**

- **A hole in the archive** where the history should be (B5).
- **A working anomaly she can study.** Dobby is a thousand-year-old oversight eating toast at the Burrow: a master with no house, which the original casters never imagined and made no provision for. **The curse has a bug, so it has seams, so it can be worked.** She sees the first symptom years early, when Dobby gets Mrs Black's portrait off a wall no elf should be able to touch ([kreacher-and-the-manor](../scenes/b7/kreacher-and-the-manor.md)).

**Fixed points** *(settled)*

- **There is nothing to break.** The binding was cast once and what it sits on now is the elves; there is no seat of it, no artefact, nothing to find and destroy. The only instrument still standing is the house (DIV-075). Grimmauld Place is the seat of *Kreacher's* binding, in that sense and no other.
- **It is a finesse job.** Precision, research, rules-lawyering. She is not powerful and the method must not need power: she finds the seam rather than beating the magic.
- **It is not a repeat of what Harry did, and could not be.** There is no procedure in that corridor at all: what happened was one elf changing his mind because he was suddenly given something to hope for (DIV-075, DIV-076). Nobody watching would have learned a technique, because there wasn't one. All it tells her is that an elf is alive who should be dead, therefore the magic has seams — and nothing whatever about how to open one. Whatever she builds stands on its own mechanism, and this makes her job harder rather than easier.
- **And liberty is not the goal.** Elves do not want to be free; an elf is a spirit of the hearth and wants a family worth nursing (DIV-075). So the method cannot be an emancipation, and S.P.E.W. is named for the wrong thing by a girl whose instincts are nonetheless right.
- **The cost is houses.** Whatever it turns out to be, performing it ends the house the elf is leaving. Grimmauld Place dies when Kreacher goes — mechanically, not sentimentally — and his consent is consent to end the house of Black (DIV-078).

**Why the cost stays** *(settled)*

It is the whole political payload, and it cuts both ways on purpose.

- **It cannot be scaled.** Old houses are the only thing that ever made elves, and they are already disappearing (DIV-035). Every elf saved costs one of the few left, so running the method widely finishes what the ancient wizards started. There is no version of this where she simply wins.
- **It hands the purebloods live ammunition.** Dracaena's standing grievance is that Mudbloods respect nothing and will take an axe to what they have not troubled to understand — the grievance she first voices in the Dessendre corridor (DIV-074, [painted-life](../setting/painted-life.md)). And here is a Muggle-born whose life's work is dismantling the oldest houses in Britain, for the sake of house-elves of all things. They are bound to hate her more than they already do, and they have a point, and it is the best one they will ever get.
- **And she is right anyway.** The book gives the objection its full weight and does not let it win. → [society](../setting/society.md), [hermione-granger](../characters/hermione-granger.md), [dracaena-malfoy](../characters/dracaena-malfoy.md)

**Still open**

- **The mechanism.** Deliberately parked. *Ruled out:* inducing the glitch on purpose — release and catch inside the dying window, to a person holding a house the elf is not bound to — because that is a reproduction of Harry's accident, which does not extend. *Still live:* rediscovering **transfer**, which the old magic could do and nobody now can, though that only moves an elf and does not restore one; and whether the household's character is doing all the real work anyway, since an elf is made of what its household is.
- **Whether she ever publishes,** which decides whether the ending is one elf or a beginning for all of them.
- **Whether Harry ever realises** she built it out of something he did at twelve without understanding it.
- **Dracaena's part, after the story** *(post-story, not canon)*: the author's lean is that she ends up campaigning for Hermione behind closed doors, in the rooms Hermione cannot get into. It is earned in B4 and nowhere else — she is the one pureblood in Britain who has stood in a kitchen and watched a dismissed elf kill itself, instead of being told it went away (DIV-077, Q-098). Q-096.

**Settled and upgraded 2026-11-25** · DIV-129. She is not a witness: **she kills the basilisk with him**, and he would have died without her. ### Q-114 · The clue chain for Moody (DIV-086)

Canon rewarded the rereader with the flask, the tongue and the ferret. The disguise is gone, so those have to be replaced with tells that read as Moody on a first pass and as Crouch on a second.

**The throughline the author wants: Barty Crouch Jr is a person, not only a cultist.** He went to Azkaban very young and has not finished growing up in there, so what gives him away is not villainy — it is the boy underneath coming up for air. Every tell below should be something a frightened, arrested young man would do, worn by a grizzled Auror who would never.

**Candidate: the thing he did not prepare himself.** *(the author's, leading)*

- **The rule it breaks is canon's own.** Moody eats and drinks nothing he has not made with his own hands — the reason the flask works as a clue in the original. Ours inverts it: the tell is the one time he doesn't.
- **A smell from his schooldays gets him** on an outing to Hogsmeade, and he gives in and takes Moody over to have it himself. Not planned, no advantage in it, no plan value at all — the same register as the ferret (DIV-086).
- **The tell is that he does not check it** *(settled)*. Moody's guard has an off-switch marked *among allies* and the books show it — he sniffs a chicken leg at Grimmauld and eats it, with half a nose — so *drinking something he did not pour* is covered and would leak. **A public house is not that room.** He is among strangers, the mug came out of a stranger's hands, and he takes it without doing the one thing he personally taught Harry to do (DIV-097). His own trust-but-verify is broken in front of the boy he gave it to.
- **Harry sees the whole exchange and misreads it** *(settled)*. He is under the Cloak at the next table, he has had the lecture in November, and he is the one person in Britain equipped to notice — and what he files it under is *even the best of them slip sometimes; we aren't so different*. **Affection, not suspicion**, which is the reader's reaction too, and which makes it worse on the reread for both of them at once.
- **Placement: after the First Task**, necessarily, since the rule has to be armed before it can be broken (DIV-097). Canon's Three Broomsticks scene is spring, so nothing moves.
- **Butterbeer, settled.** No need to engineer a plate of something when a drink does the whole job. A grizzled Auror putting away a mug of a children's drink reads as a bout of nostalgia and nothing worse — **to the reader it says our Moody is not a stick in the mud about vigilance quite all the time**, which is a likeable thing to learn about him, and then they find out it was a rare Crouch moment and it curdles.
- **Placement:** the Three Broomsticks, in the canon scene with Hagrid that the author recalls. If it turns out to be food after all, the recipe can be Rosmerta's from years back, or her predecessor's that she learned.

**Candidate: Winky's name.** *(the author's)*

- **The way in is canon's own joke, kept:** at the Yule Ball, Moody admires Harry's socks. We keep the exchange whole, because it is funny and because it is the only reason the next beat can exist. *(Dracaena stiffens against Harry's arm while he does it and lets go when he walks away — DIV-101, DIV-095.)*
- **It is now link three of four** *(settled)*. The chain runs **cavorting with the enemy → the warning about the Malfoy girl → and why is the Malfoy elf giving you socks? → Dobby is minding another elf, Winky → the startle** (DIV-101). **No trigger is needed: he was at the Ball and watched the whole thing**, and he admired the socks himself. The few days before he acts are deliberate — he sits on what he saw, decides it is a problem, and then moves.
- **Why he asks is the good part, and the chain makes it better.** He knows Dobby is the Malfoy elf, and a paranoid Auror wants to know why a Malfoy creature is handing Harry Potter socks. **Coming out of a warning about the Malfoy girl it stops being a convenient opening and becomes the next sentence in an argument already about that family** — and it is the question that walks him into the tell.
- **And Harry is arguing about his girlfriend while it happens**, which is a far better reason to miss the startle than being oblivious.
- **The startle comes days later,** when Harry answers that Dobby is in the castle minding another elf — Winky. **Moody startles, very slightly, and covers it.**
- **The real Moody has no reason to know the name. Barty Crouch Jr has every reason:** she was his family's elf. A reader who remembers whose elf she was has the whole thing from one word.
- **It re-homes what Winky was carrying in canon** (Q-094). She confirmed the secret as a witness there; here she gives it away as a name, without ever being in the room.
- **Dracaena is in the Ball beat too,** and her reaction is open. Three on the table: incensed that he wore peasant socks and that she failed to notice; genuinely impressed, since our Dobby is no caricature and the socks may simply be good; or she says nothing at all and allows herself a small smile, because her old servant is alive and happy — which is the one that feeds Q-098 and costs a line.

**Settled: the voice he keeps having to shut** *(DIV-086)*

Not a candidate — recorded. Moody remembers putting the name in the Goblet and remembers having had a reason at the time, so he would simply say so if it came up, and he keeps nearly saying so. Crouch takes him over for a split second each time to stop him.

- **On the page:** passing notes of Moody opening his mouth about the culprit and then not saying it — a shrug, a grumble. Reads as a hard old man thinking better of a theory.
- **Every one of those costs Crouch grip** (DIV-086), which is why the hold is frayed by the Third Task and why the office goes wrong.
- **The confession pays it in one line:** *I had to keep his voice down all year — he was dying to tell them he put the name in.* The reader recontextualises every shrug at once.

**Settled: the flask is spent** *(DIV-097)*

It is whisky, and after the First Task Moody pours Harry a measure of it in his office. Everyone suspects the obvious thing, a second person drinks it and stays himself, and the clue is disposed of in half a page — which is also the book telling the rereader that the props are clean this year and the man is where to look. **It is also what makes the Butterbeer work:** the verb *check* is planted there, as a drill aimed at Harry, so the tell breaks something the reader heard him teach rather than something a narrator explained. Written out in [the-flask](../scenes/b4/the-flask.md).

**Also on the table:**

- **The trunk.** Seven locks, and one of them is never opened in front of anybody.
- **The eye.** It sees through wood, so it can see into his own trunk — and he looks at it, and looks away. The problem becomes the clue.
- **The Imperius lesson is *not* a tell** *(settled, DIV-105)*. He casts the curse on students because you cannot learn to beat a thing you have never felt, and he would do it in any year whatever. There is no trail and no hobby to weigh — **the irony is the reader's and the evidence is nil**, which is better than a clue that needed an explanation propped under it. Harry throwing it off in front of a man who cannot stays in, as the thing that decides Moody on him (DIV-105).
- **The lessons themselves,** since the reader can never be sure which were his — and there was never a list to sort, only permission (DIV-096).

### Q-102 · Do house-elves eat? (DIV-075, DIV-076, DIV-077)

*Answered: yes — and exactly one of them does.* A **housed** elf eats nothing and never has: the house maintains the body the way it maintains its own roof. That is also why no elf has ever been paid, since wages buy food and lodging and the house is both. An **unhoused** elf has no house behind it, so the body falls back on matter like anything else's. Every dismissed elf is briefly unhoused and none of them has ever cared; Dobby is the only elf in a thousand years who has had to feed himself and wanted to, so Harry's vault is not a convenience — it is the thing that feeds him.

Two canon beats re-light at no cost. **Winky refuses money,** which is refusing food: taking it would be admitting she means to go on living. **And she only drinks** — butterbeer is not sustenance but oblivion, which is why it is the one thing she will accept from Dobby, and which turns his keeping her into forcing meals on someone who means to starve (DIV-077).

A third beat goes the other way and is **cut**: canon has Dobby ask Dumbledore for wages. He does not, here. He has a person, which is all he ever wanted; the castle needs nothing from anybody and has no work to give; a Headmaster who employs freed elves makes Hogwarts a refuge in a small way, which the design cannot afford; and an independent income would dilute the one thing the Winky thread runs on, which is that Harry's vault is the only money in the system. He and Winky are the castle elf's guests, and Dumbledore does not know (DIV-077).

It also marks Dobby as the outcast and the glitch he is. As far back as he can remember he never ate anything; he was a spirit in a house, and spirits do not eat. Now he is hungry for the first time, with nobody alive who could tell him what the feeling is. His first meal is Molly's cooking at the Burrow in the summer of 1993 — not a house's output but a woman's work, by hand, for him — and he bawls his eyes out. Harry is in Diagon Alley that summer and misses it, so Dobby tells him instead, at length, and Harry cannot see why it matters.

**The false dawn is the point.** Out of the yoke and fed for the first time, he feels better than he ever has, and everyone reads that as freedom agreeing with him. Then it thins — his powers go by degrees across four years while he eats perfectly well — because food sustains the body and nothing sustains the spirit. The last of them goes evacuating Malfoy Manor. Feeding him never buys him a day (DIV-050). → DIV-075, [setting/house-elves.md](../setting/house-elves.md)

### Q-097 · Does the Manor ever make another elf? (DIV-075, DIV-050)

*Answered:* no. Making a whole elf is laborious — an unbroken line in one house over a span nobody has managed — and **nothing has been made since the ancient binding**, which may have broken the capacity outright. Every elf alive is one of the originals, the population only shrinks, and no house in the story gets a new one. Dobby is gone for good. The Manor stands empty of itself until a foreign elf colonises it: Kreacher (DIV-078). → DIV-075, [setting/house-elves.md](../setting/house-elves.md)

### Q-076 · Dobby as courier (DIV-065, DIV-050, DIV-075)

*Answered:* he can. A free elf is anchored, not confined — Dobby is anchored to Harry rather than to any building, and the Manor's claim on him is residual, not severed, so he can come and go. ### Q-053 · The remedial Boggart (DIV-017)

*Answered: it goes on the page, from her POV, before the lap pillow — and the Boggart is Harry.* Recorded in **DIV-017**, [remedial-boggart](../scenes/b3/remedial-boggart.md).

- **On the page, her POV.** It is the one Dracaena chapter that doesn't risk explaining her hostility away ([style](../core/style.md)). It shows no soft side at all: a girl who cannot name a fear, who finds out her whole interior life is one boy. That makes her more alarming, not softer, and it explains why she defends the feud so viciously — it is the only footing she has. **Lupin's POV is out**: it tips the werewolf early, and it hands the reader an adult's correct reading, which kills the blindness the next four books run on.
- **It also pays the lake,** which is the real reason to show it. Dementors serve worst memories and she hasn't got one, so what the swarm shows her is the Boggart ([buckbeak-rescue](../scenes/b3/buckbeak-rescue.md), DIV-018).
- **And it pays the lap pillow twice:** the reader watches her break off at "I got-" knowing it is him, with his head about to go in her lap.
- **Lupin knows and keeps it.** The open part carried on as Q-136.
- **When.** No longer free: the sling has to come off between the November swarm and the Ravenclaw match, or the Dementor-impression beat loses its cause ([timeline](timeline.md)).
