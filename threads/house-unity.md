# House Unity

Cross-House unity as a subplot: Slytherins who turn out to be people, and who are there at the end.

## Shape of the arc

*(draft)* DIV-055. Canon states the theme and never delivers it. The Hat warns the school every year, Dumbledore calls for unity at the end of B4, and the Houses go on hating each other to the last page. Slytherin in particular is a wall of sneers: in seven books almost no Slytherin is allowed to be an ordinary person, and before the battle the entire House is marched out of the castle.

We deliver it, but as a subplot, with canon's own device and nothing structural touched. The main argument is Harry and Dracaena: the romance crosses the deepest House line in the school, with real stakes, in the part of the book the reader is already watching. This thread is what grows around that.

- **The seeding (B1–B6).** A handful of named Slytherins behave like ordinary people. Not allies, not secretly nice: just pupils with homework, friends and bad days, who don't join in when Dracaena's circle is being vile. It costs a line here and there, and it means the ending isn't a conversion.
- **The DA (B5).** Some of them join. Canon bars Slytherins outright, and the reason given is distrust, so getting them in has to cost something and someone has to vouch. One is a friend of Dracaena's (Q-070). Dracaena herself is in the Inquisitorial Squad that year (DIV-013), which pulls hard the other way: see Q-070.
- **The battle (B7).** When McGonagall clears Slytherin House out of the castle, some refuse to go. They fight, and they're alongside Slughorn at the end (DIV-023, DIV-025).

The measure of the theme is who is standing in the Great Hall at the finish: not a House, a mixture.
