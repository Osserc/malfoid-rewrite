# The Duelling Club

Flitwick's Duelling Club, where Harry and Dracaena fight for years and learn each other by heart.

## Shape of the arc

*(settled)* DIV-028. After Lockhart's disastrous first session (canon), the students petition the teachers to keep the club going with a proper instructor, and Flitwick steps in. Harry and Dracaena face each other regularly and are evenly matched: sometimes he wins, sometimes she does. She's the better technical duellist. He's instinctive, and thrives in chaos and improvisation. The club is the even one of their three arenas: Quidditch is his and Potions is hers ([quidditch](quidditch.md)).

*(draft)* They keep facing each other because Flitwick pairs them on purpose: iron sharpens iron. He was a duelling champion in his youth (canon), and he'd like Hogwarts to produce more. Two evenly matched duellists who push each other are exactly what he's looking for. His stake in them as a pair also makes him a natural source for the resonance hint (Q-028).

It runs from B2 through B4. In B4 it stays open and the visiting students from Durmstrang and Beauxbatons join: the Tournament doesn't take much of anyone's time, and with Quidditch cancelled (canon) the club is the only arena their rivalry has left in the year of the Yule Ball. Early in B5 Umbridge shuts it down, one of the first things she does, and they're both miffed.

*(settled)* **The DA is this club in exile** · DIV-125. Umbridge shuts it down and they are both miffed, so when the DA starts Dracaena joins it and co-teaches — not for the cause, which she doesn't have, but to get her piste back. The room gets her school against Moody's argued in front of it: she drills form, Harry drills what to do when form fails. Full entry: [house-unity](house-unity.md).

*(settled)* In B6, with Umbridge gone, Flitwick reopens it. Harry offers it to Dracaena as a date, because he's missed their bouts. She refuses, and the fight that follows ends their relationship (DIV-015, [the-breakup](../scenes/b6/the-breakup.md)). She never comes back, so their next duel is Sectumsempra (DIV-043).

Through the clashes they come to know each other. They learn to read each other's body language and anticipate each other's reactions. At some point they start looking forward to the "games", and we see them smiling as they fight.

*(draft)* Early on, Dracaena wipes the floor with Hermione: she's that much more powerful, and a talented duellist too. It puts power by blood on the page early ([society](../setting/society.md)), and casts Hermione as the brains rather than the muscle. When: open.

**And Pansy walks out of it** *(draft)* · DIV-121. Dracaena ropes her in because that is what Dracaena does. Pansy attends two or three sessions at most, hates all of them, and tells her she isn't coming back — the first preference she has ever stated out loud. **Dracaena takes it perfectly well and never asks what she is doing instead;** she has no use for a maidservant, and it does not occur to her that a friend has preferences.

- **This is the room that humiliates Pansy specifically.** She has the power her blood gives her and no technique at all (DIV-122), and a duelling piste is precisely where technique shows and power doesn't help — next to a Malfoy who has both. She leaves and finds a chessboard, which cares about neither her blood nor her manners ([ron-pansy](ron-pansy.md)).
- **It is the other half of the blood argument this club is already making.** Hermione loses here for want of power; Pansy would lose for want of skill. Blood matters and is not everything, and the club shows both halves of that within a year of opening (DIV-008).

When she wins she sometimes forgets to gloat. No sneer, just a bright, happy "Did you see that? I won!", and Harry finds it endearing enough that losing doesn't seem so bad. It stands out against her usual teasing and casual cruelty, towards Ron and Hermione too.

## What Flitwick teaches *(settled)* · DIV-080

He drills the spells that can be cast clean — Expelliarmus, Stupefy, Protego, Petrificus Totalus, Impedimenta — and not the ones built only to afflict. It reads as a schoolmaster keeping things decent, and it is not: Dark magic leaves a residue in whoever works it ([magic](../setting/magic.md), Q-027), and he is teaching children the only magic they can throw at each other a thousand times a year without cracking themselves.

- **He never explains it that way,** and nobody asks. He is also, quietly, the man who built a vicewick.
**And he teaches by consequence** *(settled)* · DIV-127. Asked in B2 why a duellist does not simply glue his wand to his hand, he offers a demonstration instead of an answer: he glues Harry's, lets Dracaena Disarm him, and sends him to the infirmary with a dislocated shoulder — *"I'll deal with Madam Pomfrey myself"* ([the-glue-demonstration](../scenes/b2/the-glue-demonstration.md)). He knows a Ministry-approved instructor would never permit it and is entirely unbothered: a lesson somebody was carried out of is a lesson the whole room keeps.

- **It is the first sight of the hard edge under the squeak** — the same man who drills only the clean spells for reasons he never states, and who quietly builds a vicewick (DIV-080). Whether he eventually gets a character file of his own is open.
- **And it gives Dracaena material for life.** She laughs herself sick at the time, and she is still using it years later.

- **It gives Harry's Expelliarmus a reason.** Canon never justifies his signature spell as anything but sentiment. Ours makes it the one thing you can cast forever and stay clean — which is what the series ends on, against a man who is nothing but accumulated stain (🔒 DIV-006).

## Her technique *(settled)* · DIV-109

She shields on the point of her wand — coin-sized Protegos placed exactly where the incoming spell will be — so she pricks curses out of the air and bounces them back, spending almost nothing. It is where the reader sees that she is the better technician, and it is the thing the Killing Curse goes straight through (DIV-107). Full entry: [dracaena-malfoy](../characters/dracaena-malfoy.md). Planting it: Q-144.

**The other school is Moody's**: move, and put matter in the way (DIV-108). **Her objection is not taste but concession** — using cover admits the other duellist is better, and with a pin-shield and a wide knowledge of counters she usually does know the answer. She scoffs, Harry tries it on her and she overcomes it, he perfects it, and the mature version beats Krum (Q-123). Full entry: [dracaena-malfoy](../characters/dracaena-malfoy.md).

***(open)* Whether any of this happens in the club.** Flitwick's is a noble art with rules, and he can think Moody's technique correct and still not want it dragged over his piste — which would push Harry's practice, and the bout where she overcomes him, somewhere private. Q-144.

**And Moody watches a session**, does not recognise it as fighting, and takes the theory back to his own classroom. That is where interposition comes from (DIV-108).

**Nobody else here can do it either, and she never notices** *(draft)* · DIV-125. For three years the club is full of people failing or declining to copy her pin-shield, and the fact never reaches her, **because on this piste there were only ever two people.** She finds out in the DA, from Harry, in one flat sentence, and blushes ([the-only-two-of-us](../scenes/b5/the-only-two-of-us.md)).

## The trophies, and the two hoards *(settled)* · DIV-059

At some point the bouts acquire a forfeit: the loser gives the winner a trophy, something of their own. It starts as her challenge at the last duel of their second year, and she wins it, taking his Gryffindor scarf. *(Not Christmas: canon puts the club's first meeting on 17 December, so by the holidays there is no rivalry yet for a forfeit to come out of.)*

- **Both of them keep everything they win,** and neither has ever broached the subject with the other. She parades her spoils — the scarf, and at the B3 final one of his Seeker's gloves, worn on the pitch while he wears one of hers (DIV-069). What is never said out loud is what the things have come to mean, or where they end up.
- **And the scarf is public evidence — but she only ever wears it when she has a reason to.** She never puts it on to be seen in it. A wool scarf in warm weather looks ridiculous, and looking ridiculous is the one thing her taste does not permit (DIV-070).
- **What happens instead is that it becomes the scarf she reaches for.** A cold evening at home over the summer of 1993, a cold morning at school, every Hogsmeade weekend from October. By B3 the reaching is automatic and she thinks nothing of it, **which is the whole point: she needs the excuse, and the weather keeps handing her one.** Same engine as the compliment at Madam Malkin's — she wants the thing, so she finds the terms on which she is allowed to have it (DIV-009).
- **Which is why "trophy" holds for about a year.** Nobody can call it a parade when she only ever has it on when everyone has something on. It is most visible on a winter Hogsmeade weekend, where a Slytherin in Gryffindor colours is standing in a crowd of scarves. Her House stops believing it from B3 regardless — Pansy first, Daphne next, and for opposite reasons (DIV-094).
- **Hers,** by B4, is a shrine in the Malfoy room (DIV-060): a quill, a Chocolate Frog card with Dumbledore on it, a worn Seeker's glove and the scarf, on a stand under a Gryffindor banner. *(draft)* Where the banner came from is open.
- **His** is a smaller trove of her things in his dormitory. He admits to it the night he finds hers ([the-malfoy-room](../scenes/b4/the-malfoy-room.md)).
- **What it's for.** It turns years of feuding into a physical record, and it makes her sincerity something she has been accumulating in secret since B2 — one more thing that should have told Harry and didn't (Q-035).
- *(draft)* **Planting it.** The custom and her first win need to be on the page in B2–B3 for the B4 reveal to land. Where: Q-073.
