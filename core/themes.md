# Themes

What the rewrite is about, and what the change to Dracaena is for.

## The thesis *(settled)*

Romantic love is a different kind of thing from friendship and family love, not just a stronger version of them.

- **Belonging.** The Weasleys, Hermione and Hagrid are Harry's found family. They make him feel welcome and accepted.
- **Desire.** Dracaena gives him what they can't: she wants him. She went from "you're an eyesore" to "you make my life worth living". Being loved and desired like that is what gives him a reason to live.
- **It runs both ways.** She makes him better and gives him a reason to improve, and he does the same for her.
- **Not a put-down of friendship.** His friends are his family and they matter. They just do a different thing.

Where it shows:
- **The magic.** Resonance needs it: friendship can't resonate (DIV-021).
- **King's Cross.** He goes back because he wants his girl, not for the job (DIV-048).
- **The Manor.** Possibly how their meeting turns: he's looking for a reason to live, and her not wanting him dead may be his only hope (Q-063).
- **The Hallows are demoted.** Mastering death by accepting it is Dumbledore's story, not ours (Q-011).

## The dream and the nightmare

The dream is the belief that you can live outside the script you were born into. Harry is the one who lets Dracaena dream. The nightmare is the script reclaiming her: her family's allegiance, the Mark, Voldemort at her table. Its sharpest form is the death of faith between B5 and B6, when the dream turns out never to have been real (DIV-013, DIV-015). Carried by the Sleeping Beauty motif: see [threads/sleeping-beauty.md](../threads/sleeping-beauty.md).

It gets a second case from outside her head, which Harry carries from B1: the Dessendres, a family who could actually build the dream and were destroyed by it (DIV-072). Harry and Dracaena have the same story and take opposite lessons from it — he sees what dwelling costs, she sees a technique that nearly worked. It also carries the warning aimed at Harry rather than her: the father who went into the painting to bring his wife out, for the best reason there is, and lost years to it anyway. See [setting/painted-life.md](../setting/painted-life.md).

## Pureblood ideology with a point (DIV-008)

_Open._ See Q-016. Her Muggle reading pulls both ways: it wears down her contempt, and it shows her why purebloods fear being swallowed.

*(draft)* **The atrocity on our side of the ledger.** DIV-079, DIV-080, DIV-083, DIV-084. The Ministry has a weapon that exterminates Dementors and keeps the Dementors anyway, because they are useful — and it pointed that same weapon at the homes of its political enemies. The weapon judges nobody: it scorches whatever Dark magic it finds in a person, by whatever route it got there, which means it cannot tell a torturer from a man who hexed his neighbour — or a perpetrator from a victim, since a curse somebody else put on you burns exactly as well. The war leaves marks on the good ones too, and the fire reads the marks. It finds something in almost everyone. The Ministry did not outsource its judgement to a spell. It burned houses on the word of an instrument that measures one thing, and called the output a verdict nobody could appeal. Then, having discovered it never had a hold on the Dementors at all, it bought one from the people it had just fought, and let a man walk free for it. The other side gets a grievance that is documented rather than invented, and the castle itself tells the story, as a legend. See [problems/azkaban.md](../problems/azkaban.md) and Q-016.

*(draft)* **The ruins.** In the vein of FromSoftware's games, wizarding Britain lives in the ruins of a once-thriving world: a castle built for a thousand that holds a third of that, and a people shrinking since the Statute of Secrecy (DIV-035). That gives Voldemort's promise to take it back real credence, except that his war killed so many it made the decline worse. The resolution after the war is the author's idea (Q-048).

## Unity across the Houses *(draft)* · DIV-055

Canon states this theme (the Hat's warnings, Dumbledore's call for unity at the end of B4) and never delivers it. The Houses stay fiercely tribal, so crossing them means something.

It's a subplot, not a structural change. Harry and Dracaena carry it at full strength already: the romance crosses the deepest House line in the school, and it's where the book's attention is anyway. Around them it's argued through the DA, canon's own device: some Slytherins join it, and some stay for the battle and fight alongside Slughorn (DIV-055). The groundwork is a handful of Slytherins behaving like ordinary people across the early books, so the turn at the end reads as something that was always there rather than a conversion.

## Love as a literal force (DIV-006)

The series ends with love working as actual magic: resonance (DIV-021). It's literally the power of love, and only love, since friendship isn't enough (why: *The thesis*, above). Together, Harry and Dracaena are never disarmed: they're each other's strength. That only lands if it has been a theme, and a rule, all along. See Q-014 and Q-028.

*(direction)* **And it is a force rather than a sentiment** — Love and Strife, joining and separating (DIV-113). A Horcrux is one person divided; resonance is two people joined. Voldemort's project and theirs are the same physics run in opposite directions.

### Virtue is the argument, not the mechanism *(settled)*

**We win because we are more virtuous — narratively, not in-universe.** That is what the rewrite is saying, and the point it is making: that it is worth doing things right. It is never how the world works. Nothing in the magic rewards anybody for being good, Love is not on our side, and Strife is not a punishment. The model is Lustrum, which makes no judgement of anybody and only counts spells (DIV-080).

**Keep the two apart on the page.** The moment virtue becomes an input to the magic, we have rebuilt cosmic fiat favouring the good with new vocabulary, which is the thing this rewrite exists to get away from (DIV-112). Harry and Dracaena win because of what they are to each other, not because they deserve to.

## The Chosen One isn't enough *(settled)* · DIV-026

Harry opens the way to Voldemort's destruction, but he can't destroy him alone, and his death in the forest protects no one. Everyone has to play their part: Dracaena, Neville, the DA, even the Malfoys (their role: Q-032). If any of them fails, it's over. They're all inspired by Harry, but being the Chosen One isn't enough, and he was never really chosen in the first place. Neville holding the line is the clearest case (DIV-025).

### The prophecy enabled it; it did not cause it *(settled)* · DIV-112

**It did exactly one thing: it got Voldemort to do his own killing, once.** He believed it, so he went to the cottage personally instead of sending the people he sends. Everything after that is mechanics (DIV-110). **It would have happened anyway, the first time he decided a child was worth his personal attention.**

- **So nothing in this story is ordained.** The series never says *this happened because it was meant to*. It says *this happened because these people were in this configuration, and at this time because these two were uniquely suited to it*. That is the difference between our metaphysics and authorial fiat, and it is most of why the Chosen One dynamic is gone.
- **It is not a cheat retired quietly. It is a theme paid out.** B5 becomes a year of men killing each other over a sentence that never meant anything, and Sirius dies for a recording. The Ministry, the Order and Voldemort all treat it as the hinge of the war; it was only ever the thing that put one man in one nursery.
- **And it is the first brick out of Dumbledore.** He did not have secret wisdom. He had a worse theory of reality than the one the world actually runs on, and he bet a child on it.
- How prophecy works at all, once defused, and what B5's plot becomes: **Q-146**.

### Voldemort caused all of it *(settled)*

The engine of the whole series is one man trying to bend reality to his whims and being maimed by the attempt. He played with his soul until it was unstable; he used the foulest spell in existence himself, on a pristine target, through a woman who had put herself in the way; the reaction maimed him and handed parts of himself to the boy; and those parts are what keeps letting the boy out by the skin of his teeth (DIV-110, DIV-111). **No coincidences of wandmaking, no destiny, and no technicalities.** It needed a very specific set of circumstances, and it could in principle happen again — it had never happened before only because he sends followers, and this once he went himself.
