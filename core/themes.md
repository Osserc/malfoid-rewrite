# Themes

What the rewrite is about, and what the change to Dracaena is for.

## Why a daughter

_Open._ What making Draco a girl lets the story say that canon couldn't. See Q-001.

## The dream and the nightmare

The dream is the belief that you can live outside the script you were born into. Harry is the one who lets Dracaena dream. The nightmare is the script reclaiming her: her family's allegiance, the Mark, Voldemort at her table. Its sharpest form is the death of faith between B5 and B6, when the dream turns out never to have been real (DIV-013, DIV-015). Carried by the Sleeping Beauty motif: see [threads/sleeping-beauty.md](../threads/sleeping-beauty.md).

## Pureblood ideology with a point (DIV-008)

_Open._ See Q-016. Her Muggle reading pulls both ways: it wears down her contempt, and it shows her why purebloods fear being swallowed.

## Love as a literal force (DIV-006)

The series ends with love working as actual magic: resonance (DIV-021). It's literally the power of love, and only love, since friendship isn't enough. Together, Harry and Dracaena are never disarmed: they're each other's strength. That only lands if it has been a theme, and a rule, all along. See Q-014 and Q-028.

## The Chosen One isn't enough *(settled)* · DIV-026

Harry opens the way to Voldemort's destruction, but he can't destroy him alone, and his death in the forest protects no one. Everyone has to play their part: Dracaena, Neville, the DA, even the Malfoys (their role: Q-032). If any of them fails, it's over. They're all inspired by Harry, but being the Chosen One isn't enough, and he was never really chosen in the first place. Neville holding the line is the clearest case (DIV-025).
