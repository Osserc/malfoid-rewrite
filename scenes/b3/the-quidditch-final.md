# The Quidditch Final

## Purpose

The first full Seeker's dilemma under our rules (DIV-066), and the day Dracaena learns that playing straight gets her the one thing she wants and can't take by force. It supplies the second half of her Buckbeak motive (DIV-003): the guilt makes her want to act, this makes her believe acting straight will work. It is the loudest of the supporting scenes for Harry's turn at the lake (Q-035), the first visible crack between her and her house (Q-081), and the one clear look Harry gets at what her cruelty is really like (DIV-071).

## The shape of the match

Gryffindor are ahead almost throughout and it never helps them. They need to be **60 clear before Harry may take the Snitch**, or Slytherin keep the Cup on aggregate, and every Slytherin goal pushes that line back by a goal. Final score **140–50**.

1. **Flint's strategy.** Slytherin foul their way through it, as in canon, but here it's a plan. Flint knows his Chasers are the weaker side, so he stops playing for the match and plays for the aggregate: batter the Gryffindor Chasers, hold the margin under the Cup line, and have his Seeker end the game early. It costs him the match and would have won him the Cup.
2. **Harry's job never changes:** stop her ending it. The phases belong to her, which is right, because she's the one the day changes.
3. **Her phase one: hunting.** Catching the Snitch caps Gryffindor's margin and saves the Cup — losing the match on purpose. She does it willingly and hates it, and says so to Harry in a low voice mid-air: she doesn't want to win like this, some of them have dignity. Sincerity only he hears.
4. **Her phase two: marking.** Once Gryffindor reach the margin she can't take her eyes off him and has to mark instead of hunt. Her rage is at being contained, not at being confused.
5. **The fake-out.** The margin comes within reach and Harry breaks for the Snitch. His fingers are on it when her own Chasers score, the line moves back a goal, and he has to let it go — saved by her team, who have no idea. He turns and gives her a smug look: *I could end this whenever I want.* She is incandescent.
6. **The real duel** takes seconds. She sticks far closer this time, having seen what he has in reserve, and it still isn't enough.

## Why she loses

Two things, and neither is equipment.

- **She is legible to him.** She has watched him fly for two years, and he has worked out what the watching looks like from the inside. He baits her with the trick she is waiting for, and she commits to it.
- **She will not be reckless.** The winning move is an improvisation she finds distasteful, not one she lacks the skill for (DIV-070). At the lake she says it herself, unprompted: she would never have done that, it's uncouth.

**The cost is his.** Harry spends the match minding a Firebolt that obeys every stray thought and is nearly spent by the end; she never fights her Nimbus for a second (DIV-068). He wins on the one move where he stops minding it and simply trusts it.

**Nothing to blame.** A better broom wouldn't have helped her, and the reader has watched the better broom cost Harry all afternoon. She never reaches for the excuse — what she names is taste, which is the more damning answer, because it's about who she is. Lucius doesn't offer her a Firebolt either, before or after: he celebrates achievement, he doesn't console failure ([lucius-malfoy](../../characters/lucius-malfoy.md)).

## The crack

Her house stops laughing over the course of the match (Q-081).

- **The cheer.** Harry needles her about her loose hair and she answers with a Muggle cheer — Muggle Studies surfacing in the worst possible place (DIV-012). Lee Jordan tells the whole stadium what it just saw. The Slytherin stands stop cheering and scowl, and she watches it happen.
- **The tirade.** Her Beater nearly takes Angelina off her broom and she finally snaps, flies at him and calls him an **inbred idiot troll** in front of witnesses (DIV-070). What the stadium sees is not principle: it's *this is my match, mine*. Harry has her private version and her public one and cannot reconcile them, which is the point (Q-035).
- **What it looks like up close.** Harry is a few feet away, having gone to check on Angelina, and watches her face come apart. He has seen her furious at him a hundred times; this is nothing like it (DIV-071).
- **The moment neither of them takes.** The Snitch is as catchable as it has been all match, and she leaves her post to scream at her Beater while he leaves his to check on his Chaser. Neither notices. It's the first time either has chosen something over the Snitch, and it is the behaviour she gets accused of in B5 (DIV-067).
- **It's written home about.** Bole tells his family what she called him, which gives the resentment a route out of the castle (Q-081, DIV-040).

## The gloves and the hair tie

- **The gloves** (DIV-059). They come onto the pitch wearing each other's — she in a Seeker's glove she won off him at the Duelling Club, he in one of hers with her initial stitched into the palm. The first time the trophies are visible to anyone else, played as a joke by both, and never mentioned again by either. He catches the Snitch with her glove on his hand.
- **The hair tie.** It comes loose mid-duel and he takes it out of the air without deciding to — his broom giving him the reach because it obeys what he hasn't said (DIV-068) — and pockets it. At the lake he gives it back, she turns round and starts to ask him to tie it for her, and Madam Hooch arrives and cuts her off. Being interrupted is what lets her pretend tomorrow that she never asked.

## The hand

1. **Her team desert her.** She's left alone in the middle of the pitch on her knees, staring at her hands, crying: she tried everything she had, played it straight, and lost anyway.
2. **He looks back.** Harry is being hauled off by his own side. He sees her there, alone, and it bothers him, because the flying was the best he's had.
3. **He goes back,** crouches, and holds out his hand — the one wearing her glove. Something close to: *I had a lot of fun playing with you. Can we do it again?*
4. **It mirrors the train.** She offered her hand and he didn't take it, and that refusal is the root of the whole grudge (DIV-009). Carried by her, not by him: she stares at his hand rather than his face, reaches, snatches her own hand back as though she expected to be stung, and only then takes it. Harry stays oblivious throughout, which is better — he doesn't know he's repaying anything.
5. **She takes it.** She wants to read it as mockery and can't — the smile is too plain for that.
6. **The race.** He's back on his broom challenging her to one, something daft as the forfeit, and she chases him. They fly off together.
7. **The lake.** The flying, the laughter, *how did you do that*, the uncouth line, the hair tie, Hooch.
8. **Detention** for both, from Madam Hooch, separately, plus twenty points from each House.

## Must keep

- He is not being noble. He is happy, and the generosity is a by-product. Conscious kindness would read as pity and she'd throw it back at him.
- She loses to *him*, and to herself — not to his equipment.
- The mask goes back on the next morning, colder than ever (DIV-017's reflex), and Harry is confused and a little hurt by it. Nothing between them visibly changes for the rest of the year. Every soft beat at the lake raises the price of that reversal, which is why Hooch interrupts the last one.
- The final gives Harry **affection**; the lake gives him **revelation** (she stole Buckbeak, the lap pillow was sincere, the Patronus study was real). None of that leaks here ([buckbeak-rescue](buckbeak-rescue.md)).
- Her public outburst is possessive, not principled. Don't let it argue for clean play — the principled version is the one only Harry heard.
- The uncouth line sits between Harry admitting he's hurt and her reaching to squeeze his arm, so it reads as concern wearing criticism.

## Open

Where the common-room fallout goes (Q-081) · whether the match wants trimming when it becomes a chapter: the stretch where Harry watches her fly is the densest mechanics in the scene and sits at the point the reader most wants speed.
