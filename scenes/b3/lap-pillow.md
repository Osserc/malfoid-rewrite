# The Lap Pillow

## Purpose

The memory behind Harry's Patronus at the lake (DIV-018), and one of the things that add up for him there ([buckbeak-rescue](buckbeak-rescue.md)). It's also where her own Patronus arc starts (DIV-027): she studies the charm for him before she ever wants it for herself.

## What she's trying to do

*(settled)* She sought him out to play with him, not to kick him while he's down. What she wants is to give him something memorable and loving, and the best she can come up with is how her mother loves her: Narcissa's tenderness is her only reference point for love, so she copies it for him. His head in her lap, her hand stroking his hair and cheek, the way mama does whenever she's upset.

Underneath, never put into words, it's also a twisted way of owning him and humiliating him. She's delighted he can't cast the charm, because if he has no happy memories, she can give him the only one he'll have. *I had to step in and give you a reason to be happy. Your Mudblood and blood-traitor friends couldn't. Don't you regret rejecting me now?* The rejection is the train in B1 ([hogwarts-express](../b1/hogwarts-express.md)).

## Beats

Settled, from the author's sketch. The order and wording can still move.

1. Harry takes secret Patronus lessons with Lupin (canon). Dracaena spies on them and reads up on the charm in the library. She wants him to succeed.
2. After another failed lesson she finds him alone, eating chocolate, and opens with a jab about his mother's manners. He tells her flatly that his parents are dead, and shuts down. She knew: to her, being an orphan is an abstract thing, and it made a good jab. She expected him to trade blows with her, and her small "oh" is the teasing already backfiring.
3. She wants her sparring partner back, so she pushes harder to provoke him into fighting back. She guesses he's struggling with the charm: a Patronus needs a strong, happy memory, so he mustn't have any. The taunt is also the run-up to her offer (*What she's trying to do*). It gets her far more than she bargained for: he explodes before she gets there.
4. **The outburst.** Harry looms over her and screams what the Dementors make him hear: Voldemort killing his parents, over and over. She's terrified, and for the first time she feels he truly hates her. Kept on purpose: it shows Harry can become unstable, which we can use later (Q-024).
5. **What she didn't know.** Not that his parents are dead, but that the Dementors make him relive it. Seeing how deeply it hurts him, she wishes she'd never said it. She apologises, stammering.
6. **Her Boggart.** To make a connection, she offers a vulnerability of her own: she couldn't beat her Boggart either. It was her father, looking at her with disgust, and she froze. She faced it alone with Lupin, after sitting out Slytherin's session in her sling, so Harry is the first person she tells ([remedial-boggart](remedial-boggart.md)).
7. **The lap pillow.** She offers to help and pulls his head into her lap, stroking his hair and cheek, because that's what mama does whenever she's upset. He laughs at "mama". She blushes but doesn't stop. For a few precious seconds he catches her giving him a heartfelt, sweet smile, with none of her usual venom.
8. **The tsun excuse.** He asks why she's being nice. She sounds almost wronged: if the Dementors make him faint during their Quidditch match, how can she prove she's better than him? She wants to beat him fair and square. (Wording to polish.)
9. He ends it. Did it work? It did *something*. She runs off, threatening to hex him if he tells anyone, with the skip in her step she has after beating him at the Duelling Club (DIV-028). He walks back touching the cheek she stroked.

## Her parents

The scene shows both of them through her: "father" in the Boggart, "mama" in the comfort. Who they are to her: [dracaena-malfoy](../../characters/dracaena-malfoy.md). What Lucius really thinks: [lucius-malfoy](../../characters/lucius-malfoy.md).

## Why the memory waits for the lake

*(settled)* Harry doesn't use it before the lake, because he has no reason to think of it as happy. As far as he's concerned, she insulted him, rubbed salt in the wound, then played a prank on him. At the lake, once he sees she risked everything to make things right with Buckbeak, he realises she was sincere. Only then is it a memory he can cast with ([buckbeak-rescue](buckbeak-rescue.md)).

Before the lake he still goes back to her smile now and then, and finds it very pretty. It confuses him greatly.

## Notes on the sketch

- **The taunt** (beat 3) is to be reworked so it reads as her trying to provoke him back into the game, and as the run-up to her offer, with the owning motive underneath.
- **Her introspection** during the outburst is too on the nose. Stay with Harry and let her terror show from outside.
- **Her "oh"** reads as regret too early. Make clear it's the teasing backfiring (beat 2): she expected a sparring match, and he shut down instead.

## Must keep

- She studies the charm for him and wants him to succeed.
- The mama gesture: her only model of love, borrowed for him.
- The owning motive under the cruelty, never spoken.
- Her end goal steers every line she says, even the cruel ones: get him sparring again, then make her offer. Keep it in view when drafting the chapter.
- The outburst, and her Boggart.
- The smile he catches.
- The tsun excuse about the match.
- He doesn't count it as a happy memory until the lake, where it wins the day.
