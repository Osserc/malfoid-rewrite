# The Laughing Witch

## Purpose

Pays off the Dementor garrison that has hung over the whole book, turns Harry's Patronus at the lake into an event with consequences, and gives Dracaena the cover she needs to reach Sirius. It also opens the Azkaban argument ([azkaban](../../problems/azkaban.md)) and puts the Thynne–Laurel pair on the page.

## The rule that protects the climax

**Harry and Dracaena never get a POV on this.** The spectacle belongs to side characters — Balthier and Daphne at a tower window — so it cannot displace the book's real ending, which is intimate: the Patronus, the confession, the flight. The fight itself is short. Minerva is having a field day; it is not a battle.

## Beats

1. The Dementors, maimed and starving after the lake, mass on the castle. **Nothing is holding them and nothing ever was** — the garrison is an arrangement, not a leash (DIV-084), and an arrangement does not survive a thirteen-year-old maiming the horde. *"They are wounded, Filius, and they seek to replenish their strength"* — six words that recast what a thirteen-year-old did at the lake.
2. The staff hold the walls with their Patronuses. The castle is awake; the corridors are full.
3. McGonagall transfigures her battle garb onto herself as she walks, in front of the students. Nobody has ever seen it.
4. Flitwick gives her back the enhancer he has been holding since the spring — a small brass vial on a chain, a reliquary of ash — and covers her. She goes up, casts Lustrum, and the swarm burns. The fire does not spread: the grass and the trees are untouched, because grass has never cast anything (DIV-080). The reader is never told that; it is derivable and better left.
5. The orb wanes; she gathers the embers into a halo of smouldering orbs and comes down, throwing them at the fleeing Dementors on the ground.
6. She laughs. Not cruelly — joyously, with a tinge of mania. The whole castle hears it. **It is the one night of her life when the thing she loves doing is also unambiguously the right thing to do**: the enemy outside the walls, an empty lawn, nobody of hers inside the burn. She has never had it clean before and never will again. DIV-079, DIV-080
7. She lands with fresh burns and a sleeve gone, showing the marked flesh. Fudge arrives with Macnair and shouts about an unauthorised incantation and destroyed Ministry property. She taunts Macnair — a courteous wish that he had been out there too, which reads as spite and is in fact a reference, because **he used to lead that horde** (DIV-084) — takes Fudge by the collar, and walks off for medical attention.
8. **The aftermath is the cover.** An empty sky, survivors that will not come near the castle, and grounds covered in cinders nobody can touch. Dracaena flies Buckbeak out over it — her first sight of what a war actually leaves on the ground (DIV-018, [buckbeak-rescue](buckbeak-rescue.md)).
9. Balthier and Nola in the common room afterwards: *"I never knew fire could be so beautiful."* He decides to stop hiding her from his mother. She is not frightened and never was — she is the one who got him through the Quidditch match in November. DIV-082
10. The morning after: the grounds cordoned off, the theories, McGonagall's flat denial, and the legend. It is **the Laughing Witch** — settled; the castle names her for the sound, because the sound is all most of them got.

## Seeding, earlier in the book

- **September: the necklace.** She commissions the enhancer before term, pre-emptively — a woman who wants the Dementors exterminated does not wait for a garrison on her lawn to misbehave. What she hands Flitwick in August is the urn of ashes she has kept since the war, and what comes back is the whole of it distilled into one vial. Nobody sees the handover. The castle sees only that McGonagall has started wearing a necklace. It is the first enhancer she has ever had, after twelve years of wanting one, and in lulls she takes it out and holds it, absently, and puts it back. **Hermione notices, cannot place it, looks it up and finds nothing** — and gets at it in B4 instead, through the Restricted Section (Q-112).
- **November: the pitch.** She has carried it for two months when the Dementors swarm the match, which means she can end the whole garrison from the stands — along with fourteen children in the air and the school behind her, because the fire weighs everything inside it. She stays her hand. Scorch marks on the railing in a downpour; Lee Jordan sees and says nothing.
- **December: the handover, planted where nobody can watch it.** At the Three Broomsticks, Hermione catches her telling Flitwick to hold on to it for her, because she does not trust herself with it. No elaboration, and nobody asks. **The camouflage is the scene itself:** this is the table where Harry learns Sirius betrayed his parents, so the reader has nowhere to spare. Hermione is the only one who registers it, which is her payoff for having noticed the necklace in the first place — and it is still a frustration rather than a clue, since "I don't trust myself with it" says nothing about what *it* is.
- **Needs its own scene file** when we take the Three Broomsticks up; the beat is currently only recorded here and in the outline.
- **Balthier.** Takes Harry aside earlier in the year to tell him he has nothing to be ashamed of for fainting. He is mostly talking to himself — and his reasoning is the opposite of pity. He does not faint, so he assumes he has the easier end of it; which means whatever a third-year is hearing must be worse than what he hears, and what he hears is bad enough. He is the only person in the castle who works that out, and he is right.

## Must keep

- Harry and Dracaena have no POV here.
- The fight is short.
- The fire spares the grass.
- The laughter, and the flat denial afterwards.
- She spends the entire vial on this one casting. There is no second one unless she burns more people (Q-113).
- Fudge loses the prisoner, the hippogriff and the garrison in one night and answers with a hearing.

## Open

- **Q-003** — where Fudge, Macnair, Dumbledore and Snape are through the night, and how it fits the rebuilt climax. Recommendation on the table: they hear it perfectly well and do not come, because Fudge will not recall the garrison he authorised and will not let Dumbledore leave. Not "they were out of earshot", which reads as furniture and hands Dumbledore an alibi he should not have.
- **Q-106** — Snape does not cast, settled. What he is doing instead is open; the proposal on the table is that he is underground with his House, which is where a Head of House belongs and which places him for Q-003 for free.
- **Settled since:** the Dementors on that lawn may well be the ones that went over to Voldemort and were put back into service afterwards (DIV-083, DIV-084), which is what makes her restraint at the match enormous and what she is actually burning in June.
- Where Dracaena is while every other student is at a window.
