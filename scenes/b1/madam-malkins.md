# Madam Malkin's

## Purpose

Their first meeting, and the origin of the Sleeping Beauty motif. It's the only time either of them is seen without a name: not the Malfoy heiress, not the Boy Who Lived. It's also the first showing of the engine that runs her for seven books — when something she wants turns out not to be allowed, she finds a way to be allowed it rather than giving it up.

**It goes well first, and that is the design.** Nothing in the first half is a feint.

## Beats

1. The shop. Madam Malkin is already fitting a girl in a white dress by the window; Lindsay, her American clerk, calls off the mannequins and takes Harry in hand. She clocks the scar and says nothing about it — unlike everyone at the Leaky Cauldron, who were kind *because* of it. **The fringe stays down**, which is why the girl by the window never works out who she is talking to (DIV-115).
2. Sunlight on the girl's hair reminds Harry of *Sleeping Beauty*. He watched the film in secret from the living-room doorway, the one time Aunt Petunia forgot to lock the cupboard.
3. That same day magic turned out to be real, so his only question is whether she's the real princess.
4. She opens an eye and asks whether he's for Hogwarts too. He blurts out whether she's Sleeping Beauty.
5. She's confused, but flattered. She's had plenty of compliments, never one this sincere and awestruck.
6. He goes up on tiptoe to check: she doesn't have purple eyes, but hers are pretty too. Lindsay fishes for one of her own and frightens him.
7. **The laundering.** She asks what the story is, finds out it's Muggle, and goes cold: who taught him Muggle stories, what happened to his parents, were they *our* kind. Both magic — so she warms straight back up, delighted, and offers to bring him up to speed. The snobbery is the price of keeping the compliment.
8. Hagrid appears at the window with two ice creams. She repeats her father's opinion of him almost absently, and never notices Harry go cold.
9. She asks his name, last. Lindsay has him off the stool and away to the counter before he answers.
10. Harry leaves thinking the "princess" is cruel. Lindsay apologises for her on the way out.
11. *Coda, the shop.* Dracaena goes off humming. Lindsay asks why they don't just refuse the Malfoys; Madam Malkin shows her the blank cheque (DIV-115, DIV-008).
12. *Coda, the Manor.* Dracaena tells her parents she's made a friend, and asks for the Muggle books. Narcissa sends Dobby. Lucius takes his one consolation — a half-blood at worst — and dislikes the whole thing anyway (DIV-010, [lucius-malfoy](../../characters/lucius-malfoy.md)).
