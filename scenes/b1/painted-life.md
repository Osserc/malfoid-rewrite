# Painted Life

## Purpose

The escapism theme stops being an assertion and becomes a thing Harry has stood in front of. It also does three jobs nothing else in B1 does: it shows Dracaena competent before it shows her cruel, it puts the true Dessendre story in her mouth rather than a book's, and it lets her shut down a rescue five years before she refuses one.

Chapter-length, not scene-length. The aftermath of the slur is a separate scene (Q-092).

## Beats

1. **The find.** Hermione comes back to the common room with the Dessendres. Harry had given her the name to get her off his back; she went digging into magic like the Mirror on his behalf and found them by accident. She explains the difference between a portrait and painted life, and Harry gets there on his own: *they could paint my parents back.*
2. **Ron opts out.** He is playing chess and isn't interested. His absence matters later, because he is the one who knows what Mudblood means.
3. **The dead end.** An hour of asking portraits gets nothing. They are oblivious or they refuse, and the refusal isn't explained yet.
4. **She finds them.** Dracaena, on a staircase, takes the linked arms for a date and is wrong about it. She hears the name Dessendre and her manner changes.
5. **The slur.** Hermione recites the thin public version and calls them interesting. DIV-074. Hermione goes still, Harry feels her nails and doesn't understand what he's looking at.
6. **Sir Nicholas.** Dracaena explains that the portraits despise painted life because it reminds them what they are, then charms the Gryffindor ghost — a curtsy, a tailored compliment, the Baron held in reserve — and has the location in a minute. *Magic is not the only weapon in the arsenal of a witch.*
7. **The corridor.** Fifth floor, on the way to the prefects' bathroom. The landscape, the sleeping creatures, the half-built castle in the sky, the clay man grinding boulders in his chest.
8. **The true story.** Verso's death; Aline painting her family back and moving in; Renoir going in after her; Alicia following her father in and never getting out; the wasting; the fire. Harry asks whether Painted Verso was really him, and gets *yes and no*.
9. **The argument.** Hermione sides with Renoir. Dracaena: she got what she was after, she merely had to ration her time better. Harry wants that to be true, because his parents' faces were warm, and leaves it unsettled.
10. **The creature speaks**, at Clea's name. The routine, the promise, the castle he is building badly so that she will come back. A century of it.
11. **Harry's two angers.** At Clea, for leaving him. At himself, for thinking about painted parents — and what would happen to them when he grew up, or died in there.
12. **The name, the offer, the refusal.** The creature was never given a name. Harry offers to come and keep him company. *No. Mistress will be back.*
13. **Dracaena stops him.** *There is nothing you can do for him.* Her eyes are glassy; she sniffs, sneers, declares the lesson over, and her steps are unsteady as she goes.
14. **The walk back.** Hermione tells him it was noble and that the creature wouldn't have let him help. He doesn't hear a word of it, and is grateful she is there.

## Must keep

- **The refusal is a wall.** No option he declines. What puts the creature beyond help is his own hope, not the limits of what Harry can do. Hermione's comfort is part of the wall: kind, correct, useless.
- **Harry does not settle the argument** before the creature speaks. The corridor demonstrates what the Mirror asserted; he must not have reached the conclusion first.
- **No visible crying from Dracaena.** Glassy eyes and unsteady steps, nothing Harry can be sure of (Q-035).
- **Harry does not know what Mudblood means.** The slur passes unanswered in front of him, and the reader feels it even though he doesn't.
- **Verso's death has no stated cause,** and nobody asks.
- **Nothing joins this corridor to Sectumsempra.** The reader does it or doesn't.
