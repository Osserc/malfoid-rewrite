# Which One Is Real

The corridor's second debt. **The subject is Moody and the scene is not about Moody.**

## Purpose

September left two charges standing. **The verdict** — *you looked hideous back there* — is answered at the balcony at Christmas. **The carry** is answered here: she has spent six months deciding it meant nothing, and this is where she says so and is corrected.

It also does the thing the year has been avoiding. Harry is the apprentice of the man who put her in the hospital wing, and she has watched him laugh with him.

## Why it waits until now

- **Before the Ball there is no reason to raise it.** Moody is not at the centre of either of their minds while Harry is working himself up to ask her.
- **After the Ball she is holding her breath.** One night does not undo four years; she does not entirely believe him yet, and she will not go near a dangerous subject in case it costs her the thing she has just been handed ([sleeping-beauty](../../threads/sleeping-beauty.md)).
- **The Goblet is what changes it.** Her standing defence since September is that Potter is decent to everybody and it costs him nothing — and **an impartial instrument that gains nothing by lying looked at Harry Potter and named her** (🔒 DIV-005). She cannot run that sentence past a referee. She can refuse a kindness and an amends; **she cannot refuse a verdict.**

## Beats

1. **She raises it, and she quotes the flask.** Not the corridor — *you laughed with him, and I was holding on to you while you did it* (DIV-097).
2. **The question is his own, handed back.** *Which one is real?* — the exact charge he laid on her in the hospital wing, which she never answered.
3. **Harry has no tidy position and is not given one.** It was monstrous. He cannot give the man up. **He does not understand that either.** He does not defend Moody and promises nothing about him. *(In February he does not know Moody is innocent, and will not until June.)*
4. **Then the fact she has never had.** She has assumed the carry was reflexive decency, which is exactly why it is worth nothing to her. **He was furious with her when he picked her up.** He had just watched her be vile to his friend, he was angrier at her than he had ever been, and he did it anyway and did not think about it.
5. **He does not let go of her hands.** September reversed — in the hospital wing she was the one holding on. No speech required.
6. **She does not thank him and does not concede.** She goes quiet, or deflects, because that is what she does with anything she cannot repay. **What she leaves with is that she is not disposable.**

## Must keep

- **He never cites the Goblet.** It gives her the nerve to ask; his answer predates it by five months.
- **He never says they are alike.** Out loud it is an insult and it is smug. **The comparison is hers to make**, and she makes it silently or not at all.
- **He is not talked out of Moody and she is not talked into him.** Nothing about Moody is resolved here.
- **No tidy ending.** She is still angry about Moody when she leaves, and reassured about herself, which is a more interesting state than peace.

## What it sets up

- **The thread goes quiet, and it is not dragging.** It is waiting for the person who actually owes her an apology, who makes one in B5 through Harry (DIV-100, Q-139).
- **June annuls both of them.** Harry learns the man he refused to give up never did it; she learns she spent a year hating someone innocent of the only charge she had. **What survives is this conversation**, which was true regardless of whose hand it was.
