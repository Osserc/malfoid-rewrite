# The Third Task

## Purpose

The Tournament's finish, and the one place the book cannot let anybody off. Canon ends the maze in a tie offered out of mutual decency; ours ends it in a fight, because the prize is a life's worth of magic and all four champions know it (DIV-087). Harry wins, outright, wanting to, in front of the entire school — and that is what he has to carry.

It also disposes of the mechanism problem at the root. The Goblet never has to decide what to do with two claimants, because it never gets two.

## The shape

Four champions, one Cup, and a maze built to make them meet. Three fights:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **Cedric beats Fleur** | She is faster and more inventive and she is beating him. He does not break. | The audience learns the Task is a fight |
| **Harry beats Krum** | Outright and hard. Krum knows the shape of a contest; Harry does not fight in one (Q-123) | The audience learns what Harry is |
| **Harry beats Cedric** | Both at the height of the charge, neither will stand down, Harry first of all | The Tournament ends, and so does Cedric |

Nobody yields, because nobody knows where the Goblet draws the line between being stopped and giving up, and finding out costs a year of your magic (DIV-087). That is the floor. The appetite is on top of it.

## Beats

1. *(settled)* **The ribbon, before he goes in.** She ties it round his wrist — a charm for her brave knight — and Hermione overhears it (DIV-058). It is on him for everything that follows, and Lucius takes it off him in the graveyard (DIV-057).
2. *(settled)* **The maze herds.** Not a bracket anyone drew. It is an instrument for generating contribution and it funnels them together, and the 1994 committee chose it believing it was the safe option: no dragons, no drowning, just trained champions and wands, and nobody dies in a duel (Q-120). The sphinx and the rest of the furniture: Q-118.
3. *(settled)* **Cedric and Fleur.** He wins the way he will nearly win against Harry — by not losing. She is stopped for the second time in a year and has not quit either time (DIV-087).
4. *(settled)* **Harry and Krum.** Krum is the only professional in the maze and is beaten by a fourteen-year-old. Harry's edge is the one that beats Dracaena's technique: he has no shape (DIV-028, DIV-090). Exactly what he does is **Q-123**.
5. *(settled)* **The duel in front of the Cup.** Both riding the charge at its peak. Harry will not stand down because her ribbon is on his wrist and he wants to show her what her knight is made of. Cedric will not because he beat Harry in B3 only through a Dementor and a fall, Amos has been crowing about it since August, and he wants to hand his father something real — the rematch he offered in B3 and never got.
6. *(settled)* **Harry wins it, fair.** He is the better duellist, and this is the one book where he goes for the prize instead of giving it up. The prize is for her.
7. *(settled)* **Cedric does not stop.** From the ground, beaten, he Summons the Cup. Harry takes it out of the air, being also the better Seeker. Cedric catches his leg as the Portkey goes.
8. *(settled)* **Touch is not claim.** The Portkey carries whoever is in contact; the charge goes to whoever claimed. Cedric's hand is on Harry's ankle and not on the handle, and that inch is why he is the spare.
9. *(settled)* **They think the Task is still running.** The charge takes minutes to settle and is churning in Harry the whole time, so neither of them reads the graveyard as the end of anything. The boys' misreading and Voldemort's window are the same fact.
10. *(settled)* **Their last exchange is about the bout.** Then canon, at speed.
11. *(settled)* **The echo asks for two things:** take him back to his father, and well done. The congratulation is Cedric accepting that he lost. Harry can neither refuse it nor believe it.

## What the school sees

Relayed, all of it (DIV-088).

- **Dracaena** watches him win wearing her ribbon, and then watches the relay go dead and stay dead.
- **Amos Diggory** watches his son lose and then vanish.
- **The judges** witness the contribution, which is what judges are for (DIV-087).
- Nobody thinks Harry killed him — they saw both boys go. But several hundred people saw Harry beat Cedric Diggory minutes before Cedric died, and Rita Skeeter is in the building. **Q-125.**

## Afterwards

- *(settled)* **He tries to give the Diggorys the gold.** Not a canon beat; ours. It is the only thing he has for them besides the body, he knows what it is worth, and the attempt is for him as much as for them. Where the money ends up: DIV-062, Q-124.
- *(settled)* **He never connects any of it to November,** when Krum told him why you take what is still takeable out of a match you have lost (DIV-066, DIV-090). Nothing in the text joins them.

## Must keep

- The ribbon is on his wrist from before the maze until Lucius Summons it (DIV-057, DIV-058).
- One claimant. The Goblet never adjudicates a tie.
- Harry wants to win, and wins. No fake sportsmanship and no accident.
- Cedric is not diminished: he loses to a better duellist, refuses to stop, and says so afterwards.
- Harry's victory is his own, not Crouch's (DIV-086, DIV-087).
