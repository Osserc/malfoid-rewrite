# The Ferret

## Purpose

Canon's beat, re-aimed. It still delivers the reader's best clue to what is wearing Moody (DIV-086), and it now does three other jobs: it proves Dracaena is not on her way to being nice, it gives Harry the most public act of his year, and it hands the Yule Ball its question.

**The target changes, because the canon version cannot happen here.** Canon's Draco hexes Harry in the back as he walks away. Ours spent the back half of B3 proving to herself that she can own a mistake and put it right, to live up to his example (🔒 DIV-003, DIV-017). She will not go for him, and she will certainly not go for him from behind.

## Beats

1. *(settled)* **It opens as a routine corridor sneer and escalates.** Her usual insults, aimed at Hermione — very possibly "Mudblood" with real venom in it, which is the last time it is said in Harry's hearing without consequence (DIV-074). **Hermione snaps**: *you are never going to take Harry, because you are a nasty and vile person.* True, said in heat, and about character — and **Dracaena hears *take* as *take from me***, which is the whole misreading in one word (DIV-041). She is not insulted, she is threatened, and told she has lost by the girl who is always at his side. She also agrees with the first half, which is why it goes off like a struck match.
2. *(settled)* **She goes for Hermione, in the open, in front of a crowd.** No ambush, no back turned — she is not hiding it, which is the only version of this she would do. **She is still vicious to everybody who is not Harry.** Hermione is the rival in her head (DIV-041), Hermione is not him, and Hermione is Muggle-born and therefore barely a person to her yet (DIV-074). Three reasons stacked, and the third is the only one she would admit to.
3. *(settled)* **Crouch takes Moody over and transfigures her** — his one unforced act of the year, no plan value in it, spite at Lucius Malfoy spent on Lucius Malfoy's daughter (DIV-086).
4. *(settled)* **Harry laughs.** Briefly and honestly: she makes a very fine ferret. This is not him enjoying her being punished — he is not reading an assault on a girl, he is looking at a small white animal where his rival was standing.
5. *(settled)* **Then the bouncing, and it stops being funny at once.** What Harry feels is horror, and it is specific: *that is her.* He has no framework for it and does not examine it, which is the point — the reader is ahead of him, as usual (DIV-037).
6. *(settled)* **Ron thinks it is the best thing he has ever seen.** Canon's reaction, kept, and now it is a measuring stick: Harry has drifted off the party line and nobody has noticed, least of all Harry.
7. *(settled)* **McGonagall stops it and makes him change her back.** Canon's beat, unaltered.
8. *(settled)* **Harry picks her up and carries her to the hospital wing.** On impulse, unprompted, with nobody having asked him and nobody quite able to stop him. He does not plan it and could not explain it afterwards. The whole corridor watches.
9. *(settled)* **The hospital wing, and the first thing anybody says to her about it.** Harry stays. What he says is not an argument about blood prejudice — he hasn't the words and would not attempt it. What he means is **that what she did was hideous, and that it makes her ugly.** He says it as a boy who has just watched the girl who flew a condemned hippogriff to a wanted man for him be vile to his friend: bewildered and disappointed, not moral.
   - **It is the only channel that reaches her, and he has no idea.** Her objections run through taste — wrong things are ugly things (DIV-070) — so a verdict about ugliness is the one kind of judgement she can act on, and he arrives at it by accident, because he is not making an argument at all.
   - **This is where the slur stops.** From here she drops "Mudblood" in front of him, sometimes catching it mid-word ([the-little-basilisk](the-little-basilisk.md)). **It is the promise she breaks in B6** ([sleeping-beauty](../../threads/sleeping-beauty.md), DIV-015). It is not a conversion: it is a thing she has been told is beneath her, by the only person whose opinion can reach her.
10. *(settled)* **And she cannot make the rest of it mean anything.** He carried her. Her account of that is that Potter is decent to everybody and it costs him nothing, so it is not about her — she cannot bring herself to believe he would want someone like her near him ([dracaena-malfoy](../../characters/dracaena-malfoy.md)). The conversation is small, awkward and unresolved, and nothing like the Ball. **Three months later she asks the same question out loud on a balcony** ([yule-ball](yule-ball.md)).

## What it sets up

- **The Yule Ball balcony.** Three months later he tells her he wishes the night would never end, and she asks whether she is allowed to dream — *someone like me* — and uses his given name for the first time. She is thinking of this corridor: what he saw her do, and what he did about it ([yule-ball](yule-ball.md), DIV-095).
- **The school's running verdict on the two of them,** which is already under way by the flying lesson (DIV-091) and which Pansy is assembling all year (DIV-093, DIV-094). A Potter carrying a Malfoy through a crowded corridor in September is the first entry in that ledger, and when he asks her to the Ball nobody is entirely astonished.
- **Hermione.** Her attacker is carried off in her friend's arms while she is still standing there. Nothing is said about it and it does not go away; it is the earliest sediment under the rift at the end of the year (DIV-063).
- **Why Harry stops tolerating the slur.** This is the cause, and the hospital wing is the moment (beat 9). It is also where the promise he holds her to in B6 is made.
- **Moody.** Harry files the cruelty under *that is how Moody does things* and goes on to like the man anyway — and he carries it. **The payoff is in the confession:** the one act of the year Harry had to excuse is the only one that was not Moody's, and the real man has to live with having transfigured a student and thought it just (DIV-086, Q-117).
- **Lucius.** An Auror assaulted the Malfoy heiress in front of witnesses, and this family does not let that go. What he does about it is **Q-117**, shelved by the author.

## Must keep

- She never attacks Harry, and never attacks anybody from behind.
- Harry's objection in the hospital wing is aesthetic, not ethical. He must never be made to deliver a lesson about prejudice.
- She is not converted by anything in this scene. She is cruel in it, on purpose, to someone who is not Harry.
- Harry laughs at the Transfiguration and not at the bouncing.
- He carries her, unasked, in public, and does not know why.
