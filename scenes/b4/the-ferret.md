# The Ferret

## Purpose

Canon's beat, re-aimed. It still delivers the reader's best clue to what is wearing Moody (DIV-086), and it now does three other jobs: it proves Dracaena is not on her way to being nice, it gives Harry the most public act of his year, and it hands the Yule Ball its question.

**The target changes, because the canon version cannot happen here.** Canon's Draco hexes Harry in the back as he walks away. Ours spent the back half of B3 proving to herself that she can own a mistake and put it right, to live up to his example (🔒 DIV-003, DIV-017). She will not go for him, and she will certainly not go for him from behind.

## Beats

1. *(settled)* **It opens as a routine corridor sneer and escalates.** Her usual insults, aimed at Hermione — very possibly "Mudblood" with real venom in it, which is the last time it is said in Harry's hearing without consequence (DIV-074). **Hermione snaps**: *you are never going to take Harry, because you are a nasty and vile person.* True, said in heat, and about character — and **Dracaena hears *take* as *take from me***, which is the whole misreading in one word (DIV-041). She is not insulted, she is threatened, and told she has lost by the girl who is always at his side. She also agrees with the first half, which is why it goes off like a struck match.
2. *(settled)* **She goes for Hermione, in the open, in front of a crowd.** No ambush, no back turned — she is not hiding it, which is the only version of this she would do. **She is still vicious to everybody who is not Harry.** Hermione is the rival in her head (DIV-041), Hermione is not him, and Hermione is Muggle-born and therefore barely a person to her yet (DIV-074). Three reasons stacked, and the third is the only one she would admit to.
3. *(settled)* **Crouch takes Moody over and transfigures her** — his one unforced act of the year, no plan value in it, spite at Lucius Malfoy spent on Lucius Malfoy's daughter (DIV-086).
   - **It is an assault, not a prank.** He beats her half to death with the floor. She comes back battered, bleeding, spitting blood and in shock. **Canon's register is slapstick and ours cannot be**: we have called this a Death Eater venting hatred on a child, and it should read as one.
   - **The cost is not medical.** Pomfrey mends it inside the hour — broken ribs are an afternoon's work here — so nothing moves in the calendar and there is no convalescence.
4. *(settled)* **Harry laughs.** Briefly and honestly: she makes a very fine ferret. This is not him enjoying her being punished — he is not reading an assault on a girl, he is looking at a small white animal where his rival was standing.
5. *(settled)* **Then the bouncing, and it stops being funny at once.** What Harry feels is horror, and it is specific: *that is her.* He has no framework for it and does not examine it, which is the point — the reader is ahead of him, as usual (DIV-037).
6. *(settled)* **Ron thinks it is the best thing he has ever seen.** Canon's reaction, kept, and now it is a measuring stick: Harry has drifted off the party line and nobody has noticed, least of all Harry.
7. *(settled)* **McGonagall draws on him.** Not canon's reprimand: **she puts her wand on an Auror in a crowded corridor and demands he put the girl back.**.
   - **And because Harry carries her off, Moody cannot march her down to Snape as he does in canon. McGonagall takes *him* to Snape instead** — offstage, a thing that happened, never dramatised. **He never does anything remotely like it again, so it reads as an intervention that worked.** It was not one and could not have been, and nobody finds that out for nine months.
8. *(settled)* **Harry picks her up and carries her to the hospital wing.** On impulse, unprompted, with nobody having asked him and nobody quite able to stop him, and with blood on him. He does not plan it and could not explain it afterwards. The whole corridor watches.
   - **He is furious with her while he does it**, and that is the fact the whole year turns on. She never learns it until March (DIV-104).
9. *(settled)* **She will not let go of him.** She clutches him and cannot be made to stop; Pomfrey cannot treat a patient holding on to a third year and settles for Harry holding her hand while she works.
   - **Not romance, and never staged as any.** It is a girl who has just discovered she is not exempt. She has duelled and she has been slapped (DIV-069); **she has never been assaulted.** So she latches on to the nearest thing she reads as competence and protection: the boy who held off a horde of Dementors for her and killed a basilisk at twelve.
   - **It is her ledger working**, which is why it is in character. She is not being emotional, she is valuing — and under shock she values correctly for the first time in her life.
   - **It concedes nothing.** She would deny it afterwards and is humiliated by it, and it costs her nothing because she refuses to count it.
   - **It sets the balcony up exactly.** Here she grabs him because she cannot help it; at Christmas she asks whether she is *allowed* to.
   - **And it is the first time reality touches her body.** The second is the Mark (DIV-015). Dreams were kinder than the facts both times.
10. *(settled)* **The hospital wing, and the first thing anybody says to her about it.** Harry stays. What he says is not an argument about blood prejudice — he hasn't the words and would not attempt it. What he means is **that what she did was hideous, and that it makes her ugly.** He says it as a boy who has just watched the girl who flew a condemned hippogriff to a wanted man for him be vile to his friend: bewildered and disappointed, not moral.
   - **It is the only channel that reaches her, and he has no idea.** Her objections run through taste — wrong things are ugly things (DIV-070) — so a verdict about ugliness is the one kind of judgement she can act on, and he arrives at it by accident, because he is not making an argument at all.
   - **It comes after Pomfrey has finished**, when she is sitting up with her face back on — **not while she is holding his hand.** The armour gets no chance to work, which is crueller in effect and far kinder in conduct.
   - **He is angrier about the contradiction than about the cruelty.** Not *you hurt my friend* but *then which one of you is real* — which keeps him from prosecuting a girl who has just been beaten, and which is the charge she carries to Christmas and hands back to him in March (DIV-104).
   - **She does not get a pass for being frightened.** What she did was serious whatever was done to her, and the scene must never trade one for the other.
   - **She promises nothing, concedes nothing, and changes nothing she would admit to.** This is a plant, not a turn. She is handed a verdict and carries it, unanswered, for three months — **the promise comes later and must come later** (Q-135).
11. *(settled)* **And she cannot make the rest of it mean anything.** He carried her. Her account of that is that Potter is decent to everybody and it costs him nothing, so it is not about her — she cannot bring herself to believe he would want someone like her near him ([dracaena-malfoy](../../characters/dracaena-malfoy.md)). The conversation is small, awkward and unresolved, and nothing like the Ball.
12. *(settled)* **Then she stews, for three months.** She would like to be open with him and cannot make herself do the work, because in her own accounting there is nothing in it: it would not undo the corridor, and conceding she was wrong with nothing coming back is not a transaction she has a form for ([dracaena-malfoy](../../characters/dracaena-malfoy.md)). **What she carries to the balcony at Christmas is this, unresolved** — *am I allowed to dream, Harry? Someone like me?* is his verdict coming back out of her mouth, three months later ([yule-ball](yule-ball.md)).

## What it sets up

- **The Yule Ball balcony.** Three months later he tells her he wishes the night would never end, and she asks whether she is allowed to dream — *someone like me* — and uses his given name for the first time. She is thinking of this corridor: what he saw her do, and what he did about it ([yule-ball](yule-ball.md), DIV-095).
- **The school's running verdict on the two of them,** which is already under way by the flying lesson (DIV-091) and which Pansy is assembling all year (DIV-093, DIV-094). A Potter carrying a Malfoy through a crowded corridor in September is the first entry in that ledger, and when he asks her to the Ball nobody is entirely astonished.
- **Hermione.** Her attacker is carried off in her friend's arms while she is still standing there. Nothing is said about it and it does not go away; it is the earliest sediment under the rift at the end of the year (DIV-063).
- **Why Harry stops tolerating the slur.** This is where the charge is laid (beat 9). She stops saying it in front of him, quietly and without ever conceding anything — and **the promise he holds her to in B6 is made after the Ball**, not here (Q-135).
- **The carry, answered in March.** She decides the carry meant nothing because Potter is like that with everybody — and after the Second Task the Goblet takes that sentence away from her, so she finally says it out loud and is corrected ([which-one-is-real](which-one-is-real.md), DIV-104).
- **Moody, and why Harry gets away with not flagging him.** **He is angry at her as well**, and the cheapest thing a fourteen-year-old can do with two incompatible feelings is stop looking at them. **And no adult treats it as a scandal** — Ron thought it was the best thing he had ever seen, McGonagall stopped it, the man kept his job. Children calibrate off adults. By the time it could matter it has set as *that is how Moody does things*, and the mentorship is built on top of it (DIV-102). Harry files the cruelty and goes on to like the man anyway — and he carries it. **The payoff is in the confession:** the one act of the year Harry had to excuse is the only one that was not Moody's, and the real man has to live with having transfigured a student and thought it just (DIV-086, Q-117).
- **Lucius.** An Auror assaulted the Malfoy heiress in front of witnesses, and this family does not let that go. What he does about it is **Q-117**, shelved by the author.

## Must keep

- She never attacks Harry, and never attacks anybody from behind.
- Harry's objection in the hospital wing is aesthetic, not ethical. He must never be made to deliver a lesson about prejudice.
- **Nothing is promised and nothing is mended here.** She leaves the hospital wing with a charge against her and no way to answer it. Anything that looks like her starting to change in September steals the balcony's question (Q-135).
- She is not converted by anything in this scene. She is cruel in it, on purpose, to someone who is not Harry.
- Harry laughs at the Transfiguration and not at the bouncing — and with the beating this hard, the laugh is very short indeed.
- **The clinging concedes nothing**, and the reprimand still lands. Neither one cancels the other.
- He carries her, unasked, in public, and does not know why.
