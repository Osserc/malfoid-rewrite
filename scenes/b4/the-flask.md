# The Flask

Two scenes, one motif: a lesson, and then the lesson passed. Rungs one and two of the year's four (DIV-102).

## Purpose

**It burns canon's loudest clue, on the page, in front of everybody.** The flask is the thing every reader of the original is watching, and Moody drinking from it settles nothing. **A second person drinking it and staying himself is the only version that disposes of it** (DIV-097) — and the shout gives the reader's held breath somewhere to go, so the alarm is spent as a laugh instead of fizzling. They were right to be frightened of the flask and wrong about why, which is the book's whole clue structure in miniature.

**It plants the word the Butterbeer breaks.** Not *pour* — ***check***. The rule Moody teaches here is trust but verify, and the tell at the Three Broomsticks is a man taking a stranger's drink without doing it (Q-114).

**It is DIV-096 in a single gesture.** The doctrine with no spell in it: adult standing he has not earned, handed over by the most decorated Auror in Britain, in the year he is being hunted. Nobody else in the castle offers him that.

**And it teaches Harry that he is safe with Moody**, which is true, and untrue. That is the scene's real payload — see *Must keep*.

## Beats · November, after the First Task

1. **Moody tells him to come up when Pomfrey has done with him.** An instruction, not an invitation, with the enclosure still emptying.
2. **The office.** The trunk is against the wall and nothing whatever is made of it. *The reader's eyes are on the desk.*
3. **"Well done, Potter."** And then the debrief, run exactly as if Harry were a new Auror back off his first job: what he did right, what he did late, what would have killed him against something that could think. **He is the first adult all year to treat the dragon as an engagement Harry survived** rather than an ordeal he was put through.
4. **Then the flask, with no ceremony.** Ogden's Old Firewhisky. He takes a swig and offers it across — *for a job well done* — as procedure rather than mischief, because his frame is the Auror office and not a school, and the boy's age never arises.
5. **Harry drinks.** It burns, his eyes water, he coughs — and **nothing whatever happens to him.** *(The beat the scene exists for.)*
6. **"CONSTANT VIGILANCE!"** Harry nearly goes off the chair.
7. **The lecture.** He'd have been dead twice over if Moody had been a Dark wizard: he could have handed him poison just then and Harry would have swallowed it. **He should have checked it.** Rookie mistake.
8. **Harry's defence, and it is sound.** *But you drank it too, sir — you'd have died as well.* **"Good thinking."** And then: an antidote taken beforehand, which is a classic trick, so the reasoning is correct and still not enough. **Harry is never made to look stupid in this scene.**
9. **The rule, and the blessing.** *Never accept something you don't know where it's been.* Then the smile: **do that, and you'll go far.**
10. **The full-marks version he had in his head** — and it should be visible to the reader, whether or not he says it: Harry casts to detect, finds nothing, drinks, and Moody approves. ***Which is exactly what happens in February***, so this is a plant and not a fantasy. **The offer was sincere and the test was real at the same time.** Not a trick dressed as a gift and not a gift spoiled by a trick: one act. He cannot hand the boy anything without turning it into a drill, and he meant both halves.
11. **Then down to the party, late, with whisky in him, and he does not say where he has been.** Gryffindor Tower is a children's party and he has just been treated as something else. *He is pleased with himself about it, which is the point.*

## Beats · February, coming up from the lake

**Short.** They are on their way to celebrate and Dracaena is waiting a few feet off; nothing else happens in it.

1. **Moody calls him over and throws him the flask.** *For a job well done!*
2. **Harry grins, draws his wand and casts a detection charm he looked up in his own time.** Nothing in it. **He drinks, without coughing — much — and throws it back.**
3. **The verdict is a compliment wearing a criticism.** *You were only dead once this time, Potter, you're improving. Next time, eyes on the target instead of the misdirection — oldest trick in the book.*
4. **And that is the year's reading instruction, said out loud and missed.** Harry watched the flask and stopped watching the man who handed it to him. **The flask is the misdirection; Moody is the target.** True tactically, true of the book, and true of what happens in June.
5. **Moody says nothing whatever about Dracaena.** He said all of that in December, days after the Ball (DIV-101). **She holds on to Harry harder throughout**, and tells him why within the week ([which-one-is-real](which-one-is-real.md), DIV-104).

## Must keep

- **It is the real Moody, start to finish.** Crouch stays asleep; no take-over and no reason for one. The warmth is genuine, the lesson is genuine, and both belong to the man who is also the instrument (DIV-086).
- **The reward is sincere and the test is real, and they are the same act.** If he is only playing a prank, the scene is a cheap gag; if he is only being kind, there is nothing to teach. He means it twice.
- **The word *check* has to be explicit.** The Three Broomsticks depends on it (Q-114).
- **He is teaching Harry that the rule has exceptions**, and the first exception is him. *You are safe with me.* It is true — and in the five hours of the year when Crouch is riding him it is not, and Harry will never be able to sort which was which. **This is the year's cruelty in miniature, and it is the real man's doing, not Crouch's**, which is far worse than hanging the breach on the thing in the trunk.
- **No mischief and no conspiracy.** No *don't tell Dumbledore*, no shared crime. Moody thinks it correct, and the comedy is the gap between his frame and a castle's.
- **One measure, and Harry is not drunk.** Not a comic drunk scene, no aftermath, no hangover.
- **The trunk is in the room and nothing is said about it.** The reader is watching the wrong container.
- **The flask is never anything but whisky**, here or later.
- **February is a page at most**, and it is warm. No lecture, no shout, no reversal — he set a test in November and the boy passed it, and that is the whole beat.
- **Two drinks, not one.** A single swig in a lifetime is sad; a habit between the two of them is something Molly Weasley can be furious about (DIV-098).

## What it sets up

- **The Butterbeer at Hogsmeade.** *Check* is planted here so that not checking reads there — and Harry, who watches the whole exchange from under the Cloak, misreads it as the best of them slipping sometimes (Q-114, [alastor-moody](../../characters/alastor-moody.md)).
- **Grimmauld Place, a year later.** Sirius tries to get Harry a Firewhisky at the prefect party and Moody says he already has the taste for it, in front of Molly Weasley (DIV-098, [the-prefect-party](../b5/the-prefect-party.md)).
- **Rung three, in the spring.** Moody walks in on the Conjunctivitis practice and approves instead of reprimanding, and *that* is where the doctrine goes — *hit them first, hit them hard, and you won't be an easy target when you go down* (DIV-102, DIV-096).
- **The Malfoy stay-behind**, days after the Ball and so between rungs one and two, and the *junior Auror* address that runs through all of it (DIV-101, DIV-102).
- **Moody's guilt.** He fed the boy, drilled him, debriefed him and delivered him — and told him he was safe. This is the scene the reader will remember when he admits what he spent the year doing (DIV-100).
