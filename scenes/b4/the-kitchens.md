# The Kitchens

Winky's decline and death, Dobby's failed attempt to pass on what Harry gave him, and S.P.E.W. walking into the middle of it. Not one scene but a thread through the book, mostly re-lit from canon's own material.

## Purpose

To prove, three books before Dobby dies, that what happened in the B2 corridor does not generalise. The remedy exists; it needs an elf willing to beg and a person with nothing to own them with, and there is exactly one of each in the world.

It also puts the rule in front of the reader so Dobby's death in B7 is a recognition and not an explanation.

## What canon does

Crouch sacks Winky at the World Cup after she is found with Harry's wand. Dobby brings her to Hogwarts, where Dumbledore gives them both work and Dobby takes wages. Winky will not take wages, drinks butterbeer steadily, mourns her master, deteriorates visibly, and is still alive at the end of the series. Hermione founds S.P.E.W., knits hats and hides them around Gryffindor Tower to free the elves by trickery; the elves refuse to clean the tower, and Dobby collects every hat himself.

## Ours · DIV-077

Every canon beat above stays on the page. Only what it means changes.

- **The sacking is a death sentence and Crouch knows it.** Not as mechanism — nobody has that — but as the thing everyone knows: a dismissed elf does away with itself (DIV-075). He is not disposing of a witness. He is telling her to go and kill herself, in front of witnesses, and nobody present thinks it remarkable.
- **Her decline is dissolution.** The drinking, the shaking, the not being herself: she is coming apart, over months, and canon wrote the symptoms for us.
- **Dobby is trying to save her,** all year. He drags her to the castle, finds her warmth and a corner and meals, and keeps her — out of Harry's vault, because she will not take money from anyone and Dobby has Harry's to spend (DIV-076). Harry never notices he is paying for a second elf.
- **They are the castle elf's guests.** It feeds whoever turns up, because that is what it was made for and because no order has ever forbidden it (DIV-075). **No wizard decides anything, and Dumbledore does not know** — or never mentions it; he is not omniscient and nothing about this would reach him.
- **And there is no work for her.** Canon gives Winky chores she neglects. Here the chores were never real: the castle has needed nothing from anybody for a thousand years. She is the guest of a house that does not need her, with no house and no function, which is exactly the thing she cannot survive.
- **She refuses every anchor, including his.** Crouch told her she was not worth housing and she believed him; she will not beg, and she does not want to be saved. Dobby cannot be her anchor anyway — he is barely one himself.
- **She has to eat now, and will not.** A dismissed elf is unhoused, and an unhoused elf is hungry — most likely for the first time in its existence ([house-elves](../../setting/house-elves.md), Q-102). So refusing Dobby's money is refusing food, and taking either would be admitting she means to go on living. She drinks instead, because butterbeer is not sustenance but oblivion, which is the one thing she wants and the one thing she will take from him. Dobby paying for her meals is forcing food on someone who means to starve.
- **Canon's wage is cut.** Dobby never asks the school for work or pay. He has a person, which is all he has ever wanted, and being kept by Harry is the arrangement rather than a debt to be worked off. It also keeps Harry's vault the only money in the system, which is what Harry not noticing depends on.
- **She dies,** in the lull after the Second Task, **with Harry, Ron, Hermione, Dobby — and Dracaena — there.**

## How Hermione finds out *(draft)* · Q-093

Early, and from Harry, not from an enemy.

- **Harry says he has an elf.** *Has*, never *owns* — he would not use the other word and does not think in it, which is exactly why Hermione's horror lands on the fact rather than on him. She is appalled, and says so, at length.
- **Dobby turns up without being called.** Not because he needs anything: he feels that **Harry** is in a pinch, because Hermione is hounding him, and an elf always knows when its people are calling even when they haven't (DIV-075). Better than Harry summoning him, which would have proved her point for her.
- **So she gets it first-hand, from the elf.** It is nothing she was prepared for.
- **So she learns what freeing does early in the year,** in time for it to ruin everything she is building, and from the person she least wants to hear it from, which is Harry.
- **What she does with it** is the rest of her life's work (Q-100).

**The hats are undecided** and may not survive at all. If they stay, they are traps: freedom hidden around Gryffindor Tower for elves to pick up by accident, the castle's elves refusing to go in as self-preservation, and Dobby collecting every one because he is already out and immune. The wrinkle that saves her from having been a murderer is that **she is not the castle's household** — hats from a fourth-year may have done nothing whatever, and nobody can tell her, and she will never know (Q-099). Current lean: Dobby talks her out of them, and she only understands what she nearly did after Winky dies. Q-093.

## Why it takes her eight months *(settled)* · DIV-075

The reader watched Dobby start to go in under a minute, so this is the first question they will have, and the answer is the whole horror of the thing.

**Dissolution runs at the rate the elf lets go, and nothing else is counting.** Dobby went fast because he had wanted to go for decades and had never been allowed to; the sock opened a door he was already standing at. **Winky does not release. She erodes.** She does not want to live either, but she has not finished *wanting* — her master back, the verdict overturned, to be worth housing — and every one of those is a hand still holding on. So she comes apart slowly, over eight months, conscious of it the whole time.

**Which inverts what the reader thinks they learned.** The quick death is the merciful one and almost nobody gets it. What Crouch ordered at the World Cup was not what nearly happened in the corridor; it was this.

**Why nobody's grief saves her** *(draft)* · Q-093. The remedy in B2 ran on grief witnessed — somebody minding that you are going (DIV-075) — and Winky spends eight months among people who mind. It has to fail, and the reasons should be visible on the page.

- **Dobby minding is not it.** He is another cast-off, and a cast-off objecting to Crouch's verdict confirms it rather than overturning it. The grief that could reach her would have to come from the side that did the dismissing.
- **Hermione minds about elves** *(Claude's proposal, not settled)*. She does not mind about *Winky*, not for most of the year — Winky is a cause, and being a cause is not being wanted, and Winky can tell the difference. It costs nothing on the page and gives Hermione the wound she carries into B5: she minded enormously and never once asked Winky a question about herself.
- **And by the time anyone minds personally, the window has gone.** Harry caught Dobby in the first minute of it. Nobody catches anything in month seven.

## The grave *(settled)* · DIV-077

**Dobby asks Harry to bury her,** and Harry does.

- **An elf asking for something is the extraordinary part.** He is not asking for himself and does not frame it as a favour; he simply thinks Winky should be honoured, and has decided it, and says so.
- **He has been working on his own mortality since the corridor,** quietly, without knowing it is already under way. He nearly went himself, he has now watched someone go properly, and he has concluded that this is what ought to happen when an elf dies — which is him arranging his own funeral two books early and not knowing it (DIV-050).
- **It is the first elf grave in Britain.** A thousand years and not one, because housed elves do not die and nobody has ever minded the ones that did. The nearest thing to an elf memorial in the country is the row of heads on the stairs at Grimmauld Place (DIV-075).
- **What is left of her is dust,** as it will be of him. They bury that.
- **And a Malfoy is standing there for it,** which has never happened either.
- **It is why Dobby gets a tomb in B7.** Nobody asks that time. Harry simply knows to, and the knowing is Dobby's own doing ([malfoy-manor](../b7/malfoy-manor.md), DIV-050).

Open: where she is buried, who digs, and what, if anything, the marker says (Q-094).

## Dracaena, and the better master *(settled)* · DIV-077, Q-098

She is in the room when Winky goes, and it is where her class's account of elves stops working for her.

**Before: "a better master."** In the weeks after the Second Task she works out how Harry keeps the elf he took from her family — which is to say that he has never once thought about it — and tells him flatly that providing for his own elf is his responsibility and he had better be a better master at it. Noblesse oblige, the code she was raised in, and in the short term **she is entirely right**: it is the only advice anyone gives all year that would keep an elf alive.

- **He has never used the word.** Not master: friend, since the corridor (DIV-076). She uses it because every elf alive does and it is the only vocabulary she has (DIV-075). They are arguing about different things and neither notices.
- **She is lecturing him about an elf she believes he killed.** She has known since B3 that Dobby is somehow alive and has no idea how, and she cannot raise it without admitting she cared (Q-098, Q-101). What the scene is really about is the thing neither of them will say.
- **The joke underneath it.** He has been keeping Dobby lavishly for two years, by accident, out of a vault he never thinks about — and is, that very month, feeding a second elf without knowing it.
- **And it sets Hermione off,** since from the other chair this is the Death Eater's daughter arguing for better masters rather than none. Both of them are right about different timescales and neither can act on the other's.

**Then she watches.** She comes because Hermione has been talking about it where she could hear, and because it involves Dobby, and because by this point in the year nobody can keep her out of a room Harry is in.

- **The stories she was raised on say the elf goes away.** Every good house tells it that way, nobody has ever had to watch, and the softness of the nursery version is part of what keeps the institution standing (DIV-075).
- **It is not going away.** It is a suicide, in a kitchen, over eight months, and she is standing there for the end of it. Far worse than the thing she was told, and nobody ever told her because nobody has ever seen it.
- **She makes no argument of it and does not change sides.** When she breaks with her cause it will be out of fear and then love, not out of being convinced (Q-083). This is not a conversion; it is something she has seen and cannot unsee, and nobody who gives her the old version afterwards will be quite believed.
- **It is why she can end up on Hermione's side.** Not friendship and not agreement: she is the one pureblood in Britain who stood in the room, which is what makes her the only one who could ever back the method quietly, long after the war (Q-100, Q-096 — post-story, not canon).
- **And she is in a room with Ron and Hermione, not fighting,** very likely for the first time.

And she is there for the burial, which is a thing no Malfoy has ever done (*The grave*, above).

## Hermione and Crouch *(settled)*

She has disliked Crouch since the World Cup, when he sacked Winky in front of her (canon). **This is where it settles into hatred for good,** and it stops being a political objection. She has watched a man's discarded servant take eight months to stop existing, in front of her, because he told her she was not worth housing.

It costs nothing and pays twice. Canon spends the back half of B4 on Crouch's unravelling and his son, and she is the only person in the castle who will not extend him an ounce of sympathy. And S.P.E.W. stops being a hobby on the day it acquires a body.

## Must keep

- Crouch knows.
- Dobby spends the year trying to be Harry, and fails.
- Winky will not take money; Harry's vault keeps her, and Harry never knows.
- Nobody is employed. No wages, no Dumbledore, no work — they are the castle elf's guests.
- Dracaena is there when Winky dies, and she tells Harry to be a better master first.
- Winky takes eight months because she does not release; Dobby went in minutes because he did. The slow one is the ordinary one.
- Dobby asks Harry to bury her, and that is why Dobby gets a grave in B7.
- She refuses, and the refusal is shame, not despair alone.
- She refuses food as well as money. Butterbeer is oblivion, not sustenance.
- All of them are there when she goes, Dracaena included.
- Hermione's dislike of Crouch settles into hatred here, and never softens.
