# Before the Forest

## Purpose

Their goodbye, and the hope that carries her through the final sequence (DIV-023). It reverses DIV-015: this time, whatever happens, she won't fall into despair.

## Beats

All beats are *(settled)*, part of DIV-022.

1. Harry has seen Snape's memories and is walking to the forest to die. He and Dracaena are back together by now, since the Manor raid ([malfoy-manor](malfoy-manor.md), DIV-052).
2. In canon he can't stop for Ginny. Here he stops for her.
3. He tells her never to give up hope.
4. He has a faint hope of his own. He has few reasons for it, but Dumbledore is one man and may be wrong, and didn't he always tell them to keep faith and be brave? With that glimmer he marches to his death.
5. She doesn't know exactly what's going to happen. She trusts him and prepares for the worst.

## ⚠ Conflict, unresolved · DIV-114 vs DIV-022

**The author has settled that Harry knows what he is and uses it** — he holds the Horcrux in front of himself and makes it take the curse (DIV-114). **Beats 4 and 5 and the Notes below were written for a Harry who is marching to his death with a faint hope**, and they no longer describe the same scene.

Both DIV-022 and DIV-114 are settled, so **nothing here has been rewritten**. What has to be decided (Q-147):

- **How much of the plan is in this scene.** He has one, it is a gamble, and the "faint hope" line is now a different thing — not *Dumbledore may be wrong* but *this should work*.
- **Whether she is told.** "She doesn't know exactly what's going to happen" survives either way, and is arguably stronger if he has a plan and still cannot promise her anything.
- **What the stop for her is now for.** The goodbye works harder if he is walking into a gamble than if he is walking into a certainty — and *never give up hope* becomes something he has a reason to say.

## Must keep

He stops for her. "Never give up hope." She trusts him without knowing what he's going to do. Whatever happens, she won't succumb to despair as she did in B6.

## Notes

His hope doesn't cheapen the sacrifice, as long as he'd go without it. The sacrifice counts because he's willing, not because he's certain. His hope also makes what he tells her honest.

The payoff is King's Cross, where he goes back for her ([kings-cross](kings-cross.md), DIV-048). Stopping for her is the seed.
