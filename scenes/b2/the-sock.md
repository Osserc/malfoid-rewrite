# The Sock

## Purpose

The one working exit from the house-elf curse, performed by accident, by a twelve-year-old, out of ordinary decency, with nobody present who understands it — Harry least of all. It is the hinge the whole elf design hangs on (DIV-075), and it sets up Dobby's five-year unravelling and his death (DIV-050).

It also quietly answers Harry's own want: the only person who could solve this is the one who has never had a house either.

## What canon does

Harry slips the diary to Lucius with his sock inside. Lucius strips the sock off and flings it aside, Dobby catches it, and is free. Dobby is delighted. Lucius goes for Harry and Dobby blasts him down the corridor.

## Ours · DIV-076

Canon's trick stands, start to finish. What changes is what freedom turns out to be.

## Beats

1. The trick, as in canon: Harry returns the diary, Lucius finds a filthy Muggle sock wrapped round it, and flings the thing aside as rubbish. **Dobby catches it.** The agency is Dobby's; what Lucius supplies is indifference.
2. **He is not throwing it at anyone.** No temper, no aim, no malice — a nobleman discarding something unclean, which is both less dramatic than canon and much worse. The whole charge against him is carelessness.
3. **He knows what he has done, and he does not attack Harry.** Our Lucius does not brawl with a twelve-year-old; it is beneath him, and he has never needed to. What he does instead is stop at the end of the corridor and say something like *I hope you know what you have just done, Mr Potter*, and leave. Everyone knows a dismissed elf does away with itself (DIV-075). Harry has no idea what he has just been told, and neither has the reader, and both can come back to it.
4. Canon has Dobby blast Lucius down the corridor. **Cut** — there is no attack to answer, and nothing for him to defend.
5. **Dobby starts to go.** Not at once and not dramatically: he thins. Harry has no idea what he is seeing. Dobby, joyful a minute ago, understands perfectly and is not distressed by it.
6. Harry asks what's wrong. Dobby tells him, plainly and without self-pity, what happens to a dismissed elf. It is what they do. It is proper. **He is not asking for anything** — he already wants to go, and has done for a long time. A hearth-spirit kept by a household it cannot love has nothing to be, and he has been that for longer than he can account for ([house-elves](../../setting/house-elves.md)).
7. **Harry understands the enormity of what he has done,** all at once: he meant to save him and he has killed him, and there is nothing to undo it with.
8. **Harry cries.** This is the hinge of the entire design. Not an argument, not an offer, not a clever idea — a boy crying over an elf.
9. **Dobby changes his mind.** That is all that happens here, and it must not be written as a mechanism unlocking. He had decided he was finished and he meant it; he looks again, and finds he is not quite done. What he finds is the thing he has wanted for longer than he can account for — a great house, full of a happy, kind family — and, for the first time, somebody in front of him who might make a version of it real. He does not know the dream is Narcissa's (DIV-075, [narcissa-malfoy](../../characters/narcissa-malfoy.md)); he has never known where it came from.
10. **He starts crying too,** and decides to give living another go. He asks whether he can be Harry's elf — the shame of asking loses to wanting something. **He is not asking for freedom and never was.** Freedom is what he wanted ten minutes ago, because it kills him; what he wants now is a family to keep ([house-elves](../../setting/house-elves.md)).
11. **Harry accepts, and refuses the word.** Not master: friend. He means it as decency and not as a theory, and does not know the condition is the entire point, or that he qualifies at all only because he hasn't a house for the curse to take hold of.
12. The hold catches — wrong, partially, in a way the curse has no provision for (DIV-075). Dobby stays.
13. **The half-joke.** Afterwards, Harry points out that Dobby is going to be waiting a very long time for the big happy family, seeing as he is twelve. It is a joke, and it is a promise, and it is the only thing in the scene either of them says lightly. **He never delivers it.** Kreacher gets it instead (DIV-078).
14. **Nobody sees any of it.** No adult, no portrait, no witness. The only time in a thousand years it has ever worked happens in an empty corridor and is never reported — and there was no method in it to report (Q-100).

## Why he goes so fast *(settled)* · DIV-075

Readers will ask it the moment they meet Winky, so it is worth having straight. **Dissolution runs at the rate the elf lets go, and nothing else is counting.**

Dobby had wanted to go for decades and been unable to do anything about it, because a bound elf cannot leave and cannot refuse. The sock does not kill him. It opens a door he has been standing at for a very long time, and he goes through it at once.

So this is not a rescue from an accident. **It is talking someone off a ledge,** and it works because of what the person on the ledge has been waiting to hear, which is that anyone minds. Winky, who does not want to live either but has not finished wanting things, takes eight months to erode instead — the ordinary way, and far worse ([the-kitchens](../b4/the-kitchens.md), DIV-077). The reader gets the merciful version first and only learns in B4 what Lucius actually did here.

## Afterwards *(settled)*

- **The Burrow.** Harry has nowhere to put him, so he gives him the house he wishes were his own, and thinks nothing of it. Molly has always wanted an elf and Dobby is treated well. It is lodging, not a demesne — he is anchored to Harry, not to the building (DIV-075).
- **He is hungry.** For the first time in his existence: the house that used to sustain him is gone, and an unhoused elf has to eat like anything else ([house-elves](../../setting/house-elves.md), Q-102). He has no idea what the feeling is, and there is nobody alive who could tell him.
- **The first meal.** Molly's cooking, at the Burrow, in the summer of 1993. A house's output is one thing and a woman standing at a stove is another, and nobody has ever worked *for him* in his life. He bawls his eyes out. **Harry is not there** — he spends that summer in Privet Drive first and then Diagon Alley — so Dobby tells him about it afterwards, at length and at speed, and Harry cannot work out why it matters so much.
- **The false dawn.** Out of the yoke and fed, he feels better than he has ever felt, and so does anyone who looks at him. It is the last good stretch he gets, and it is the reason nobody is alarmed by anything that follows (DIV-050).
- **Harry's money is Dobby's.** Functionally complete access to the vault. It is what keeps him fed, which nobody including Harry ever frames that way. He cannot walk into Gringotts, but the funds are there to call on, and Harry never gives it a second thought. It is how Winky gets kept in B4 (DIV-077), and it is how Harry finally pays the Weasleys back.
- **The absurd improvements.** The Weasleys will not take anything from Harry, so he does it sideways: a small room added on for himself that nobody asked for, the structure quietly shored up, repairs on transparently silly pretexts, all of it paid for out of his vault and built by Dobby. Molly cannot refuse a room for Harry. Dobby gets to be the architect he has always wanted to be — enlarging a house is the oldest thing an elf does — and it is the happiest he has been in centuries.
- **And it is costing him.** Every one of those improvements comes out of a thing with no house to draw on. Harry's generosity and Harry's homelessness are killing him together, and neither of them knows it (DIV-050).
- **Clothes do nothing now.** Unhoused, the curse has nothing to grip, so Harry could hand him a jumper every day of the week to no effect, and Dobby could walk away whenever he liked. He doesn't. That is what makes Harry a shelter and not an owner, and it means every day Dobby stays is chosen (DIV-075).
- **The Manor goes cold** from this night, and no one ever connects the two ([house-elves](../../setting/house-elves.md)).
- **He begins to unravel.** From here he slowly reverts towards the true shape, and the reversion is what kills him over five years. Harry's "Dobby, have you gotten taller?" in B3 is the first sign (DIV-050).

## Dracaena's side *(settled)* · DIV-076

She knows the folk fact too. So from where she stands, Potter did not steal her family's elf — he killed him, out of a Gryffindor's idea of a good deed, and took a dependent from the family that owed him protection. By her own code that is both theft and cruelty, and under our rules she is right about the second.

Then the elf keeps turning up alive, for years, and she is the only person in the world positioned to notice that this is impossible. She has no idea what it means and nobody to ask. By the time she has to ask him a favour (Q-076, DIV-065) she is asking something that should be dead. Q-098.

## Must keep

- Harry never understands what he did, in this scene or afterwards.
- Lucius discards the sock. He does not throw it at anyone and he does not raise a hand to Harry.
- Dobby catches it himself. Canon's blasting is cut.
- "I hope you know what you have just done, Mr Potter."
- **Dobby wants to go, and is calm about it.** He is not begging and not bargaining.
- **Harry cries, and Dobby changes his mind.** Not a lock opening, not a rule firing: he finds he is not quite done, because there is suddenly something to hope for.
- Dobby cries too, and only then asks.
- "Not master — friend," said as decency, not as cleverness.
- The joke about waiting a long time for a big family. It is never kept.
- He has never eaten before. The first meal is Molly's cooking, he cries over it, and Harry misses it.
- He goes fast because he had already decided, years ago. Winky is the ordinary rate.
- No witnesses.
