# The Sock

## Purpose

The one working exit from the house-elf curse, performed by accident, by a twelve-year-old, out of ordinary decency, with nobody present who understands it — Harry least of all. It is the hinge the whole elf design hangs on (DIV-075), and it sets up Dobby's five-year unravelling and his death (DIV-050).

It also quietly answers Harry's own want: the only person who could solve this is the one who has never had a house either.

## What canon does

Harry slips the diary to Lucius with his sock inside. Lucius strips the sock off and flings it aside, Dobby catches it, and is free. Dobby is delighted. Lucius goes for Harry and Dobby blasts him down the corridor.

## Ours · DIV-076

Canon's trick stands, start to finish. What changes is what freedom turns out to be.

## Beats

1. The trick, as in canon: Harry returns the diary, Lucius finds a filthy Muggle sock wrapped round it, and flings the thing aside as rubbish. **Dobby catches it.** The agency is Dobby's; what Lucius supplies is indifference.
2. **He is not throwing it at anyone.** No temper, no aim, no malice — a nobleman discarding something unclean, which is both less dramatic than canon and much worse. The whole charge against him is carelessness.
3. **He knows what he has done, and he does not attack Harry.** Our Lucius does not brawl with a twelve-year-old; it is beneath him, and he has never needed to. What he does instead is stop at the end of the corridor and say something like *I hope you know what you have just done, Mr Potter*, and leave. Everyone knows a dismissed elf does away with itself (DIV-075). Harry has no idea what he has just been told, and neither has the reader, and both can come back to it.
4. Dobby blasts him down the corridor (canon) — unprovoked by any attack, which makes it Dobby's own act rather than a rescue.
5. **Dobby starts to go.** Not at once, not dramatically: he thins. Harry has no idea what he is seeing and Dobby, joyful a minute ago, understands perfectly.
6. Harry asks what's wrong. Dobby tells him, more or less cheerfully, what happens to a freed elf. It's what they do. It's proper.
7. **Harry asks why he doesn't just find someone else.** The question no wizard has ever asked an elf, asked out of plain ignorance. Dobby's answer is the shame: an elf does not beg.
8. **He begs anyway.** He says he doesn't want to go yet, and tells Harry what he wants — a great house, full of a happy, kind family. It is the most humiliating sentence an elf can say and the only one that works, and he says it to the first person who ever treated him as a person.
9. He asks Harry to be his master.
10. **Harry accepts, and refuses the word.** Not master: friend. He means it as decency and not as a theory, and he does not know that the condition is the entire point. He also does not know he qualifies only because he hasn't got a house for the curse to take hold of.
11. The hold catches. Dobby stays.
12. **Nobody sees it.** No adult, no portrait, no witness. The only working remedy in a thousand years happens in an empty corridor and is never reported.

## Afterwards *(settled)*

- **The Burrow.** Harry has nowhere to put him, so he gives him the house he wishes were his own, and thinks nothing of it. Molly has always wanted an elf and Dobby is treated well. It is lodging, not a demesne — he is anchored to Harry, not to the building (DIV-075).
- **Harry's money is Dobby's.** Functionally complete access to the vault. He cannot walk into Gringotts, but the funds are there to call on, and Harry never gives it a second thought. It is how Winky gets kept in B4 (DIV-077), and it is how Harry finally pays the Weasleys back.
- **The absurd improvements.** The Weasleys will not take anything from Harry, so he does it sideways: a small room added on for himself that nobody asked for, the structure quietly shored up, repairs on transparently silly pretexts, all of it paid for out of his vault and built by Dobby. Molly cannot refuse a room for Harry. Dobby gets to be the architect he has always wanted to be — enlarging a house is the oldest thing an elf does — and it is the happiest he has been in centuries.
- **And it is costing him.** Every one of those improvements comes out of a thing with no house to draw on. Harry's generosity and Harry's homelessness are killing him together, and neither of them knows it (DIV-050).
- **Clothes do nothing now.** Unhoused, the curse has nothing to grip, so Harry could hand him a jumper every day of the week to no effect, and Dobby could walk away whenever he liked. He doesn't. That is what makes Harry a shelter and not an owner, and it means every day Dobby stays is chosen (DIV-075).
- **The Manor goes cold** from this night, and no one ever connects the two ([house-elves](../../setting/house-elves.md)).
- **He begins to unravel.** From here he slowly reverts towards the true shape, and the reversion is what kills him over five years. Harry's "Dobby, have you gotten taller?" in B3 is the first sign (DIV-050).

## Dracaena's side *(settled)* · DIV-076

She knows the folk fact too. So from where she stands, Potter did not steal her family's elf — he killed him, out of a Gryffindor's idea of a good deed, and took a dependent from the family that owed him protection. By her own code that is both theft and cruelty, and under our rules she is right about the second.

Then the elf keeps turning up alive, for years, and she is the only person in the world positioned to notice that this is impossible. She has no idea what it means and nobody to ask. By the time she has to ask him a favour (Q-076, DIV-065) she is asking something that should be dead. Q-098.

## Must keep

- Harry never understands what he did, in this scene or afterwards.
- Lucius discards the sock. He does not throw it at anyone and he does not raise a hand to Harry.
- Dobby catches it himself.
- "I hope you know what you have just done, Mr Potter."
- Dobby begs, and the begging is the hard part, not the asking.
- "Not master — friend," said as decency, not as cleverness.
- No witnesses.
