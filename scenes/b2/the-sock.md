# The Sock

## Purpose

The one working exit from the house-elf curse, performed by accident, by a twelve-year-old, out of ordinary decency, with nobody present who understands it — Harry least of all. It is the hinge the whole elf design hangs on (DIV-075), and it sets up Dobby's five-year unravelling and his death (DIV-050).

It also quietly answers Harry's own want: the only person who could solve this is the one who has never had a house either.

## What canon does

Harry slips the diary to Lucius with his sock inside. Lucius throws the diary down, Dobby catches the sock, and is free. Dobby is delighted. Lucius goes for Harry and Dobby blasts him down the corridor.

## Ours · DIV-076

Canon's trick stands, start to finish. What changes is what freedom turns out to be.

## Beats

1. The trick, exactly as in canon: the diary, the sock, Lucius's temper, Dobby free.
2. **Lucius knows what he has done.** He knows the folk fact — a dismissed elf does away with itself — because everyone does (DIV-075). He throws it anyway. He is in a rage and the thing is only an elf. Whether this registers on the page here or only in B7 is open; the reader should be able to go back and find it.
3. Dobby blasts him down the corridor (canon). Lucius leaves.
4. **Dobby starts to go.** Not at once, not dramatically: he thins. Harry has no idea what he is seeing and Dobby, joyful a minute ago, understands perfectly.
5. Harry asks what's wrong. Dobby tells him, more or less cheerfully, what happens to a freed elf. It's what they do. It's proper.
6. **Harry asks why he doesn't just find someone else.** The question no wizard has ever asked an elf, asked out of plain ignorance. Dobby's answer is the shame: an elf does not beg.
7. **He begs anyway.** He says he doesn't want to go yet, and tells Harry what he wants — a great house, full of a happy, kind family. It is the most humiliating sentence an elf can say and the only one that works, and he says it to the first person who ever treated him as a person.
8. He asks Harry to be his master.
9. **Harry accepts, and refuses the word.** Not master: friend. He means it as decency and not as a theory, and he does not know that the condition is the entire point. He also does not know he qualifies only because he hasn't got a house for the curse to take hold of.
10. The hold catches. Dobby stays.
11. **Nobody sees it.** No adult, no portrait, no witness. The only working remedy in a thousand years happens in an empty corridor and is never reported.

## Afterwards *(settled)*

- **The Burrow.** Harry has nowhere to put him, so he gives him the house he wishes were his own, and thinks nothing of it. Molly has always wanted an elf and Dobby is treated well. It is lodging, not a demesne — he is anchored to Harry, not to the building (DIV-075).
- **The Manor goes cold** from this night, and no one ever connects the two ([house-elves](../../setting/house-elves.md)).
- **He begins to unravel.** From here he slowly reverts towards the true shape, and the reversion is what kills him over five years. Harry's "Dobby, have you gotten taller?" in B3 is the first sign (DIV-050).

## Dracaena's side *(settled)* · DIV-076

She knows the folk fact too. So from where she stands, Potter did not steal her family's elf — he killed him, out of a Gryffindor's idea of a good deed, and took a dependent from the family that owed him protection. By her own code that is both theft and cruelty, and under our rules she is right about the second.

Then the elf keeps turning up alive, for years, and she is the only person in the world positioned to notice that this is impossible. She has no idea what it means and nobody to ask. By the time she has to ask him a favour (Q-076, DIV-065) she is asking something that should be dead. Q-098.

## Must keep

- Harry never understands what he did, in this scene or afterwards.
- Dobby begs, and the begging is the hard part, not the asking.
- "Not master — friend," said as decency, not as cleverness.
- No witnesses.
- Lucius knew.
