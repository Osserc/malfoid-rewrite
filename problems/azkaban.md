# Azkaban and the Dementors

## In canon

The state keeps its prisoners in a fortress guarded by creatures that feed on the mind and can destroy a person entirely with a kiss. Innocents are held there without trial (Sirius, Hagrid). The Dementors breed. Nothing in the books offers a way to destroy one — a Patronus drives them off and that is the limit — so the arrangement reads as a fact of nature rather than a policy.

The heroes never do anything about it. Dumbledore objects and does not act. At the end of the series the Dementors are removed from Azkaban, offstage, in a line.

Canon also has them loyal to the Ministry straight through the first war and defecting only after Voldemort's return, which does not track: they sit in Azkaban for a decade while he is at his strongest, then change sides within two years of his coming back.

## Why it matters for us

- **It is an atrocity our heroes live beside.** Harry's third year has a garrison of them stationed around a school.
- **Once they can be killed, keeping them becomes a choice.** DIV-080 makes them destructible and puts a woman on the staff who has destroyed them and says so. Azkaban stops being scenery and becomes policy. The Ministry has hunted a species to the edge before, when it wanted to; the Dementors survive because they are useful, not because they are invincible.
- **It is first-rate DIV-008 fuel, aimed at us rather than at them.** The Ministry had a weapon that exterminates Dementors and kept the Dementors anyway, because they are useful. And it used an unauditable judgement-weapon on the homes of its political enemies, with a verdict nobody could appeal. The pureblood side gets a documented grievance that is not a lie told by losers: the castle itself tells the story, as a legend. See Q-016.
- **Minerva's position is the argument in person.** She has been a loud proponent of destroying them since the war, and it has cost her.

## Settled so far

- Dementors can be destroyed: a dangerous creature, not a fixture of the universe, and huntable the way canon's wizards hunted the British giants. Lustrum is McGonagall's method, not the only one (DIV-080).
- The Ministry knows it and keeps them regardless, because they are useful.
- We start tackling this in B3, with the garrison, the siege and Fudge's reaction to losing it ("precious manpower").

## Open

- **The first-war defection** (Q-105): did some go over to Voldemort then, and if so what became of them afterwards? Taking them back into service is the nastier answer and gives Minerva her grievance.
- **Where it lands.** B3 opens it; canon's B5 has Fudge insisting Dementors cannot leave Ministry control, which becomes a documented lie. Whether the series resolves Azkaban, and how, is unplanned.
- **The Kiss.** Still on the table as a sentence in B3 (Sirius). Untouched so far.

## Resolution

_Open._ DIV-080 settles the mechanics; the policy and its fate are not yet decided.
