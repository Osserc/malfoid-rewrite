# House-Elves

## In canon

House-elves are a sentient species bound in hereditary servitude, and most of them are shown as content with it. Hermione's campaign for them is played for comedy, and the story never resolves the issue.

## Why it matters for us

- DIV-008 gives the pureblood ideologues an actual point. Elf servitude is the institution most likely to make pureblood society indefensible, so whatever we decide here shapes how far that point can stretch.
- Dobby belonged to the Malfoys. That ties the issue directly to Dracaena's household in B2 and to Malfoy Manor in B7.

## Options

The one we took is below. Two framings we tried and rejected along the way, kept because the reasons matter:

- **Harry unbinds Dobby.** Rejected: if a twelve-year-old's decency can undo centuries of dark magic in an afternoon, the curse has no weight and the problem is trivial. Harry is not the second coming. He works inside the binding as, at most, an absentee master.
- **Nobody knows freeing is lethal.** Rejected: ignorance lets the society off. Everyone knowing, and nobody ever asking why, is both worse and more useful — it makes the whole of wizarding Britain complicit without requiring anyone to be a monster.

## Resolution

Elves are not a species. A house-elf is a house: where a family line has lived long enough, the building takes on the household's character and expresses it as a person, to keep the house and protect the people in it. Wizards bound them in dark magic before Hogwarts was built, and both sides forgot. The binding took their obedience and never touched their will, which is the cruelty of it; it dwindled them from near-human to what everyone knows; and the one remedy — an elf asking some other household to take it — is so shameful that no elf has ever used it.

It is used once, by accident, in a corridor in B2, by a thing that chose to beg over dying and a boy with no house for the curse to grip (DIV-076). Nothing is undone, nothing generalises, and nobody who could act on it understands what happened. Winky's death in B4 proves it doesn't generalise (DIV-077); Dobby's in B7 is what it costs (DIV-050); Kreacher's asking in B7 is the only person who ever repeats it, on purpose (DIV-078).

**Fix:** DIV-075. Written out in [setting/house-elves.md](../setting/house-elves.md).

- **It keeps DIV-008 intact.** The purebloods are custodians of a forgotten atrocity they did not commit and have never examined — damning without making them monsters, and defensible in the way DIV-008 needs.
- **It keeps DIV-035 intact.** One house, one elf, so there are a few dozen in Britain. They index the decline and could never be its engine.
- **Hermione gets the history and no remedy; Harry has the remedy, once, and no history.** They never put it together, and the institution still stands at the end.
- **The only real hope is Hermione's,** and it comes late: not freeing, which kills them, but a method for a bound elf to recover its natural state over time inside a household that has decided never to use what it holds. Slow, partial, procedural, and undesigned (Q-100). Kreacher at Malfoy Manor is the prototype (DIV-078).
