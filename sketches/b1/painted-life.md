# Painted Life

"Harry! Harry!"

The boy in question groaned as his bookworm friend called out to him. He raised his tired eyes from his essay and saw Hermione's big round eyes looking at him with the same spark they had when she knew she had an answer for a teacher's question and was dying to let everyone know.

"What is it, Hermione?", he yawned, and did his best to give her a welcoming smile.

Hermione did not notice at all how strained it looked as she continued, "I found them!"

"Found who?"

"The Dessendres!", Hermione snorted impatiently, "You asked if I heard of them, remember?"

"Oh, yeah, that..."

He didn't have the heart to tell her the only reason he did that was to get her off his back for a while: ever since she came back from the Christmas break and he made the mistake of telling her about the Mirror of Erised and his nightmares she stuck to him like glue, determined to make sure he was alright. She had always been a bit overprotective of him, going as far as insisting he did his homework beside her, so she could spot any errors before he handed it in, but this was new, and decidedly unpleasant. It made him feel weak, as if he would have fainted without her constant hovering around him.

"The books say they are an ancient family who could create Painted Life!"

Harry gave her a puzzled look, not quite sharing her enthusiasm, "Isn't Hogwarts full of living paintings?"

"Don't be silly Harry," she laughed, "our paintings are a simple trick. The Fat Lady isn't really there, she's just an echo of the real person. But the Dessendres created entire worlds, with real people living in their own Painted World!"

Harry saw Hermione's smile getting larger and larger as his mind started slowly putting the puzzle pieces together. She always did that when she saw someone listen to her and actually make an effort to work through what she said, instead of letting her run them over with a torrent of words.

"Then... that means... they could... paint my parents back?"

The girl's eyes sparkled as she struggled to contain her excitement, "I found out there's a Dessendre Painting here, at Hogwarts! Why don't we go ask its people? I'm sure we'll find something out!"

She grabbed Harry's arm with both hands and started dragging him towards the door, before halting and turning around to scan the common room, looking for Ron. She found him in a corner, playing wizard chess with Neville, and called out to him.

"Ron! Do you want to come with us?"

The red-headed boy was looking so intently at the board that he barely muttered a "To do what?" in response.

"We're looking for a Painting!"

"I think I'll give it a miss. You two have fun."

Hermione made a face at him, annoyed at his dismissal, but soon brightened as she linked arms with Harry and marched both of them out of the room.

______________________________________

They spent the better part of an hour trying to interrogate the other paintings to work out where to find the one they were looking for, to no avail. The Hogwarts paintings were either oblivious, or outright unwilling to talk about that particular work, and Hermione was now sulking, dejected, as the two walked around the castle, looking for more paintings to get information from. Harry felt a bit bad about it, she was doing it for him, after all. But before he could say anything, a haughty voice called out to them.

"On a date with Granger, Potter?", inquired Dracaena Malfoy, looking down at them from atop a staircase.

She stared in disgust at Hermione's arm, still linked to Harry's. The Muggle-born witch blushed slightly but did not move aside, pulling Harry closer to her as if to shield him from Malfoy.

"What's it to you, Malfoy?", Hermione shot back at her.

"We're looking for a painting, but we only know the artist's surname. Dessendre. The other paintings haven't been very helpful," he explained, hoping to defuse the situation before either girl said something that would end in a fight.

Malfoy's eyes narrowed at the name, and she descended towards them.

"And why, pray tell, are you interested in the Dessendres' magic?"

"Well, because Du-", and a small *ouch* escaped Hermione's lips as Harry stamped on her foot to stop her spilling their secret, "B-because we wanted to see the Painted People, they seem very interesting."

"And what would you know about that?"

Hermione forgot for an instant who she was talking to, and started rattling off everything she had learned while reading about the French wizard-artists.

"The Dessendres are an ancient line of wizards from France. Their ancestral magic made them able to craft entire worlds out of paint and populate it with beings that could act on their own, just like normal human beings. They produced countless Painted Worlds that unfortunately were lost when a fire ravaged their manor. Hogwarts has the only known Dessendre work left. The entire family perished in the accident, and so their magic was lost. Which is a real pity, I would have loved to talk with them, they sound so interesting..."

It was the wrong thing to say. Malfoy clutched her robes with barely contained fury, and was visibly struggling to avoid screaming at Hermione. After a few instants she seemed to calm down, enough to turn towards Harry and address him with her usual mocking tone, her mouth curved in an evil smile.

"'Interesting', she says. See Potter, this is why I told you to choose your friends more carefully. These *Mudbloods* have no respect for our traditions and our accomplishments, for them they are nothing but curios, to be poked and prodded for their entertainment, and then forgotten."

Hermione went still at the word Mudblood, and Harry felt her nails dig into his flesh. He gave her a quick, worried look before turning his attention back to Dracaena, annoyed at her usual arrogance.

"And are you going to tell us about them, or are you just here to heckle us?"

"I suppose I could lend you a hand. I would not be able to live with myself if I allowed anyone to suffer Granger's company."

She beckoned him forward as she passed him, inviting both of them to follow her. After exchanging a glance with each other, Harry and Hermione decided to oblige. After all, she might just be their best chance at finding the painting.

______________________________________

"First of all, Painted Life is anathema to regular paintings, they despise it. I believe it reminds them of their... condition. Which is why we're asking a different entity for directions."

Dracaena gave Hermione a condescending look, as if it were the simplest thing in the world and she couldn't even figure that out, as she looked around.

Hermione was still clutching Harry as she retorted "The book did not mention anything like that."

"Goes to show that books are a poor substitute for actual knowledge," she chortled, before continuing, "Ah, there he is."

She nodded towards the Gryffindor ghost, Nearly Headless Nick, and started walking towards him, straightening her robes as she approached him.

"Greetings, Sir Nicholas", she said with a courteous bow while holding the hems of her clothes, followed by a couple of "Hi Nick" from Harry and Hermione.

The spectre looked a bit surprised as he spun around and found himself face to face with Malfoy.

"Ah, Miss Malfoy, good afternoon. Harry, Hermione," he added with a nod, before fixing his eyes on Dracaena, "To what do I owe the pleasure?"

Armed with her most charming smile and her honeyed voice, she said "I was wondering if you could assist us with a matter of the castle's history. I am told you are one of the most knowledgeable ghosts in all of Hogwarts", then, with eyes downcast and a lowered voice, "Unless you think I ought to ask the Baron instead, since I do belong to his house after all..."

Harry and Hermione exchanged a look. Was she trying to butter up Nick? It did seem to work however, as the ghost straightened up, clearly pleased by the compliment.

"Now now, dear, no need to fret! I am more than happy to aid such a charming young lady. What would you require of me?"

"Much obliged, Sir Nicholas. We were looking for the Dessendre Canvas. Would you happen to know where it is?"

"Ah, the Canvas, a most woeful tale. It's not exactly hidden, but not displayed either. It should be located in the corridor that leads to the prefects' bathroom, on the fifth floor," then, with a more worried tone, he asked, "May I inquire as to why you would want to see it?"

"It seems that the task of educating Mr Potter and Miss Granger about it has fallen to me. I would not want them to get the wrong idea from some old books written by careless hands."

Nick's troubled expression softened considerably, "Yes, yes, that would be best. The Dessendres deserve to be remembered properly. Do you require an escort?"

"Oh, I would not dare to presume further upon your generosity, Sir Nicholas. I am already in your debt", she said with another bow.

Then, as the ghost nodded solemnly, she started towards the nearest staircase, followed by the other two.

"What did you just do to him, Malfoy?", asked Harry with a slightly wary look as they left the ghost to his own devices.

"Magic is not the only weapon in the arsenal of a witch, Potter", she smirked.

______________________________________

Harry had to admit that he was not particularly impressed by the Painting, not that he was trying to hide his displeasure. Hermione, however, was deeply enthralled by it.

She was staring at the odd looking stone figure sitting among mountains, seemingly sleeping like the rest of the Canvas's denizens. He looked like a clay figure of a man, with long, spindly arms and legs, but with a huge misshapen pot for a torso, and no head.

"Look Harry, if you look at him from above he has rocks in his chest! And the mountains, they look like they were crushed! Do you think he did it?", then her pitch rose as she pointed at it, "Wait, is he... grinding the rocks in there?!"

He was big enough for any onlooker to gaze into his cavity, and one could see the huge boulders churning in their container. Now that he took a better look at it, it looked rather like a huge cement mixer on legs.

"I suppose he is building that manor over there", said Dracaena, nodding at the unfinished building in the distance, floating in the sky. It looked like a child's crude imitation of a palace.

"So they *can* alter their environment! Normal paintings can interact with it, sure, but they aren't really capable of something like this."

Still not seeing what was so interesting about it, Harry turned to Dracaena, asking: "Are you going to tell us about the Dessendres now, or do we have to beg?", and he launched into his best impression of her talking with Nearly Headless Nick, hands clasped in prayer, "Your Highness Malfoy, could you spare some of your time to reveal to us your secrets?"

Hermione could not help but snigger as she inspected the half-built castle in the Canvas.

Dracaena regarded him with a smug smile before answering, "A poor attempt at flattery, but I shall humour you. Granger was at least a bit right, the Dessendres could indeed create Painted Worlds. What her books won't tell her, however, is what exactly happened to them."

"So they didn't die in a fire?", asked Hermione.

Dracaena shot her an annoyed look for interrupting her.

"They did. But surely you'd think it unlikely that an accomplished wizarding family would perish in a regular fire."

She looked at Harry, who was now a bit more intrigued by the story, before continuing. He did not like where Dracaena was going, and he barely heard Hermione ask whether they were murdered.

"That is certainly possible, but no signs of a magical blaze were found at the time. The reason why they did not put out the flames was because they were *inside* the Painting, and they had been for years."

Dracaena's voice darkened, and she sighed heavily as she resumed her tale, "It all started with the death of Verso, the son. Aline, mad with grief, Painted her own little, happy world in a Canvas, and abandoned her remaining family. Renoir, her husband, joined her in an attempt to drag her out. He never got the chance to save her, as the fire destroyed the Canvas, with them inside, Alicia included," then, as Hermione regarded her quizzically at the sudden mention of the name, she added, "The youngest daughter. Did not have much passion for painting, but it seems she joined her father's endeavour."

Harry went pale, feeling a knot in his stomach. He remembered Dumbledore's warning: a magic similar to the Mirror had nearly destroyed the Dessendres. Was this what he would have become, had he kept looking for the mirror? Would he have forgotten to live, lost as he was in an imaginary world?

"Her family. Verso. In the Canvas, was he... I mean, could he-", his voice was hoarse, and Hermione put a hand on his shoulder to comfort him.

Dracaena's voice was strangely soft as she saw his sad eyes, "Yes and no, Potter. Aline was an exceptional witch, so his Painted self was very much like him. This, the stories agree on. He was still, however, not the same person. He was a copy, an imitation, given memories not of his own and forced to live as the living ghost of a man. Which was Renoir's argument at the time. He thought it unfair to Painted Verso. As well as unhealthy for Aline. Not even they could stay long in a Canvas, I am told it affected their bodies quite badly, in the long run."

"I think he was right. Renoir, I mean. Running away into the Canvas was not going to end up well for Aline," Hermione said.

Dracaena sounded a bit sceptical as she addressed her, "Really? I should think she got what she was after. She merely had to ration her time better."

A heavy silence fell between them, as Harry thought about Aline's decision and the girls' remarks. Would he have done the same, in her place? If simply seeing the family that was taken from him made him stare into the Mirror for hours in the dead of night, would he have lived in a Painted World? With Painted parents? Forever? And what about Dracaena's words? Was it really possible, to indulge in dreams responsibly?

He thought back to how deeply the Mirror had affected him, and the more he recalled the hold it had on him, the more he agreed with Hermione. And yet... maybe Dracaena had a point. Maybe it was fine to just let yourself dream. And his parents' smiles were so warm, in the Mirror...

Sensing him tense up, the girl hugged him softly, eliciting a snort from Dracaena.

Trying to break the awkwardness, Malfoy added, in a low voice, "The only survivor of the Dessendres was Clea, who was away at the time, and whose Canvas you are admiring at the moment."

At the mention of Clea, the seemingly sleepy clay man stirred. A voice came from his chest, and it was akin to a rumbling of rocks falling from a peak.

"Is... Mistress... here?"

The three wizards startled, turning towards the source of the voice. Hermione was the first to regain her senses and address the creature.

"Erm, excuse me, sir, who is this 'mistress' you speak of? Is it Clea?"

The rock man seemed to perk up at hearing the name again.

"Yes... Mistress's name is Clea. She promised she would be back. She used to play with me all the time when she was small, like a pebble."

He pointed at the ground with a large arm.

"I haven't seen her in a while. I thought that if I were good, if I finished her Castle, she would be back."

As he turned to look at the floating building, Harry thought that the creature looked extremely sad, despite the lack of a face. His shoulders were slumped and his voice had taken on a distinctly sad tone.

"But I am not good at it. Not like Mistress was. And so I wait."

"Since when has Clea...?", Hermione asked Dracaena.

The Slytherin girl shook her head, "The Dessendres perished over a hundred years ago. Clea died a couple of decades after, leaving no children."

"So he's been waiting all alone the whole time?"

Dracaena could do nothing but nod at Hermione's question. Suddenly, Harry felt angry. Angry at Clea, for leaving the clay man alone. And angry at himself, for even thinking about his Painted family.

What would happen when he left them behind, growing up? Would they have remained forever trapped in their Canvas, mourning the departed son who was not theirs? And what if he died in there, without ever leaving the Painting?

But most of all, he felt sad for the creature. He did nothing to deserve this. Much like him.

"Can you tell us your name?"

Both girls turned to look at him in surprise.

"Harry, what are you-"

The rumble came yet again, carrying the creature's answer, "Mistress never gave me a name."

*That's it.*

He had to do something, anything, for this rejected creation, waiting for a person who was not only never going to arrive, but who had cast him away.

"I could come here. I mean, I can't come into the Canvas. But... I could keep you company. You don't deserve to live like this. Nobody... nobody does."

"No. Mistress will be back," said the creature, settling back down to gaze, in his own weird way, at the flying manor.

"But-!"

"Don't, Potter. There is nothing you can do for him."

Harry snapped towards her, but his anger instantly subsided when he saw Dracaena's eyes. They looked... sad, and a bit glassy.

*Is she...?*

But the moment passed. She sniffed and turned around, her usual sneering tone coming back.

"Consider the lesson over, Potter, Granger."

As she walked away, Harry noticed her steps were a bit unsteady.

Harry gave one last look at the clay man, and sighed, before letting Hermione guide him back to the Gryffindor common room.

He heard none of the kind words she had for him, about how noble it was he wanted to help but the creature really wasn't going to allow him to.

But he was grateful for her presence.
