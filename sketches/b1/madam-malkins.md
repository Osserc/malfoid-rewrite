# Madam Malkin's

A happy chime greeted him as he opened the door to the robes shop Hagrid directed him to, pointing to the sign that said "Madam Malkin's Robes for All Occasions" in an elegant cursive.

"Lindsay dear, we have a customer! Can you attend to him? Hogwarts robes, on the smaller side," said who Harry assumed to be the proprietress, in a warm and gentle tone, "And do tell the mannequins off, they've become rather unruly as of late."

The mention of unruly mannequins made Harry worried, and his eyes darted frantically around the room. He noticed one of them creeping up to him, a wooden human-like figure carrying what he thought were supposed to be formal clothes for a male wizard. When it realized Harry noticed him, it straightened its posture and put on the flowing black robe it was holding in one fluid motion, and started strutting in front of him with a very self-assured air about it.

"Yes, ma'am!" answered a different voice, much perkier and livelier.

It came as a relief, as two other mannequins holding a jacket between them were closing in, and the owner of the voice appeared out of nowhere in a flutter of darkness. She shooed the armatures away with a flick of her wand.

"Git, you gremlins! Or I'll have the lot of you for kindling!"

The mannequins quickly retreated towards their stands, sulking, before the young woman turned to Harry with a big smile.

"Come on with me, sugar, I'll have you fitted in no time at all."

Harry could only look upon the tall woman with awe and a little bit of fear as she bent towards him to get a better look at him. Her pitch-black hair was styled in a long ponytail, with no visible hair tie. Instead, a number of oversized dressmaker pins formed a tidy knot that kept her mass arranged in a neat style. She wore a very tight sable robe covering her from neck to toe that appeared to be made entirely out of crow feathers, and of such darkness that she looked like a walking skeletal crow. Keeping track of her was hard, if not for her very friendly, and thankfully rather fair, face. Harry would not have been able to tell where to look if not for the contrast between her skin and the feathers.

Her eyes flickered to his forehead, and he instinctively flattened his fringe to hide his lightning scar. He had had enough of the attention at the Leaky Cauldron, and he wasn't keen on having the mysterious clerk hound him like the other wizards did. Lindsay must have noticed his embarrassment, and she winked at him, ruffling his hair. Not entirely comfortable with her ministrations, he still shot her a look of worry.

"Oh, I get that all the time," she laughed, and pushed him gently down an aisle, "Now move along, darlin', let's get you under some proper light."

Still a bit disoriented but grateful that the woman knew what he needed and was more than willing to take charge, he walked towards the footstool Lindsay pointed at, in front of a large window. Near it there were two people, a small, squat witch dressed in mauve, who was definitely Madam Malkin, and a girl in a white dress she was fussing over. Noticing Harry, the owner spared him a second to apologise, before turning her attention back to the girl. "Sorry, dear, Lindsay can be a bit of a handful, but she does a splendid job."

Harry hardly registered Lindsay's scoff at the backhanded compliment. His attention was focused entirely on the other customer. She was a small girl his age with silky platinum blonde hair that fell behind her, and shone in the bright morning light. Her face was small and pointed, with aristocratic features right out of a fairy tale. She had her eyes closed and wore a patient expression, betrayed by the light tapping of her foot as she kept an arm straight out, allowing Madam Malkin to inspect the sleeve. She almost bathed in the glow, and Harry's mind suddenly went to that old Disney film he had managed to watch by sneaking behind the doorframe, the one time Aunt Petunia forgot to lock the cupboard.

The girl opened one silver eye, and her face lit up at the sight of Harry, as if finally something interesting had arrived to relieve her of her boredom.

"Hello. Hogwarts, too?"

Her voice was exactly what he thought it would be. It was low, smooth and soft, yet carrying a distinct note of someone used to commanding attention. Exactly like-

*The princess.*

What were the odds that she *was* Sleeping Beauty? He discovered that magic was real the previous day, so why not fairy tales as well?

"Are you Sleeping Beauty?" he blurted out before he could stop himself.

Madam Malkin and Lindsay barely stifled a laugh, while the girl was visibly taken aback and opened her other eye. Her pale cheeks were now a delightful pink as she addressed Harry.

"D-did you just call me beautiful?"

Forgetting himself, he stood on tiptoe to get a better look at her, which made her blush even more, but she did not retreat. Harry's mind was in a completely different place as the girl held her breath. He was thinking of castles, and princesses, and dragons, searching his mind for any recollection of that one film. After a couple of seconds, Harry leaned back, recalling that the princess had eyes of a different colour from the girl's silver.

She breathed a small sigh of relief when he put some distance between their faces.

"Oh right, she has purple eyes," he said, a bit wistful. But then a big smile appeared on his face, and added, "But yours are pretty too!"

"Well, I declare. How I wish somebody'd say that to me," sighed Lindsay dreamily, and she fluttered her eyelashes at Harry while the blonde girl stiffened at his remarks, at once both flattered and embarrassed, "Well, little Casanova? Are my own peepers pretty as well?"

Harry could not hide his fright as his eyes flicked to hers. They were quite dark, almost magnetic, and the make-up made her look intimidating rather than beautiful. Thankfully, Madam Malkin intervened before he could make even more of a fool of himself.

"Cut that out, Lindsay, you're scaring him," then she gestured to the free footstool, "Get on it, dear, Lindsay doesn't bite. At least not in front of me."

"Well, thank you kindly for nothing, young man," pouted Lindsay, but the sulk did not reach her eyes, which had a playful glint to them.

As Harry climbed, the girl turned to him and asked, in a curious tone, "Who is Sleeping Beauty?"

A bit startled, Harry tried his best to not sound like a blubbering fool, while Lindsay quickly wrapped him in a black robe.

"Ah, she's a princess, from... from a fairy tale. Sleeping Beauty, and she's Sleeping Beauty."

She let out a small giggle, before cocking an eyebrow, as if remembering something important.

"I don't know that story. Where does it come from?"

"It's from an old film, I think."

"Film? What's that?" then her voice lowered, "Wait, is that a Muggle thing?"

*Oh.*

Harry could sense he had made a mistake. He watched the glow in the eyes of his "princess" leave as she asked more questions. He was spared her now icy gaze by Lindsay, who stepped between them.

"You weren't lyin', ma'am, he's just a little bitty thing!" Her tone was playful, but Harry could see the pained expression on her face as she looked at him.

"Who do you take me for, dear? I've been doing this for ages!" puffed Madam Malkin, as she twirled her customer around to inspect another section of the dress.

Even with her back turned, Harry could feel the rising hostility in her voice, as she kept asking questions.

"Why did your parents teach you Muggle stories?"

"My parents are dead," he replied flatly. He was liking her less and less by the minute.

"Oh, sorry," she apologised, but her tone did not soften, "But they were *our* kind, weren't they?"

"They were a witch and a wizard, if that's what you mean."

Suddenly, the princess came back. She twirled around with a joyful smile, and said "Then it's alright! I can teach you all about our world! Goodness knows those Muggles could not do the job. Father says we shouldn't even allow Muggle-borns at Hogwarts, can you imagine not knowing anything about our traditions until you get a letter?"

Harry wasn't sure he wanted her to teach him anything. In fact, he did not think he wanted anything to do with her anymore. Beside him, Lindsay let out a small *tch*.

"I hope we're in the same House. I'm sure I'll be in Slytherin, like everyone in my family. And then we—wait, who's that?" she asked, nodding at the window behind Harry.

He turned around and saw Hagrid's familiar face, grinning at him while holding two large ice creams. Harry smiled at the huge man.

"That's Hagrid," he announced, proud of knowing something about the wizarding world that she did not, "He works at Hogwarts."

"Oh," said the girl. "The big one? He's staff, isn't he."

"He's the gamekeeper."

"Yes, that. Father says they keep him on out of charity. Apparently he lives in a shed at the bottom of the grounds, and when he's had a drink he tries to do magic and sets light to things."

"I think he's brilliant," said Harry coldly.

"Really?" she asked, absent-mindedly, clearly not even listening to him anymore at that point, "Anyway, how lucky that you met me! Don't worry, I'll bring you up to speed and you'll be one of ours in no time! Oh, and what's your name?"

He hated that he found her smile enchanting, after she insulted Hagrid and was ready to throw him to the wolves due to his upbringing. It wasn't his fault his parents died, and it also wasn't his fault he had Muggle relatives. He decided he really did not like her anymore, and he regretted ever thinking she was a princess. Even if she looked the part, she was really nasty. Like a very pretty version of Dudley.

"Ma'am, I'm all done here. I'll carry him on over to the counter, that alright? I'm sure we got other folks waiting on their robes," intervened Lindsay, and with a nod from Madam Malkin she took Harry by the hand and helped him down the stool, "Come on, sweetheart."

"See you at Hogwarts!" she chirped, as she waved at him.

He did not turn around as he walked away. He felt Lindsay's hand on his shoulder, and he looked up at her. She gave him a sad smile.

"I'm real sorry about that, sweetheart. Some folks get awful... passionate... about the subject of a person's birth."

Harry nodded. After all the nice people at the Leaky Cauldron, he had thought the wizarding world would be a kinder place than the Muggle one.

Lindsay handed him a bag with his robes, and laughed as she gave him back the extra gold Galleons he had handed over to pay for them.

Well, at least there were nice people like Lindsay. Even though she still looked a bit scary to him as she waved him goodbye with her feathery arms.

Dracaena was humming to herself as the seamstress kept fussing over her dress. The encounter with the boy left her in a very good mood, and she was still on cloud nine when her mother, Narcissa, came to pick her up.

"Mama! I made a new friend!" she said, running towards her to give her a big hug.

"Did you now, Dracy?" she asked, as she patted her head.

Madam Malkin handed Mrs Malfoy a bag with her daughter's new dress inside, neatly folded.

Narcissa summoned a small piece of parchment with a twist of her wand, and handed it to the seamstress.

"Many thanks as always, Madam Malkin. I'll be sure to visit soon for my own dress, when I have the time."

"Always a pleasure, Mrs Malfoy."

Once the Malfoy women had gone, Lindsay sighed.

"I wish we could just up and tell 'em no, ma'am. Did you hear the way that child talked about Muggle-borns?"

Now it was Malkin's turn to sigh.

"Lindsay, they keep us afloat. Look at this," she said as she handed over the blank cheque, "They don't even write the price themselves. We can't afford to lose them, goodness knows school robes don't earn us enough."

"—and then he told me my eyes were pretty too!"

Lucius sighed. He was already starting to loathe this boy she had met in the robe shop, yet another reminder of why she wasn't normally allowed to roam without supervision. She might run into some... undesirables.

"Dracaena, you are talking about a bumpkin raised by Muggles. You are better than that."

"Come on, father, he's not a *Mudblood*! I asked him! His parents were both wizards!"

"A half-blood at worst, then, I suppose," drawled Lucius Malfoy.

He was not keen for his daughter to get chummy with what might as well have been a Muggle boy, but that was at least a consolation. He knew his daughter was almost unstoppable when she made up her mind, but he was still not looking forward to her spending time with a stranger of dubious standing, even if he were to be sorted into Slytherin.

Until then, he had been very careful in selecting her circle of friends from his own connections: Parkinson, Greengrass, Nott... even the two little trolls the Crabbes and Goyles had produced, just to give her some muscle in case she needed a different kind of protection at school. This was the first real acquaintance she had made on her own, and he worried. He looked at Narcissa, who was looking at the beaming Dracaena with a lovely smile.

"Can you get me those books, mama?"

"What do you need Muggle books for, Dracaena?" enquired Lucius, suspicious.

"I want to see the princess!"

"I am sure we can get Dobby on it as soon as we're back home. What did you say it was called?" asked Narcissa.

"Sleeping Beauty!"
