# The Laughing Witch

"Professor, what is going on?"

"What are all these shrieks?"

The corridors of Hogwarts were crowded with students despite the late hours and the curfew, but Minerva McGonagall paid them no mind, holding a broom. As the Dementors outside cried out in anger and outrage, the witch decided enough was enough. They would have to be handled the old-fashioned way, same as during the war. With a flick of her wrist and a date, *Ambleside, 4th April 1978*, she manifested a silvery cat, who sped off towards the nearest stair. A murmur rose from the gathered students, with more and more joining, and was eventually replaced by a stunned silence as their Transfiguration professor started changing right before their eyes.

A series of controlled and methodical wand movements accompanied each of her steps, her clothes slowly morphing to take on a new guise. Tall, black knee-high riding boots replaced her slippers, her nightgown slowly changed into a great, faded tartan kilt, belted at her waist and drawn over her shoulder, a luckenbooth brooch pinning it at the shoulder. With an annoyed frown, she conjured a cloak, which billowed behind her before taking the form of a dragonhide jerkin which wrapped her torso. It had a very old look and was singed in places, as if its owner had walked through a great many fires.

Then the wand came to her hairnet, which took the form of an elaborate hair piece that styled her tresses into a long braid, with metallic inserts keeping its shape firm. With a last flick of her wand she summoned her signature witch hat, which now sported a pair of riding goggles fastened above the brim, and which was burnt in places. Then, lastly, a small insignia appeared on her chest. A golden sword and wand crossed over a dark red background. With a satisfied nod to herself, she lowered her broom and settled on it sideways with a small hop, and flew down the flight of stairs leading to the main hall.

Minerva McGonagall was going to war.

Balthier stood in front of the great window in the tower, alongside other Slytherin students. They had been awoken by the deafening screeching sound that now enveloped the entire castle, and they could now finally see its source.

It was a huge, black formation high in the sky, from which dark tendrils occasionally shot off towards the ground, where scores of Dementors were trying to force themselves into the castle grounds, only to be repelled by what looked like silver animals, which patrolled the walls and windows to bar them entry.

He was grateful that Nola was hugging his arm beside him, her presence a welcome help keeping him steady. He had struggled all year against the influence of the creatures, and he could still see the fire whenever he thought about them. One thing that gave him relief, aside from his girlfriend's support, was that at least he didn't have it as bad as the Potter boy. Balthier had been badly affected by their incursion on the Quidditch pitch, but he was grateful that he never fainted like the Gryffindor Seeker did. He could not imagine what that boy went through if he fainted, if the death of his own father and the burning of his own house were not enough to rob him of his senses.

It was Nola burying his face in her neck and holding him tight that prevented him from having a full-blown panic attack as he recalled the fire, the eerily golden flames swallowing his house, and worst of all the witch flying lazily over the smouldering ruins.

"Should we leave, Balty?"

Nola's soft voice pulled him away from his memories. He shook his head. He felt that something important was about to happen, and he had to be present for it.

"I'm alright, Nola. I want to see this."

"I've never seen them like this Minerva, what is going on?"

"They are wounded, Filius, and they seek to replenish their strength."

The tiny Charms professor was doing his best to keep up with the witch flying low beside him, his squeaking voice betraying his nervousness at the sudden emergency.

"I can't believe this. First Black, now a horde of Dementors! You were right, we never should have allowed it!"

McGonagall did not speak, but gave him a curt nod. She had been extremely vocal with her displeasure at the Ministry's decision to station an entire garrison of the dreadful creatures around the castle at the start of term, and had made no secret of her disapproval of Dumbledore's acquiescence. It took all of her restraint to not blast them all to ash when they swarmed the Quidditch Pitch earlier in the year, and it was only out of concern for Harry Potter, caught in the middle, that she stood down. Only Lee Jordan must have noticed how tightly she gripped the wooden railings of the stadium, and how she left scorching marks where her hands had been despite the torrential downpour. But the boy had the good sense to keep his mouth shut.

But now... now she had a case.

"Do you have it, Filius?"

"Oh, of course, here it is," answered Flitwick with a jerk of his wand, and a small, brass vial on a golden chain flew towards McGonagall's neck, clasping itself around it.

As they neared the main gate, they spotted the other professors already standing guard, their Patronuses watchfully guarding any entrance to the castle proper from the black frenzied mass besieging it. A *Dunfermline, 17th September 1980*, and her little tabby cat Patronus joined its brethren. The shrieks had grown so strong that they drowned the voices that greeted the two arrivals, at least until Flitwick cast a sound-deafening charm to allow them to converse.

"Minerva, we are barely holding them back!"

It was Professor Sprout, in an uncharacteristically frightened voice. McGonagall gave her a soft smile. The woman was no fighter, and she had already done more than could be asked of her.

"We will beat them back. Filius, keep them off me," ordered the fearsome witch, "I'm going to thin their numbers."

"Minerva, what are you-"

Sprout's question died on her lips as Flitwick slowly shook his head at her. With a powerful kick, McGonagall soared towards the billowing black mass in the sky.

It was the most pathetic display Daphne Greengrass had seen in her fourteen years of life.

Thynne, a boy two years her senior, a pureblood no less, barely holding it together while his Mudblood slag, Laurel, gently caressed his hair, his head leaning against her neck. Thynne's hand went white from how hard he was clutching at the railings of the tower, as the white-golden flames lit the night sky. It was unbecoming of his lineage, and it was even more infuriating that he did not seem to mind to be seen in that state. Turning her head towards the now blazing firmament, Daphne had to admit that McGonagall's spell was indeed impressive.

She heard the hushed whispers of her parents and their friends about Minerva McGonagall's exploits during the war, the very same that earned her the nickname "Hellfire" from her enemies. As McGonagall soared to the sky to face the gathered Dementors, surrounded by a shimmering silver mist summoned by Professor Flitwick, she thundered "Lustra nefas!" and a little shiny orb left her neck, speeding towards the creatures before erupting into a small sun. The entire student body could hear the faint sizzling sound that the flames let out, but it was still drowned by the cries of anguish of the Dementors now sent hurtling towards the ground, aflame. Oddly enough though, the fire did not spread. As the Dementors scurried away desperate to distance themselves from the blaze, the grass and the trees did not catch fire.

Daphne leaned forward a bit to get a better look at the battle on the ground, and she inadvertently touched Thynne's fingers, who did not seem to register it. Laurel did though, but only gave her a suspicious look before turning her gaze towards the small star. Their professors managed to liberate the grounds by siccing silver animal-like figures against the cloaked monsters, which were now fleeing the scene running for whatever passed for their life.

Suddenly, however, the light in the sky weakened, and the blazing orb rapidly waned. Thynne breathed a sigh of relief, which was destined to be short lived, as McGonagall pointed her wand at the rapidly shrinking sun. The little piece of wood in the witch's hand started gathering the remains of the pyre, which split into a crown of smouldering orbs above her head, like a halo. Then, as their professor sped downward on her broom, a low sound rose from her. It grew as McGonagall started directing the flames above her head towards the Dementors on the ground, and it took about five fireballs before everyone could figure out what it was.

Laughter.

Minerva McGonagall was laughing. It wasn't malicious, and neither was it cruel. It was a pure, joyous sound, with a tinge of mania.

And soon enough, McGonagall's laughter covered the whole castle, dominating the laments of the dark creatures.

"Filius, what in the blazes was that?!"

The battle, if it could be called that, lasted only a matter of minutes. Flitwick could only smile weakly as he eyed Professor Sprout, who was beside herself with both worry and awe at what her colleague had just done. If anyone told her that one day, she would have witnessed the strict and curt Transfiguration professor fly on a broom while laughing maniacally as she cast fireballs at fleeing Dementors, she would have told them to go to Madam Pomfrey. Or directly to St. Mungo's for a more thorough examination of whatever delusion they were under.

"That, Pomona, was Minerva 'Hellfire' McGonagall," said the tiny man, laconically.

Sprout was about to say something, but she was interrupted by the woman of the hour as she landed among them. Her already damaged battle garb now sported a new set of fresh burns and singes, and her right sleeve was completely destroyed, revealing her marked flesh.

"I would have liked to exterminate them all, but I will settle for this. Thank you for the cover, Filius."

"Always a pleasure, Minerva," he said with a bow.

"Miner-"

Professor Sprout was once again interrupted as the Minister of Magic himself stomped towards the gathered professors.

"McGonagall! That was an unauthorised use of the Lustrum incantation, as well as wanton destruction of Ministry property!" the rotund man screamed, red in the face, flanked by the executioner, Macnair. The man was eyeing McGonagall with a fearful look in his eyes.

With a seraphic smile and a serene voice, McGonagall addressed him first.

"Ah, Macnair. Would that you were in the field as well, I would have loved for you to taste my fire, as did many of your associates."

The huge man went red in the face yet struggled to stammer a retort at the teacher's taunt. Fudge, however, was furious.

"Dumbledore will hear about this! This is a severe violation-"

With a single fluid motion, McGonagall grabbed Fudge's collar, and her calm demeanour vanished, replaced by a much more stern and angered expression.

"The Dementors, which you and your goon brought here, were trying to force their way into the castle and get at my students, something they have been dying to do all year!"

Then she pushed him aside with a disgusted sneer, as she started walking away.

"I will hear your inane mutterings tomorrow. For now, I am in need of medical assistance."

Fudge, still incensed, could do nothing but stomp his feet and shout empty threats about "hearings" and "violations" and "procedures" and how "outrageous" her behaviour was.

"Has the fireplace told you any interesting secret, Balty?"

Nola's playful tone could not conceal the worry in her voice as she finally worked up the courage to address Balthier. He had been staring at the fire for hours, refusing to go back to sleep after the events of the night, and she resolved to remain by his side in the Common Room. She grabbed a blanket big enough for both of them, and simply kept him company through the night. She must have fallen asleep against him, because she did not recall Balthier gently holding her waist. Her Balty looked down at her with a smile.

"I never knew fire could be so beautiful."

Her eyes went wide with shock. Balty, so scared of flames, saying they were beautiful? She turned around and put a hand to his forehead.

"Are you alright, Balty? Should I accompany you to Pomfrey?"

Her boyfriend laughed softly at her fussing over him.

"Never you mind that. Rather, do you want to visit my house this summer?"

"Balty-"

"I want you to meet Mum," Balthier cut her off, "I'm done hiding."

Nola gasped in surprise. She had tried for the better part of their relationship to get him to tell his mother about them, only to be met with evasion. She knew why, of course. She was a Muggle-born, and Balthier a pureblood. His mother would never have approved of him dating a *Mudblood*. And she did understand his reasons, his mother was his only family left and he was afraid of disappointing her. So she learned to keep her mouth shut.

"Listen, I don't need your mother's approval. Forget about it, I don't want you to risk losing your mother."

"I cannot keep carrying this... shadow with me whenever I'm with you. It's time I burned it away," then, with a sly smile, he added, "But be sure to wear your best dress. It might help."

Barely containing her happiness, she hugged him tight under the cover of the blanket.

"Thankyouthankyouthankyouthankyou," she murmured, her mouth pressed against his neck.

The castle did not forget McGonagall's exploit the previous night, despite the addition of the news about Sirius Black's escape from the Ministry. It came as no shock that he managed to elude the manhunt, given that its would-be captors were now mostly fertilizing the ground as cinders. The rest of the fiends that managed to escape were refusing to come within any distance of the castle, much to the anger of the Minister of Magic, who still lamented the loss of "precious manpower".

Some students tried to gather some of the remains, but soon learned that no one could touch the piles of dust without suffering severe burns, and the entire grounds were cordoned off pending a magical decontamination to be carried out during the summer. This only increased the interest in Professor McGonagall, and sparked a myriad of theories to explain it. On her part, the teacher refused to acknowledge her actions at all, much to the frustration of the students, as McGonagall started threatening to hand out detentions to whoever mentioned "this outlandish notion" of her flying around cackling while destroying Dementors with magical fire.

From that night onward, however, "the Laughing Witch" took its place as one of Hogwarts' legends.
