# Viktor Krum

**Role:** Durmstrang champion, and the only other famous boy in the castle.
**Canon baseline:** Viktor Krum. Anything not overridden follows canon — including Hermione, which does not change.
**Changes from canon:** DIV-066, DIV-089, DIV-090, DIV-091

## The other famous boy *(settled)* · DIV-090

He is the only person in that castle who knows what Harry's life is: famous at seventeen, stared at, written about, assumed arrogant, and in fact only interested in being good at the thing. That is the whole basis of the friendship, and it is why his interest is not in the Boy Who Lived but in whether the boy can actually do it.

Canon spends him and gives him nothing back — an obstacle, Imperiused in the maze, and later a compliment relayed second-hand in B5 to talk Harry into teaching. Ours shows on the page what that compliment is supposed to establish.

- **The Snitch, at the weighing of the wands.** Their first conversation. Harry asks why he ended a match Bulgaria was losing (DIV-066). *"Captain told me to."* A pause. *"And I agreed."* He will not let Harry think he was a puppet, and he will not take the credit off the veteran who called it: it was a decision by a man who knew the game, and Viktor was humble enough to listen. It also tells Harry — who has no team, no captain, and is not speaking to Ron — something he cannot use.
- **The Duelling Club.** He joins with the other visiting students and keeps coming, because he is curious (DIV-028).
- **The Durmstrang challenger.** He watches Harry take the boy apart and approves: the student had been running his mouth about both of them, and Krum is glad to see someone put him in his place. He is already settled as not being the challenger himself (DIV-029).
- **Quidditch does the rest.** Two Seekers with no season to play, and Hooch's lesson ([the-flying-lesson](../scenes/b4/the-flying-lesson.md), DIV-091).

He gets no arc of his own, and this is not a rival-suitor thread.

## The maze *(settled)* · DIV-089

**He is the only professional in it.** Real contests, real crowds, real stakes, since he was fifteen; everyone else is a student playing at it. It is why he knows the shape of a contest — and why he loses, because Harry does not fight in one.

He is formidable: seventh year, Durmstrang-trained, chosen by the Goblet out of a whole school of them. He is beaten by a fourteen-year-old, outright and hard, and it only works because Harry is the genuine article. How, exactly: **Q-123.**

He is the first person Harry has to put down, and the only one in the maze who understood him.

## Where the World Cup answer pays *(draft)*

Not in the maze. A duel has no margin to salvage, so there is nothing for him to take inside a loss short of going kamikaze, which is not him — the mirror does not exist and should not be forced.

It pays in the graveyard, where Harry is bound, bled, wandless and stripped of the charge, and takes the one thing still takeable: he Summons the Cup and brings the body home. Cedric's echo calls it and Harry agrees. Planted in November, done in June, and nothing in the text joins them (DIV-089).
