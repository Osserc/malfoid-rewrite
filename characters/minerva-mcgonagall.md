# Minerva McGonagall

**Role:** Transfiguration professor and Deputy Headmistress. In the first war, the Ministry's special-operations asset.
**Canon baseline:** Strict, fair, formidable. In the Order of the Phoenix in the second war; canon never places her in the first one, and she is absent from the photograph of the original Order. Two years at the Ministry, then Hogwarts from 1956. No combat record at all before the Battle of Hogwarts. We are filling a gap, not overwriting a record.
**Changes from canon:** DIV-079, DIV-080, DIV-081.

## The war *(settled)* · DIV-079

She was not a spy. She led raids — a small-unit asset the Ministry pointed at Death Eater houses and gatherings, working with squads of Aurors. Her signature spell was Lustrum ([magic](../setting/magic.md)), and it is why the other side called her **Hellfire**.

- **The battle garb.** Riding boots, a faded tartan kilt belted and drawn over the shoulder with a luckenbooth brooch, a dragonhide jerkin, a plaited hairpiece with metal inserts, her hat with riding goggles fastened above the brim, and a small insignia: a sword and wand crossed, gold on dark red. All of it singed.
- **She has never taken it off** *(settled)*. Her everyday clothes *are* the garb, transfigured. The kilt is the tartan the castle has seen for thirty years; the brooch is a plain pin nobody looks at twice. So the B3 reveal is not her changing into anything — it is the disguise coming off as she walks, and the reason it is instant and perfect under fire is that she has done it twice a day for decades. It is also the cage made literal: every morning she puts the war away by turning it into a schoolmistress's robes.
  - **The ritual.** She renews it alone, before the castle is up. A woman re-locking a door, every day, for thirty years.
  - **Saying it out loud: Moody, in B4.** (It is the real man speaking, not Crouch — the beat belongs to Moody and must be written as his: DIV-086.) He was an Auror; he knows exactly what she was and respects her for it, which is why he is the one person in the castle who listens to her. He also knows why it had to be her and not one of his — he is far past the window himself, and would go up like a torch (DIV-080). He is therefore also the only one who can allude to the garb in company and get away with it — once, obliquely, and she does not answer. *(Wording open; the beat is the point.)*
- **The scars.** Lustrum always burns the caster, so her arms are marked and her garb is burnt through in places. She has never had the scars treated or the garb repaired. She calls it penance. Shape and cause below, under *Lustrum*.
- **The raid on Strix palace.** Late in the first war she led the attack that destroyed the Strix household. Basil Thynne, a half-blood social climber who had married into the family and attached himself to Voldemort for the standing, died in it. His wife Arvilla Strix and their infant son Balthier survived — which, under Lustrum's rule, was not luck but arithmetic: it found little in her, and she carries the marks of it, and it found nothing at all in a child who had never cast a thing. Balthier was three: he has an image and not a story — fire, a figure moving slowly above the ruins, a laugh — and his mother gave him the name to go with it. He is in her Transfiguration class for five years, he is good at it, and she knows exactly who he is. Neither of them has ever said a word. See DIV-082, [house-unity](../threads/house-unity.md).

## What she actually is *(settled)* · DIV-079

The twist under the legend: she is a battle junkie, and she hides it almost perfectly.

- **Her happy memories are her raids.** She casts her Patronus off them, and names each one *to herself* as she does — a place and a date, like a battle honour. *Ambleside, 4th April 1978.* *Dunfermline, 17th September 1980.* She never says them aloud. They are not a seeding device and never were: they are what the reader gets of her interior when we have her POV, which is rare. The seeding is *the remarks*, below ([patronus](../threads/patronus.md)).
- **The penance is not for the killing.** It is for having loved it. The charm works because the joy was real, and that is the whole horror: the happiest nights of her life were nights she burned people, and she has never once pretended otherwise to herself.
- **Discipline as a cage.** This is why she is the strictest teacher in the castle. The rules she enforces on everyone else are the ones she needs. When the cage opens in B3 she laughs — not cruelly, and not maliciously; joyously, with a tinge of mania — and the whole castle hears it.
- **Afterwards she denies it flatly.** She refuses to acknowledge the night at all and threatens detention to anyone who raises "this outlandish notion", which only feeds the legend.

## The remarks *(settled)* · DIV-079

This is how the war gets onto the page, and it runs from B1 to B7. She drops casual, obscure, faintly unhinged asides into ordinary teaching and ordinary discipline: a place, an "incident", something that plainly happened and was plainly not a school matter. The author's two examples set the register — a botched Transfiguration compared to something *we did in Waldridge*, and a threat that one more broken rule will make *the Olnareff incident* look tame.

- **They are funny first.** That is the whole mechanism. They land as teacher-sarcasm and get repeated in the common room as a good line. Ominous remarks invite questions; jokes get laughed at and filed away. The horror is cumulative and arrives on a reread.
- **Nobody ever asks.** Not a rule she enforces — there is no rule. People simply don't, and nobody can quite say why they don't.
- **Two registers, and the reader can sort them without being told.** British place names are the war (1970–81): raids on houses and gatherings. Foreign names and "incidents" are the Ministry work that came *before* it, which is the quiet implication that she was on their books for years before Voldemort gave them a reason.
- **The payoff is the collision.** A handful of the names she has thrown away as jokes come back later as battle honours, in her own head, with a date attached. The reader who has been collecting the funny ones loses the floor.

## Lustrum

The spell's rules live in [magic](../setting/magic.md). What belongs to her:

- **The urn.** She has kept the ashes since the war — a scoop from each burn, taken indiscriminately, Dementor and human together, in a jar in her office that students have looked at for twelve years without ever asking. What she tells herself about it is *if they burnt, they deserved it*, and under the actual mechanism that is false: the fire never judged anybody, it only counted spells. She is the one person alive with the evidence to know that, and she has never once worked it out.
- **The enhancer is the first she has ever had.** She fought the entire war without one, which is why her record is houses — a raw casting kills a handful at a time — and why she is covered in marks. In August 1993 she hands Flitwick the urn, all of it, and he distils and concentrates it down into a single vial on a chain. There will never be another unless she burns more people.
  - **She has it from the start of term**, before anything has happened. She is a declared proponent of exterminating the Dementors and a garrison has just been posted on her lawn; she would not wait for it to go badly before arming herself. Nobody sees Flitwick give it to her. The castle only sees that Professor McGonagall has started wearing a necklace.
  - **It is a new temptation, and that is the point.** Twelve years of wanting the thing and never having it, and now it is round her neck, in a school. In lulls — a class scratching away at an essay — she takes it out and holds it in her hand, absently, and puts it back.
  - **She gives it back at Hogsmeade** (the Three Broomsticks, December). She tells Flitwick to hold on to it for her because she does not trust herself with it, and elaborates not at all. She has been not trusting herself with it since 1981; what is new is that the thing finally exists.
  - **He gives it back at the siege.** That is what his line is: not cover, delivery. The one man in the castle who could not fail to understand what he had been handed in December, or what he was handing back in June.
  - **She spends it that night.** The whole war, used up in one casting.
  - **And she never makes another.** The grounds are deep in the material for weeks afterwards and she walks past it daily and lets it go, having now seen what she is capable of with one. Against twelve years of keeping the urn, it is the one time she chose not to arm herself (Q-113).
- **The scars are handprints** ([magic](../setting/magic.md)): a grip across the torso, smaller ones at the wrists and forearms. The dragonhide jerkin is why her torso came through and her arms did not. She has never had them treated and calls it penance; the school, which knows nothing, reads them as the mark of a woman who has walked through a great many fires.
- **Why she can cast it at all, and why she is the one who could.** Lustrum is castable only from a narrow window: too clean and it will not light, too dirty and it kills you ([magic](../setting/magic.md)). She has worked enough Dark magic to catch and taken little enough damage to survive it — which is also why the Ministry's asset was a schoolmistress and not an Auror, the Aurors being authorised for Unforgivables in that war and carrying a career of curse damage besides. Every casting tells her the same thing about herself, to scale, and none of it washes off.
- **And it is a clock.** Each casting deposits more and walks her towards the far edge of the window. One day she will be too Dark to cast the thing she is, and nothing will tell her but the backlash growing. She has no way to measure it and no one to ask.
  - **The vicewick slows the clock,** since the fire takes the wick's fuel before hers. It spares her more than pain, which is one more reason the necklace is a temptation and not a convenience.
- **Extermination.** She is a loud and unpopular proponent of destroying the Dementors outright, and has been since the war. The Ministry keeps them because they are useful ([azkaban](../problems/azkaban.md)).
- **At the Quidditch pitch.** Two months into carrying the enhancer when the Dementors swarm the match, which means she can end the garrison, all of it, from where she stands — and it may well be the same things she hunted in 1981, taken back into service (DIV-083), so this is her own war standing twenty feet away. What stops her is that the fire would take the stadium with it. Not one child in the air is clean, because no student above first year is, and they would be scorched off their brooms from fifty feet. Behind her sit a thousand people, some of whom have done far worse than she could guess and some of whom have had far worse done to them, and they would go up in front of the school. She stays her hand, at a cost nobody in the stadium is in a position to measure, and leaves scorch marks on the railing in a downpour. Lee Jordan, sitting beside her, is the only one who sees, and has the sense to say nothing.
  - **The part she never learns.** Harry is in the air, carrying a Horcrux. He would have gone up like a Death Eater and she would never have known why (Q-111).
- **Why she cannot simply solve things with it.** Lustrum is an offensive siege weapon: you drop it on a position you do not occupy. At Hogwarts in B7 she is standing inside the target, with her own people in the burn — hands dirty, and the wounded among them the most flammable things on the field. That, not any weakness in the spell, is why there is almost nowhere on that ground she can put it down. She still casts, precisely and small, and raw — she has no vicewick by then and chose not to make a second one (Q-113). DIV-023.
- **Why she laughs at the siege.** It is the one night of her life when the thing she loves doing is also, unambiguously, the right thing to do — the enemy outside the walls, an empty lawn, and nobody of hers inside the burn. She has never had it clean before and never will again.

## Voice

Clipped, exact, Scottish. Under fire she gets shorter and politer, and the politeness is the warning sign. Her taunts are courteous ("Would that you had been in the field") and land harder for it.

**The Macnair line has a second floor** *(settled)* · DIV-084. She tells him she wishes he had been out there with the Dementors she has just burned. It reads as a schoolmistress being vicious. It is a reference: **he was**. He led that horde for Voldemort, and the two of them know it, and he walked into Ministry service for it while she was told to stop asking. That is why he is afraid of her, and it is the engine under her objection to the garrison — she did not merely lose the extermination argument, she watched the man who aimed them at her get a desk.
