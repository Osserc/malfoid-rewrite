# Minerva McGonagall

**Role:** Transfiguration professor and Deputy Headmistress. In the first war, the Ministry's special-operations asset.
**Canon baseline:** Strict, fair, formidable. In the Order of the Phoenix in the second war; canon never places her in the first one, and she is absent from the photograph of the original Order. Two years at the Ministry, then Hogwarts from 1956. No combat record at all before the Battle of Hogwarts. We are filling a gap, not overwriting a record.
**Changes from canon:** DIV-079, DIV-080, DIV-081.

## The war *(settled)* · DIV-079

She was not a spy. She led raids — a small-unit asset the Ministry pointed at Death Eater houses and gatherings, working with squads of Aurors. Her signature spell was Lustrum ([magic](../setting/magic.md)), and it is why the other side called her **Hellfire**.

- **The battle garb.** Riding boots, a faded tartan kilt belted and drawn over the shoulder with a luckenbooth brooch, a dragonhide jerkin, a plaited hairpiece with metal inserts, her hat with riding goggles fastened above the brim, and a small insignia: a sword and wand crossed, gold on dark red. All of it singed.
- **She has never taken it off** *(settled)*. Her everyday clothes *are* the garb, transfigured. The kilt is the tartan the castle has seen for thirty years; the brooch is a plain pin nobody looks at twice. So the B3 reveal is not her changing into anything — it is the disguise coming off as she walks, and the reason it is instant and perfect under fire is that she has done it twice a day for decades. It is also the cage made literal: every morning she puts the war away by turning it into a schoolmistress's robes.
  - **The ritual.** She renews it alone, before the castle is up. A woman re-locking a door, every day, for thirty years.
  - **Saying it out loud: Moody, in B4.** He was an Auror; he knows exactly what she was and respects her for it, which is why he is the one person in the castle who listens to her. He is therefore also the only one who can allude to the garb in company and get away with it — once, obliquely, and she does not answer. *(Wording open; the beat is the point.)*
- **The scars.** Lustrum always burns the caster's own account, so her arms are marked and her garb is burnt through in places. She has never had the scars treated or the garb repaired. She calls it penance; the school, which knows nothing, reads it as the mark of a woman who walked through a great many fires.
- **The raid on Strix palace.** Late in the first war she led the attack that destroyed the Strix household. Basil Thynne, a half-blood social climber who had married into the family and attached himself to Voldemort for the standing, died in it. His wife Arvilla Strix and their infant son Balthier survived — which, under Lustrum's rule, was not luck: the fire weighed them and let them go. Balthier was three: he has an image and not a story — fire, a figure moving slowly above the ruins, a laugh — and his mother gave him the name to go with it. He is in her Transfiguration class for five years, he is good at it, and she knows exactly who he is. Neither of them has ever said a word. See DIV-082, [house-unity](../threads/house-unity.md).

## What she actually is *(settled)* · DIV-079

The twist under the legend: she is a battle junkie, and she hides it almost perfectly.

- **Her happy memories are her raids.** She casts her Patronus off them, and names each one *to herself* as she does — a place and a date, like a battle honour. *Ambleside, 4th April 1978.* *Dunfermline, 17th September 1980.* She never says them aloud. They are not a seeding device and never were: they are what the reader gets of her interior when we have her POV, which is rare. The seeding is *the remarks*, below ([patronus](../threads/patronus.md)).
- **The penance is not for the killing.** It is for having loved it. The charm works because the joy was real, and that is the whole horror: the happiest nights of her life were nights she burned people, and she has never once pretended otherwise to herself.
- **Discipline as a cage.** This is why she is the strictest teacher in the castle. The rules she enforces on everyone else are the ones she needs. When the cage opens in B3 she laughs — not cruelly, and not maliciously; joyously, with a tinge of mania — and the whole castle hears it.
- **Afterwards she denies it flatly.** She refuses to acknowledge the night at all and threatens detention to anyone who raises "this outlandish notion", which only feeds the legend.

## The remarks *(settled)* · DIV-079

This is how the war gets onto the page, and it runs from B1 to B7. She drops casual, obscure, faintly unhinged asides into ordinary teaching and ordinary discipline: a place, an "incident", something that plainly happened and was plainly not a school matter. The author's two examples set the register — a botched Transfiguration compared to something *we did in Waldridge*, and a threat that one more broken rule will make *the Olnareff incident* look tame.

- **They are funny first.** That is the whole mechanism. They land as teacher-sarcasm and get repeated in the common room as a good line. Ominous remarks invite questions; jokes get laughed at and filed away. The horror is cumulative and arrives on a reread.
- **Nobody ever asks.** Not a rule she enforces — there is no rule. People simply don't, and nobody can quite say why they don't.
- **Two registers, and the reader can sort them without being told.** British place names are the war (1970–81): raids on houses and gatherings. Foreign names and "incidents" are the Ministry work that came *before* it, which is the quiet implication that she was on their books for years before Voldemort gave them a reason.
- **The payoff is the collision.** A handful of the names she has thrown away as jokes come back later as battle honours, in her own head, with a date attached. The reader who has been collecting the funny ones loses the floor.

## Lustrum

The spell's rules live in [magic](../setting/magic.md). What belongs to her:

- **The enhancer: commissioned, carried, surrendered, returned.** A charmed trinket, custom and extremely expensive, which turns one casting into the small sun over the castle.
  - **She has it from the start of term**, before anything has happened. She is a declared proponent of exterminating the Dementors and a garrison has just been posted on her lawn; she would not wait for it to go badly before arming herself. Nobody sees Flitwick give it to her. The castle only sees that Professor McGonagall has started wearing a necklace.
  - **Two months of carrying it.** In lulls — a class scratching away at an essay — she takes it out and holds it in her hand, absently, and puts it back.
  - **She gives it back at Hogsmeade** (the Three Broomsticks, December). She tells Flitwick to hold on to it for her because she does not trust herself with it, and elaborates not at all.
  - **He gives it back at the siege.** That is what his line is: not cover, delivery. The one man in the castle who could not fail to understand what he had been handed in December, or what he was handing back in June.
- **Extermination.** She is a loud and unpopular proponent of destroying the Dementors outright, and has been since the war. The Ministry keeps them because they are useful ([azkaban](../problems/azkaban.md)).
- **At the Quidditch pitch.** She is two months into carrying the enhancer when the Dementors swarm the match, which means she can end the garrison, all of it, from where she is standing. And take fourteen children out of the air and the stands with them, because the fire weighs everything inside it. She stays her hand, at a cost to her that nobody in the stadium is in a position to measure. She leaves scorch marks on the railing in a downpour; Lee Jordan, sitting beside her, is the only one who sees, and has the sense to say nothing.
- **Why she cannot simply solve things with it.** Lustrum is a siege weapon, not a duelling spell: slow, wide, telegraphed, and it does not pick sides. Everything inside the burn is weighed, her own people included, and not all of them would come out of it well. Add the price it takes out of her on every casting and she gets perhaps one real use a night. That, not any weakness of the spell, is the constraint — in the first war it is why her record is raids on houses rather than duels, and in B7 it is why there is almost nowhere on that ground she can put it down. She still casts, precisely and small. DIV-023, Q-103.

## Voice

Clipped, exact, Scottish. Under fire she gets shorter and politer, and the politeness is the warning sign. Her taunts are courteous ("Would that you had been in the field") and land harder for it.
