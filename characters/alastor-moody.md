# Alastor "Mad-Eye" Moody

**Role:** Defence Against the Dark Arts, 1994–95. Harry's best teacher, and the instrument that delivers him to the graveyard.
**Canon baseline:** retired Auror. In canon he spends the whole of B4 starved in his own trunk while Barty Crouch Jr wears his face, and arrives as a character only in the last chapter.
**Changes from canon:** DIV-086, DIV-095, DIV-096, DIV-097, DIV-098, DIV-099, DIV-100, DIV-101, DIV-102, DIV-103, DIV-105, DIV-106, DIV-108.

## The year

*(settled)* · DIV-086. **It is him.** Every lesson, every drill, every piece of coaching Harry gets this year is the real Alastor Moody's, given sincerely. He is under the Imperius Curse from late August to the night of the Third Task, and Crouch Jr is in the trunk.

- **He is not hollowed out.** The curse does not run him like a puppet; it supplies compliance and, with it, contentment. He is recognisably, entirely himself — paranoid, foul-tempered, brilliant, and a little mad already, which is the whole reason nobody looks twice.
- **There is no lost time.** He remembers everything he did, including the things Crouch did wearing him, and remembers having had good reasons at the time. That is the Imperius working as canon describes it, and it is what makes the end unbearable: he remembers *wanting to*.
- **He fights it, all year.** He surfaces, and Crouch is always a few feet away to put him back under. The grip is maintained, not cast once — which is why Crouch has to be in the castle at all (DIV-086).
- **The teaching is a ladder, not a lecture.** Four rungs across the year, each one handing over more than the last (DIV-102).
- **He drills the class on resisting the Imperius Curse, repeatedly**, and Harry is the only one who throws it off. **This is the doctrine and not a mystery** — he puts the curse on students because you cannot learn to beat a thing you have never felt, and he would do it whether or not he were under one himself (DIV-105). **The irony belongs to the reader; it is not a trail he is leaving** (Q-114).

## The doctrine *(settled)* · DIV-105

**Aggression, as a professional conclusion rather than a temperament.** Counter-curses are well and good, and by the time you need one you are already losing: a step behind, reacting, depending on the other man to have made a mistake. **So you go first and you go hard.** The enemy will not be kind and will not wait for you to finish your shield.

- **His body is the evidence and he teaches from it.** The leg, the nose, the eye — all of it bought defending, by a younger man who thought that was the job. He changed policy afterwards and **he buried fewer of his own.** That is a casualty record, not a feeling, and it is what makes *CONSTANT VIGILANCE* a syllabus rather than a catchphrase: always ready, always prepared, always first. **Showing a room of fourteen-year-olds his injuries as a teaching aid is entirely in character and entirely indefensible.**
- **He is right, and the book never refutes him.** The cost is not that the doctrine is wrong. It is that he is handing it to a child, and a man who has reduced himself to a function cannot see why that would matter (DIV-096, DIV-008).
- **So he casts the Imperius on students**, because you cannot learn to beat a thing you have never felt. No further explanation is needed and none is offered.
- **And the doctrine has a limit, drawn with a professional's reasoning rather than a decent man's.** He would never put the Cruciatus on a student — not because it is Dark, since he does not grade spells that way, but because it cannot be resisted and cannot be trained against, so there is nothing in it but hurting a child. **He demonstrates all three on spiders and casts exactly one of them on people.**
- **He is finite, and he has done the arithmetic.** Not spent — he is as dangerous as he has ever been, and the reader sees it three times (DIV-086, DIV-099, DIV-103) — but old, wounded, not improving, and one man. He is going to die in this war.
- **Which makes the plan succession.** The four rungs of the year are a handover, not a mentorship (DIV-102), and the classroom is the sane version of the same project: a hundred children a year for as long as he lasts (DIV-106).
- **His whole self-valuation is the count.** What he is worth is how many Dark wizards he has taken off the board, and the only way that number rises after he is gone is through somebody else. **It is also the ledger that the one good thing he does for Harry never appears on.**

### Why it is Harry *(settled)* · DIV-105

Three reasons, mundane on purpose. **No prophecy and no Chosen One:** he does not need one and is never given one.

- **The debt.** He put the boy's name in the Goblet and remembers having had a reason at the time (DIV-086). He cannot explain it, cannot undo it, and keeps nearly saying it and being stopped — so he does the one thing left and makes the boy survivable. **The teaching is restitution**, and nobody is ever told so.
- **The dead.** He sees the Potters in him, and he is an inventory of people he outlived. Same register as the Order photograph a year later, which is why that scene reads as continuous with this one (DIV-098).
  - **There are two of them in this castle.** Frank and Alice Longbottom were Aurors and were in the Order, and he knows exactly what was done to them, so Neville is on the same list. **He is not a candidate in 1994** — the boy who melts cauldrons is four years short of the one who holds the line (DIV-025) — and Moody does not pick on potential. **Neville gets the kindness and Harry gets the handover**: the gentleness after the Unforgivables lesson is the real man's and is entirely sincere, and it is as far as it goes. He dies without seeing what Neville turns into.
- **The evidence.** The boy throws off the Imperius in his classroom when nobody else in the room can, then walks off a dragon in one piece and well. **The First Task is the trigger**, which is why the handover starts that evening and not in September (DIV-097, DIV-102). The rest is file: three years of it, plus a castle stormed by Dementors (DIV-081).
- **And he is receptive**, which is the saddest part of the arrangement. Nobody has ever treated him as a colleague, so when somebody finally does he takes it with both hands.
- **The bitter part.** The thing that decides him is the boy throwing off the curse that is riding Moody as he watches (Q-114).

## The lessons *(settled)* · DIV-096

He makes the boy formidable, sincerely, and the damage is in the framing rather than in any spell. **He hands over a doctrine, not a syllabus** — *spells are weapons, they are not good or bad* — and in a world where Dark magic runs on intent that is enough to stain him for life (DIV-084). He also tells Harry he would make a good Auror, and means it, which a hunted fourteen-year-old wears like a borrowed jacket for a year.

- **He teaches him nothing to be ashamed of and gives him permission for everything.** Harry has improvised past every problem since he was eleven; what the year removes is the apology.
- **The Conjunctivitis Curse is not even his.** The chain runs Krum, then Sirius approving it, then Harry deciding to learn it. Moody's contribution is the climate (DIV-096).
- **He says the doctrine out loud in the spring**, when he walks in on the Conjunctivitis practice and approves instead of reprimanding: *spells are weapons, Potter. Hit them first, hit them hard, and you won't be an easy target when you go down.* **Not *if*.** The gallows humour is how he talks; the fatalism is the part Harry absorbs without noticing (DIV-102).
- **He does it in four rungs across the year, not in one scene** *(DIV-102)*: the drink after the First Task; the flask thrown back at the lake in February; the stamp on the Conjunctivitis Curse in the spring, which is where the doctrine lands; and nothing at all before the maze, so that whatever Harry does in there is his. **He calls him *junior Auror* throughout**, as an address rather than a joke.
- **The whisky is the same doctrine with no spell in it** — a colleague's drink for a boy back off a job, offered as procedure and not as mischief, and the only regard of that kind anyone in the castle offers him. **He also teaches the boy that the rule has exceptions, and that he is one of them** (DIV-097).

- **And none of it is Crouch's doing**, which is the cruelty of it. The lessons are the real man's and they are good lessons, and they are also the thing Harry reaches for in a bathroom in B6 (DIV-043).

## Interposition *(settled)* · DIV-108

**His biggest contribution, and he never knows it.** Shields do not stop the Unforgivables; mass does. It is an advanced technique rather than a trick — real Transfiguration, at speed, under fire, in the right place (DIV-107, DIV-108).

**How he gets there:** he watches Flitwick's Duelling Club and does not recognise it as fighting. Tame, decorous, conducted standing still. So he takes the theory into his own classroom.

- **Posture before technique.** *You do not stand in the open and you do not stand still.* Move, and keep something between you and the other man.
- **Shields and counters leave you on the back foot**, and a counter-curse requires you to already know the answer. A block works every time, including against the things that have no answer.
- **He concedes that both have their place**, which is what keeps him credible: bouncing something back is sometimes right, and countering keeps your own offence up. **What he objects to is the instinct** — meeting a Dark wizard head-on, matching him spell for spell, against a repertoire you have never seen, when being wrong once is permanent.
- **He points at a wound to make the point.** *This is what I got for trying to counter the —.* The body is the syllabus again (DIV-105), and this time it is an argument rather than a warning.
- **The demonstration is asked for, not volunteered.** Somebody wants to know what a man who cannot dodge is supposed to do about any of it, so he shows them: he floats a desk, turns it to stone in the air, and puts it where the spell was going. **The leg is the reason the technique exists and he makes no speech about it.** Transparent cover is the refinement, because he will not give up seeing what the other man is doing — whether that is in this lesson or later is Q-144.
- **A student finds the hole in it.** Fire that snakes around cover, and it does: trap it with geometry, which is a very high ceiling and which he demonstrates, or move and accept being singed. **Not Harry**, who takes to the technique rather than arguing with it (Q-144).
- **Mobility is the other half and is not designed** (Q-149). He cannot simply be a walking fortress, and flashy magical movement would demote brooms and turn duels into spectacle.
- **He teaches it to the whole class.** **Not a fifth rung, and DIV-102 is untouched** — the four private handovers stand and nothing precedes the maze. This is the classroom version of the succession (DIV-106), so the thing of his that outlives him is the one he gave away to everybody, and Harry receives it as one of a hundred. **It appears on his own ledger nowhere** (DIV-105).
- **Harry finishes Krum with it** (Q-123): the eye that reads the draw is the Duelling Club's, the ground is his. From B5 the DA has it, and at Hogwarts in B7 the castle is ammunition rather than scenery.
- **And it is what kills him.** In the air over Surrey there is no mass to raise and nothing to get behind (DIV-103).


## The Killing Curse lesson *(setled)* · DIV-128

Canon's most famous lesson is shocking and mechanically inert. Ours makes it causal, and the cost falls on the man wearing him.

- **The demonstration is where the reader learns what the curse is** (Q-161). He is the right teacher for it and the right teacher for the answer to it, since the cover logic is already his (DIV-108) — so the one man who explains Avada Kedavra to a classroom is a man being worn by somebody else while he does it.
- **And it loosens Crouch's grip.** The curse throws the caster's entire magic out of the body and requires it back (DIV-107); a rider is riding a road through that body (DIV-086). When everything leaves, Crouch either goes with it, which is very hard, or holds a body with nothing in it for the duration. **Either way the hold slips** — precisely the circumstance DIV-086 says lets a strong will surface.
- **One of the thousand cuts, not a dramatic failure.** It gives the degradation canon merely asserts a named mechanism, and it compounds with everything else he is spending that year.
- **The irony is the whole point:** part of what helps Alastor Moody throw a man out of his own body is an Unforgivable, cast with his own hand, on a spider, in front of children. **Nobody in the room understands it, and neither does Crouch.**

## What gives him away *(settled)* · Q-114

The tells are not villainy. **They are a frightened young man coming up for air inside a grizzled Auror**, which is the throughline: Barty Crouch Jr went to Azkaban very young and never finished growing up in there. Leading candidates:

- **He takes a stranger's drink without checking it.** A smell from Crouch's schooldays gets him at Hogsmeade and he takes Moody over to have the thing himself: a mug of Butterbeer, which passes as nostalgia in a hard old man and is worse once you know. **The tell is the missing check, not the drink.** His guard has an off-switch marked *among allies* and the books show it, so drinking what he did not pour would be covered — but a public house is not that room, and he has personally taught Harry to verify before swallowing anything (DIV-097). **Harry watches it happen and files it under *even the best of them slip sometimes***.
- **The flask is innocent, and it is spent proving it.** After the First Task he pours Harry a measure of Firewhisky, every reader of canon has a very bad second, nothing whatever happens — and then *CONSTANT VIGILANCE*, and the lecture that plants the word *check*. The offer is sincere and the test is real and they are the same act (DIV-097, [the-flask](../scenes/b4/the-flask.md)).
- **He startles at the name Winky.** Days after the Yule Ball he asks after Dobby — in character, since a paranoid Auror wants to know why the Malfoy elf is handing Harry Potter socks — and Harry answers that Dobby is in the castle minding another elf. Moody has no reason at all to know the name. Crouch has every reason: she was his family's elf.
- **He keeps nearly saying who did it.** He remembers putting the name in the Goblet and remembers thinking he had a reason, so he would say so if it came up — and all year he opens his mouth about the culprit and then shrugs it off. That is Crouch silencing him for a split second at a time, spending grip he cannot spare (DIV-086). The confession names it in one line.
- And the ones already on the table: the seventh lock, and the eye that can see into his own trunk and looks away. **The Imperius drills are not among them** — they are simply what the man teaches (DIV-105).
- **The ferret is not a tell at all, and that is worse.** Harry files the cruelty under *that is how Moody does things* and goes on to love the man — so the one act of the year he has to excuse is the only one that was not Moody's (DIV-095).

## He takes himself back

*(settled)* · DIV-086. In Dumbledore's office, with Harry in front of him, Crouch rides him too hot — appetite is the one thing Crouch has never been able to govern — and the grip slackens. Moody uses that instant and throws him out.

It is the hand on the wand wavering, not a speech. He is the reason Harry is alive at the end of the night, and the first thing he is told afterwards is what he spent the year doing.

**It prefigures what he is in the Order.** He loses to the Death Eaters twice in canon and is mostly a cautionary tale; here the reader has watched him break a hold that no one else in Britain could have broken, and B5 gets to show what he can actually do.

## He takes himself out

*(settled)* · DIV-106. **He meant to stay** — not a year's favour to an old friend, but the form his succession could take (DIV-105) — and at the end of term he resigns, and nobody makes him.

- **The shame is professional**, and it is the same shame as the apology (DIV-100). A Death Eater lived inside him in this castle for ten months; he taught a hundred children *constant vigilance* with a hand on his throat; a champion is dead. **He knows it could be done, because he did it in the end.** A man carrying that does not stand in front of that class again.
- **Dumbledore asks him to stay and he refuses.** The castle has a real Defence teacher for the first time in years and loses him to his own conscience. **The jinx's instrument is the man himself**, and the Ministry walks into a vacancy nobody had to engineer.
- **It is what makes B5 inevitable.** He gives up the sane version of passing the torch and keeps the insane one: the single boy. **The fixation is what is left when the classroom goes** (DIV-099).

- **He is perfectly well.** Canon's Moody comes out of the trunk half-starved; ours walked the castle all year and nothing was ever done to his body. **No hospital wing and no convalescence** — he is on his feet and clearing a classroom within days of the Third Task (DIV-086).

### The last drink *(settled)* · DIV-106

**Harry finds him packing**, in the Defence classroom and not the office: the dark detectors coming off the walls, the room being handed back.

- **He tells him he is going, and why.** No evasion and no softening — a Death Eater lived inside him here for ten months, he taught a hundred children *constant vigilance* with a hand on his throat, and a boy is dead.
- **Harry does not protest, because the man is right and he knows it.** That is the scene's shape, and it is what keeps it from being Lupin's goodbye a year on ([the-flask](../scenes/b4/the-flask.md)).
- **He does not offer the Imperius excuse here**, although he will in August (DIV-100). **He has not had time**: days after the graveyard, with no month alone to turn it over and no sight yet of the man doing the Order's work. In June he agrees with an adult who sounds certain; by Privet Drive he has built a position, and it turns out to be Lucius Malfoy's.
- **Then the flask.** *I never gave you a prize for winning the tournament.* **Harry's eyes never leave him** while he casts, finds nothing, drinks, and hands it straight back.
- **"You made it this time, Potter. Full marks."** **And Harry hears the subtext:** *I have nothing left to teach you.* Not praise — a discharge, and the loneliest thing anybody says to him all year.
- **Harry keeps nothing.** The flask leaves with him and is never recovered, like everything else of his (DIV-103) — the eye stays the only object of his that anybody carries.
- **It closes the object's year:** poured for him in November, thrown to him in February, offered outright in June (DIV-097).

## Afterwards

He drilled the boy and delivered him, and Cedric Diggory is dead. He knows the help was real and that the plan needed exactly that help, which is a thing with no answer in it. How much of this is on the page in B5 is open (Q-140); the guilt is not negotiable.

### What he does with it *(settled)* · DIV-099

**He doubles down.** *I made a soldier of him, and it is the one thing I did right last year* — everything else he did in 1994–95 ends at a headstone, so he holds that piece up hard, and it puts him on Sirius Black's side of the Order's argument about how much Harry is told.

- **It follows from B4 and cannot be avoided.** A man who ran a debrief and poured the boy a drink for a job well done cannot argue six months later that he is a child who should be told nothing.
- **He has the file to argue from:** a dragon, a lake, a maze stocked to kill, three charged champions, Voldemort, and a castle stormed by Dementors two years before that (DIV-081). It is a better record than most of his old colleagues ever had.
- **And they lose**, with Lupin as the voice of reason who settles nothing (Q-136). Harry stays in the dark, and the only thing the axis buys him is the knowledge that competent adults said otherwise and were overruled.
- **He is not looking at Harry either.** Sirius sees James; he sees a soldier.
- **The bill arrives at the Ministry, and he is standing in it.** Canon already puts him in the rescue party, where Dolohov drops him in one move; **ours is a terror there**, which is where the promise above gets paid — **and it changes nothing.** Sirius dies a few feet away. *I called a teenager a fellow veteran to keep my own ego intact, and it has killed his godfather.* **He does not keep his cool about it** (DIV-099, Q-140).
- **In public, first, over a drink:** at the prefect party he mentions the Firewhisky in front of Molly Weasley, entirely fondly, and the room divides along the line it will keep all year (DIV-098, [the-prefect-party](../scenes/b5/the-prefect-party.md)).

### The apology *(settled)* · DIV-100

**"I failed you both."** Not the Imperius Curse and not penitence about having enjoyed it: **his shame is professional.** He was taken at his own house before term began and he should have fought it off, and a schoolgirl went down a corridor as a ferret because he did not — **and he knows it could be done, because he did it in the end**, which turns every month he did not into a failure he can measure.

**He says it the night they bring Harry out of Privet Drive** — the old routine restored on the way, a security question that proves nothing because nobody ever impersonated him, then the flight, and a hand on the shoulder at Grimmauld as the boy is sent upstairs. **And he is downstairs vouching for him inside the hour**, which is the whole character: he is not torn, he thinks both things are true, and he is right about both. It is a sentence, not a speech. Harry offers him the excuse — *you were under the Imperius* — which is word for word Lucius Malfoy's defence, handed to him by the Malfoy girl's boy, and **he declines it** (Q-117). Then he leaves; he is not asking to be forgiven and does not wait to find out whether he is. He asks Harry to pass it to her rather than going himself; when Harry does is Q-139. **It lands on a girl who has spent a year hating him for an act he did not commit** (DIV-104).

### The last stand *(settled)* · DIV-103

**The Seven Potters.** Mundungus bolts and he takes it in stride — he brought the man knowing exactly what he was — then turns and fights, and **he is a terror**: the only time in seven books the reader sees him work with nothing on him. When Harry's Expelliarmus gives him away, **he makes Voldemort deal with him**, and that window is how Harry and Hagrid get out. Voldemort kills him, and he has done precisely what he said he would at the briefing: *I'll get you to the destination, Potter, if it's the last thing I do.*

**It is the correction, applied once.** The man who spent a year calling Harry a junior Auror dies getting him carried out like a boy. His eye is never recovered; Harry takes it off Umbridge's door and carries it until he can bury it properly ([the-seven-potters](../scenes/b7/the-seven-potters.md)).

## Relationships

- [Harry Potter](harry-potter.md): his pupil, honestly. What he taught Harry works, and the boy can never sort which parts were Crouch's. What he actually handed over was permission (DIV-096) and, from the front of a classroom without meaning it for him in particular, interposition (DIV-108) — and, after the First Task, the belief that he was safe with him (DIV-097). By B5 he is one of the two men arguing that the boy is a grown man (DIV-099).
- Sirius Black: not friends, and on the same side of everything that matters in B5. They reach the same verdict about the Malfoy girl independently, in opposite registers (DIV-101), and they lose the same argument together (DIV-099).
- [Dracaena Malfoy](dracaena-malfoy.md): he transfigured her and thought it just, he owes her an apology he will not deliver in person, and his professional read of her is that she will be the death of Harry or his greatest asset (DIV-100, DIV-101).
- [Minerva McGonagall](minerva-mcgonagall.md): he knows what she was in the first war and respects her for it, and he is the one person who can allude to it in company. He is himself when he says it — the beat belongs to the real man, not to Crouch.
- Albus Dumbledore: an old friend who tested him on arrival with something only the two of them knew, and got it right, because he is Moody. **He asks him to stay at the end of the year and is refused** (DIV-106).
- Barty Crouch Jr: asleep in his trunk, holding him down, for ten months.
