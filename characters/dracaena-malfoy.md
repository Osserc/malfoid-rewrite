# Dracaena Malfoy

**Role:** Harry's rival and his tsundere love interest. 🔒 DIV-001
**Canon baseline:** Draco Malfoy. Anything not overridden follows canon Draco, adjusted for the knock-on effects of her being a daughter (Q-001).
**Changes from canon:** DIV-001, DIV-003–006, DIV-009–018, DIV-020–023, DIV-027–032, DIV-038–041, DIV-043, DIV-045–047, DIV-049, DIV-052, DIV-054, DIV-058–061, DIV-064, DIV-065, DIV-069–071, DIV-095, DIV-109

## Core

- **Want:** *(settled)* **one beautiful thing to call her own.** Everything she has was handed to her, and she wants to be rid of none of it — she loves being a Malfoy, the line, the nobility, the unbroken descent of great people. The question underneath is **what she is worth when none of it is counted**: if she is defined by what she inherited, what is left of her when it's stripped away? That is the script she wants to live outside of (DIV-013), and the first thing that ever arrives from outside it is an unassuming boy in a tailor's shop (DIV-009). It is why her B3 Boggart is him ([remedial-boggart](../scenes/b3/remedial-boggart.md), DIV-017).
- **Need:** _open_
- **Wound / fear:** that the dream was never real (DIV-015).
- **Lie she believes:** _open_

## Gifts

Gifted and brave, as befits wizard royalty (DIV-011):

- An excellent potioneer, with a particular interest in poisons. The best in her year and possibly in the castle, ahead of Hermione (DIV-011). See *Potions* below.
- Cunning.
- A voracious reader on whatever grips her: poisons, the strange boy's stories, then Muggle novels (DIV-010).
- Competent, and no coward.

## Heiress and tomboy

*(settled)* A tomboy in spirit, at least: Quidditch, flying, duelling, dragons. But she's still a refined heiress, and the two have to live together in her. Like canon Draco she's spoiled and almost always gets her way, but here it comes off as cute rather than pathetic, all the more because she comes into her powers quickly (DIV-011). Lucius would rather shelter her, and rarely wins ([lucius-malfoy](lucius-malfoy.md)).

## Drilled manners *(settled)*

She can work any room she chooses. It was trained into her from the cradle and it is a weapon she knows she has: the curtsy, the compliment aimed at the one vanity the mark actually has, the rival held politely in reserve. In B1 she takes the Dessendre canvas's location off Sir Nicholas inside a minute by wondering aloud whether she ought to trouble the Bloody Baron instead, and tells Harry afterwards that magic is not the only weapon in the arsenal of a witch (DIV-073).

**The point is the contrast.** The same girl cannot say one civil sentence to the boy she wants. Everything she has works on ghosts, adults, strangers and her own house, and none of it works where it matters, because none of it was built for wanting something. It pays off at the Yule Ball, where the trained version and the real thing arrive together (DIV-004).

## Taste · DIV-070

*(settled)* Her taste is her moral channel. Things are wrong to her because they are **ugly** — squalid, beneath her, unbecoming of what she believes she is — and that one principle drives most of what she does and says.

- **What it covers.** Slytherin's fouling disgusts her because it's sordid, not because it's unfair ([the-quidditch-final](../scenes/b3/the-quidditch-final.md)). Buckbeak's execution offends her because butchering a beast over a grudge does not befit nobility ([buckbeak-rescue](../scenes/b3/buckbeak-rescue.md)). Under real anger her insult is *inbred*, and *uncouth* is what she calls the move that beats her.
- **It's an exit from the ideology that isn't a conversion.** She never decides blood purity is wrong. She accepts the premise and catches her own class failing it: the lines have thinned and the specimens at her own table are not impressive (DIV-008, DIV-035). Crabbe and Goyle are her standing private evidence — kept because they're useful and expected, and privately held in contempt.
- **The same move as Muggle Studies.** Cold evaluation on merit rather than loyalty (DIV-012), turned on her own side instead of the other one. Two doors out, both running on her snobbery rather than against it.
- **It is also her ceiling.** She loses the B3 final because winning wanted an improvisation she finds distasteful, and she says so at the lake, unprompted. Her taste is her compass and the thing that stops her — again in B5, where she loses to something her code doesn't price (DIV-067), and terribly in B6, when Harry grabs a spell he doesn't know and it nearly kills her (DIV-032, DIV-043).

Open: Q-083.

## The ugly side · DIV-071

*(settled)* What she aims at Harry is a game. What she aims at anyone else is not, and the difference is visible from outside.

- **At the B3 final** she turns on her own Beater with Harry a few feet away, and her face comes apart with it. He has seen her furious at him a hundred times; this is nothing like it. It's ugly, and it unsettles him. He does nothing with it — it joins everything else he can't place about her that year.
- **Becoming *dere* doesn't remove it,** it only points it elsewhere. It's still there in B6 when she goes after Hermione to make Harry hate her, and it's why that relapse reads as her rather than as a plot requirement ([sleeping-beauty](../threads/sleeping-beauty.md)).
- **The companion to DIV-029.** The feud with Harry is a private arrangement. Everyone else gets the real thing.

Open: Q-084.

## Potions

*(settled)* DIV-011, DIV-031. Snape has been her master since she was about five. She asked him to teach her so she could brew mama's medicine herself, and mama would never run out (DIV-030). Full story: [severus-snape](severus-snape.md).

- **The head start.** Six years of private lessons by the time she reaches Hogwarts, in the adult Snape's refined method.
- **B1.** At the first Potions lesson Snape shows her off, and she outdoes Hermione, whose hatred of her starts there ([uncle-severus](../scenes/b1/uncle-severus.md)).
- **B4.** She brews Harry a gillyweed extract for the Second Task (DIV-039, Q-051).
- **B4, after the Third Task.** Her first failure. She spends two days trying to brew something for Harry's night terrors and gets nowhere, and won't fall back on Dreamless Sleep because it's addictive. The answer turns out not to be a potion (DIV-061).
- **B6.** Harry beats her at the Draught of Living Death with the Prince's notes. That isn't because the Prince is better than his own older self. The sixth-year tricks are experimental and less elegant than what she was taught, but they work, and she has no drive left to one-up Harry: her priorities are elsewhere (DIV-015). She notices how much his new tricks resemble her technique, and so does Hermione (Q-042). In the same lesson she knows the Amortentia at a glance and doesn't raise her hand ([slughorns-first-lesson](../scenes/b6/slughorns-first-lesson.md), DIV-045).
- **Her perfume.** She brews her own pear perfume, the one Harry picked for her in B5, adding a new note to every batch and daring him to name it. She still wears it in B6 ([the-pear-perfume](../scenes/b5/the-pear-perfume.md)).
- **Why not paintings?** The same mindset pointed at someone else's art. She has the true Dessendre story — from family memory, not from a library, because the old houses keep their own — and reads it as a failed experiment rather than a warning: a technique that nearly worked, used badly. She says it out loud in B1, at eleven, about strangers: Aline got what she was after and merely had to ration her time better (DIV-073). It is why the Mirror ruins her in B6 — the one thing she can't approach with a method, and she cannot ration her own visits either ([painted-life](../setting/painted-life.md), DIV-072, Q-085).
- **Mama's medicine.** She takes over brewing it in Snape's stead in B7, at Shell Cottage, a tangible sign of how far she's come. Hermione's sealing spell lets her brew a whole batch ([narcissa-malfoy](narcissa-malfoy.md), Q-041).

## Duelling: the wand-tip shield *(settled)* · DIV-109

**She shields on the point of her wand.** Not a wall in front of her body — a Protego the size of a coin, made at the tip and put exactly where the incoming spell is going to be, so that she pricks curses out of the air and turns them aside. It is the cheapest possible way to block anything, and it is only available to someone with absolute control over the shape and placement of her own magic.

- **It is the character in one piece of technique.** She spends nothing and wastes nothing; she puts the right thing in the right place at the right instant. The duelling form of the same precision that makes her the best potioneer in the castle (DIV-011) and the better technician of the two (DIV-028).
- **It conserves her,** which matters in a long fight and matters more against anyone stronger. She bounces things back off it, and does, which is how she gloats.
- **And it cannot touch the Killing Curse.** Nothing scaled like a shield can (DIV-107). **The one incoming thing in the world her art has no answer to is the only one that matters**, and she knows it perfectly well.
- **There is nothing to try.** You cannot practise against it and there is no counter to research. It is not a project she failed at; it is simply something she knows, and knows painfully.
- **Her objection to interposition is not taste, it is concession.** Using cover means admitting the other duellist is better — and **Moody grants the point himself** (DIV-108): if you know the answer, a counter or a shield is the stronger move, because you can afford not to defend and keep the other man answering you instead. **That is exactly where she lives.** A pin-shield costs her nothing, and her knowledge of curses and their counters is wide enough that she usually does know the answer. Interposition is for people who do not.
- **So she scoffs, and then it is taken off her.** Harry tries it on her and she overcomes it — which is what makes him perfect it, and the mature version is what beats Krum (Q-123). **He does it so cleanly that she has to concede it is worth knowing.** She still prefers offence, and always will.
- **Where she concedes is a decision** (Q-144). *The clean version:* she grants the **technique** on her own in B4, on the evidence, and still refuses to credit the **man** — and it is Moody's apology, relayed in B5 (DIV-100, Q-139), that finally gets her to say his name without spitting. It splits the two concessions, keeps the apology scene about the apology, and lets her look past her own wounded pride under her own power.
- **And it probably does not happen in the Duelling Club.** Flitwick's club is a noble art with rules, and he can think the technique correct and still not want it dragged over his piste — which gives Harry a reason to work on it elsewhere, and makes the bout where she overcomes him a private one. Open: Q-144.
- **It is her ceiling again** (DIV-070): she loses the B3 final to an improvisation she finds distasteful, she loses in B5 to something her code does not price, and the one unanswerable curse in the Dark Arts is the thing her technique cannot reach.
- **So B7 is not a better shield.** She cannot block it, so she answers it instead — Expelliarmus, her wand, his hand over hers (🔒 DIV-006). **The thing that beats the Killing Curse is not a defence. It is two people.**

## Occlumency

*(settled)* DIV-020. She learns it from Snape in the summer between B4 and B5, continuing their old apprenticeship (DIV-031). The Malfoy discipline helps. Snape's stated reason is a general precaution for someone working under cover. It isn't enough: at their first meeting in summer 1996, Voldemort shatters her walls with ease (DIV-015). In the final duel she uses it another way. Instead of walling him out, she wraps Harry's mind in hers (DIV-021). Rules: [setting/magic.md](../setting/magic.md).

## Patronus

*(settled)* DIV-027. Mist from B3, when she studies the charm for Harry, until the Yule Ball gives her a corporeal one. Gone in B6, back at the Battle of Hogwarts. It's a dragon. Full arc: [threads/patronus.md](../threads/patronus.md).

## Scars

*(settled)* DIV-032. Snape saves her from Sectumsempra but arrives too late to prevent the scars, and she bears them for life. He's in time to dispel the curse itself, which counter-curses and potions fight off over the following days. How it happens and how Snape buries it: [sectumsempra](../scenes/b6/sectumsempra.md) (DIV-043). At least one scar runs from her belly to her neck, which reminds her a little of her mother's marks ([narcissa-malfoy](narcissa-malfoy.md)). How many more, and where: Q-044.

- **Harry** didn't know what the spell would do, and can never forget the mistake he made in a moment of anger.
- **She** goes through something similar at first. Eventually she comes to like them, as a symbol of what they got over together. They match his lightning scar.
- **Their game.** She sometimes uses them to guilt-trip him into doing her favours. He always kisses them when he sees them, hoping one day it'll make them disappear.

## Apparition

*(settled)* DIV-054. She skips the B6 Apparition lessons. She's in no state for them, and she believes the only escape from Voldemort is to carry out the mission, so she doesn't bother (DIV-015). In B7 she can't Apparate and doesn't learn: Harry dances her away instead ([apparate-dancing](../scenes/b7/apparate-dancing.md)). After the war she learns, so as not to be outdone, and still makes him dance her, because she enjoys it.

## The two times reality touched her *(settled)* · DIV-095, DIV-015

**The corridor in September 1994 is the first time another person ever did violence to her body**, and the Mark is the second. She has duelled since she was twelve and Hermione Granger once slapped her (DIV-069); neither is this. Crouch beats her half to death through Moody's hands and she comes back bleeding and in shock, and **the discovery is not that it hurt — it is that she is not exempt.**

- **What she does with it is latch on to Harry and refuse to let go** ([the-ferret](../scenes/b4/the-ferret.md)). Not romance: she is valuing, under shock, and for once correctly. The one Slytherin reflex she cannot switch off is the ledger, and it points at the boy who held off a horde of Dementors for her and killed a basilisk at twelve.
- **She would deny it afterwards**, is humiliated by it, and refuses to count it — so it concedes nothing, and the balcony is still three months of silence away.
- **Dreams were kinder than the facts both times, and both times she had no say.** It is the same lesson twice, and the second one is burned into her arm ([sleeping-beauty](../threads/sleeping-beauty.md)).

## The Mark

*(settled)* DIV-015. Unlike canon Draco, she's never proud of it. She's ashamed of it and feels defiled, and it reminds her constantly of the chains that hold her. In the hollow weeks she won't let Harry touch her ([sleeping-beauty](../threads/sleeping-beauty.md)).

*(settled)* **It makes her tinder** · DIV-080. Lustrum burns Dark magic present in a person however it got there, so from B6 she is flammable, specifically and visibly, by a thing that was done to her and is not hers. **Twice over**, in fact: the Mark on one arm and Harry's Sectumsempra scars from belly to neck (DIV-032), neither of which she chose. The fire does not do fault.

- It is the nightmare in a mechanic — the script reclaiming her, written on her body in a substance that burns ([sleeping-beauty](../threads/sleeping-beauty.md)).
- At the Battle of Hogwarts, McGonagall covers her (DIV-023) while carrying the one weapon that would kill her outright, and neither of them knows it.
- Whether it is ever used on the page, and whether she ever learns it: Q-111.

## "I'm glad it was you" *(settled)* · DIV-092

The morning after the Third Task, Harry says he should have pulled out of the Tournament — that if all he brings the person next to him is death, he should never have competed, whatever it would have cost him. She cuts him off. She does not tell him he did nothing wrong. She says she is glad it was him who made it out alive.

- **It is not comfort,** and it is not meant as any. It is a statement that it was good that he lived, again, and she is saying it over a dead boy without flinching.
- **It is the only thing that reaches him,** because what he wants and cannot ask for is to be wanted, as himself ([harry-potter](harry-potter.md)). She is the one person who gives him that, and she gives it in the hardest possible register.
- **She is honest, hard-hearted and in love,** all three at once, and the line only works because all three are true. She does not get softened for the occasion — the same reason the others' coda exists in [the-hospital-wing](../scenes/b4/the-hospital-wing.md).
- **What she does not say,** and what has to land anyway: that she would trade a hundred Cedric Diggorys for him. Saying it outright is too much. **Harry is her happiness** — he is not to be ashamed of surviving, and nobody gives up and dies because things have got hard.
- **Her upbringing earns its keep in the same scene.** The Diggorys' visit is a delicate social occasion and she is an expert navigator of those (see *Drilled manners*), so she runs it and Harry does not have to.
- **She will not take her own advice.** Put in the same position in B6 she gives up and sets about dying, and does not come back to this until the raid on the Manor in B7 ([sleeping-beauty](../threads/sleeping-beauty.md), DIV-015, DIV-049).

## What the hurdle actually is *(settled)*

**Her problem is never social, and it is never her father.** She is not hiding a friendship with Harry Potter because a Malfoy could not be seen with one, and she is not weighing what the Slytherin table would say. The usual version of this story is not ours.

**The hurdle is entirely self-centred**, and it moves:

| | What is in her way |
|---|---|
| B1 | She wants him, because at Madam Malkin's he did not see *a Malfoy*, he saw something wondrous (DIV-009) |
| B1 | Then she is spurned, and she cannot bear it: she thought she had found something she had never seen before, and he brushed her aside |
| B2 | So she sets out to prove to herself he was never special. That is what the competition is for |
| B3 | She no longer knows where to stand. She has watched him kill a basilisk, so he *is* special — and she keeps the rivalry up because it is the only footing she has. Then Buckbeak, and the first time she acts against her father (🔒 DIV-003) |
| B3, end | She has proved she can act on her own verdict against the family script (DIV-013) — autonomy, not courage, since she was never afraid of him. And she knows he is exactly the prince she thought he was. **And now she feels unworthy of him**, and is sure she has lost him to Hermione Granger, who is always at his side (DIV-041) |
| B4 | Working out that she is allowed to love him for real. She asks him outright, on the balcony at the Ball: *am I allowed to dream, Harry? Someone like me?* (DIV-095) |

- **She is too full of herself to fear the room.** She believes nothing in the world can touch her, which is her father's doing: he spent fourteen years almost never telling her no, because she is the only child he will ever have and he cannot afford to lose her — full account in [lucius-malfoy](lucius-malfoy.md), DIV-030. The single exception is her prince's opinion of her, which is why that is the only thing that can hurt her and why it takes four books. **Her B3 Boggart is the proof on the page:** asked what she could possibly be afraid of, she has no answer at all, and what comes out of the wardrobe is Harry telling her he wishes he'd never met her ([remedial-boggart](../scenes/b3/remedial-boggart.md), DIV-017).
- **She is the image of her mother,** which is part of why: he cannot look at her without seeing Narcissa and the wound in the same glance.
- **She is not on her way to being nice, and B4 opens by proving it.** In the first week of term she draws on Hermione Granger in a crowded corridor and is bounced down it as a ferret for her trouble, and Harry carries her to the hospital wing (DIV-095). The turn at the end of B3 was about *him* and about nobody else, and the balcony three months later is her asking whether a person who does that is allowed to want this.
- **And Harry is the one who gives her permission** — by asking her to the Ball (🔒 DIV-004), with the Second Task sealing it (🔒 DIV-005). Which is the mirror of what she does for him in the same year, when she tells him it is all right to want to win (DIV-089). **Each of them spends B4 giving the other permission to want something,** and neither notices doing it.

## The ledger *(settled)*

**Every affront is paid back, so that nobody tries again.** It is her father's doctrine and she has it whole, and it is the reason forgiveness is very nearly alien to her — not a hardness she adopted but an accounting system she was raised inside. Nothing is ever simply let go; it is entered, and it is settled.

- **It runs in both directions,** which is what saves her. A debt of honour has to be repaid exactly as an insult does, which is the noblesse oblige behind Buckbeak (🔒 DIV-003, Q-005) and the whole of why Shell Cottage works: Hermione's sealing spell saves her mother's medicine, and from that moment Hermione is a person. **That is not a change of heart. It is bookkeeping**, and it is the only route by which someone like her ever gets there (Q-096).
- **It is why she takes Hermione's accusation seriously** rather than laughing it off (DIV-041, DIV-095). Being told she is nasty and vile is not a slight to be filed; it is a verdict she privately agrees with. The part she disputes is only the ownership of Harry.
- **And it is why Cho has a miserable B5** (DIV-041). Not jealousy and not defence — she has already won and knows it. An affront was offered and the ledger does not care that it failed.
- **There is no entry for amends,** and that is the most useful thing about it. Paying back and repaying both have something coming the other way; conceding you were wrong and offering nothing but the concession is not a transaction she has a form for. So when Harry tells her in September that what she did was hideous, she can neither argue with it nor do anything about it. **She stews.** She would like to be open with him and cannot make herself do the work, because what would it buy — it would not undo the corridor, and in her own accounting she would be humiliating herself for no gain (DIV-095).
  - **Which fixes the order of B4, and the order is not negotiable.** She can only afford to pay once she has decided she is allowed to want the thing she would be paying for. **Permission first, amends second** — the balcony, and then the promise about his friends (Q-135). Anything that has her mending before Christmas steals the question she asks there.
- **The one person outside the system is Harry**, and she does not notice that either. He has been refusing her, beating her and embarrassing her since they were eleven, and none of it is ever entered.

## What she cannot believe *(settled)*

**She cannot make herself believe Harry would want her, and she discounts every signal that says otherwise.** Her account of his decency is that it is general: the golden boy is somewhat nice to everyone, it costs him nothing, and it is not about her. So the carry out of the corridor in September means nothing, his jealousy at the Duelling Club means nothing (DIV-029), and the evidence piles up all year without ever being read.

- **It is the exact mirror of his blindness.** He has no framework in which a Malfoy means any of it ([harry-potter](harry-potter.md)); she has no framework in which she is wantable. Both of them are looking straight at the evidence and both have the wrong filing system, which is the engine of the whole thing and the reason it takes until the Ball.
- **It is why the carry confuses her** rather than delighting her, and why the small, awkward conversation that follows it does not resolve anything (DIV-095).
- **And it is what the balcony is for.** *Am I allowed to dream, Harry? Someone like me?* is this, asked out loud, three months later ([yule-ball](../scenes/b4/yule-ball.md)).
- **The one thing that can get past it is a referee.** She can refuse a kindness, a gift and an amends, because her ledger has no form for any of them — **but she cannot refuse a verdict from an instrument that gains nothing by lying.** The Goblet looks at Harry Potter and names her (🔒 DIV-005), and *Potter is like that with everybody* stops being available to her. **That is what finally makes her brave enough to ask** ([which-one-is-real](../scenes/b4/which-one-is-real.md), DIV-104, DIV-087).
- **And what corrects her is a fact, not a reassurance:** he was furious with her when he picked her up out of that corridor, and did it anyway. She had assumed it was reflexive decency, which is exactly why it was worth nothing to her (DIV-104).

## The tsundere engine

- **When a thing she wants turns out not to be allowed, she launders it rather than give it up.** The founding instance is Madam Malkin's: the compliment she badly wants to keep comes out of a Muggle story, so she interrogates the boy's blood until he passes, and then takes it. The reasoning runs backwards — the compliment was never the Muggles' to give — and it is the shape of everything after it: the fairy tales, the novels, Muggle Studies, and eventually him (DIV-009, DIV-010). **The scarf runs on the same engine**: she will not wear a trophy to be seen wearing it, so she waits for cold weather to hand her a reason, and by B3 reaching for that one is automatic ([duelling-club](../threads/duelling-club.md)).
- **Her snobbery is the price she pays to keep things, not a weapon she reaches for.** She isn't lashing out at Madam Malkin's and she never knows she has lost him. She is cheerful the whole way through, and repeats her father's opinion of Hagrid without once looking up (DIV-009).
- **She admires competence, daring and valour, and it comes out as contempt.** Her line is "if *Potter* of all people can…" (DIV-013).
- **Her objections run through taste.** Wrong things are ugly things, which is how a bigot's snobbery becomes the way out of the bigotry, and also the ceiling she can't climb past (DIV-070).
- **She does the right thing so Potter can't beat her at it** (Q-005, answered).
- **She's not above subterfuge and pettiness when throwing darts at her target.** She milks the B3 injury for weeks (DIV-017).
- **Her hoard.** Every trophy she has won off him at the Duelling Club since their second year, kept on a stand under a Gryffindor banner in a room nobody knows about (DIV-059, DIV-060). He has a smaller one of hers. Neither had ever mentioned it.
- **She wants to own his happiness.** In B3 she's delighted he has no happy memories, because then she can give him the only one he'll have, and prove his friends couldn't. Never spoken ([lap-pillow](../scenes/b3/lap-pillow.md)).
- **Theirs is an exclusive game.** She's delighted when he's jealous, but the thought of using anyone to provoke him disgusts her (DIV-029).
- **Her own mother is not immune.** At the World Cup Harry calls Narcissa a queen without the least idea what he is doing, and Narcissa goes — then holds out a hand to be kissed, for a lark, and he obliges without reading it. Dracaena sulks: *she* is the princess, and she has never once got anything like it. Her mother only giggles (DIV-116).
- **Nothing in the Malfoy arsenal answers sincerity.** Her training is built for rooms, rivals and advantage; it has no form for someone who is not playing. It is why he gets past her every time without trying, and her mother is no better defended — the difference is that Narcissa is happily married and can simply enjoy it (DIV-116).
- **"Your *other* girlfriend."** Her permanent phrase for Hermione Granger. It starts as a real fear — the World Cup, the school's assumption, Rita's November article, all of it pointing the same way — and once the fear is gone it stays on as the scathing way to name her, which is what she needs after she gives up the slur for Harry's sake (DIV-118, DIV-041, [the-little-basilisk](../scenes/b4/the-little-basilisk.md)).
- **She studies him.** She watches all his matches like a hawk and takes overly detailed notes, not all of them technical ([quidditch](../threads/quidditch.md)).
- **Her tenderness is borrowed.** When she wants to give him something loving, her only model is how her mother loves her ([lap-pillow](../scenes/b3/lap-pillow.md)).
- **Her soft side leaks when she forgets herself.** The Potions "cooking" daydream, then a scramble to save face (DIV-017). When she beats Harry at the Duelling Club she forgets to gloat: "Did you see that? I won!" (DIV-028).
- **She'll treasure the "princess" moment forever and never admit it.** Harry regrets it.

Open: how the balance shifts in B6–B7, and what keeps her a person rather than a trope.

## Dobby *(settled)* · DIV-076, Q-098

She was good to him, and not out of kindness. He is her retinue, and a lady treasures her retinue — the same noblesse oblige that saves Buckbeak (Q-005). Not friendship, and she would be offended by the suggestion.

So Harry freeing him in B2 is, from where she stands, Potter killing her family's elf out of a Gryffindor's idea of a good deed, and taking a dependent from the family that owed him protection. Everyone knows what dismissal does (DIV-075). Under our rules she is right about the cruelty and wrong about nothing except the outcome.

Then Dobby keeps turning up alive, for years, and she is the only person in the world positioned to notice that this is impossible. When she works it out and what she makes of it: Q-098. By summer 1995 she is asking a favour of something that should have died three years ago (DIV-065).

**The B2 whiplash** *(open)* · Q-101. If she is in the Chamber of Secrets — the inbox has her watching Harry kill the basilisk — then the book hands her the best and worst thing Potter has ever done to her in the same week: she sees him kill a monster, and then learns he has carelessly killed her household's elf. It is B1 inverted — there she reached for friendship and made things worse; here *he* does the damage, meaning well — and it explains the state she is in when B3 opens.

**The B3 encounter** *(open)* · Q-101. She finds Dobby alive at the castle, and he tells her he is glad Harry freed him, because her father was a horrible master. A bound elf could not say that. She cannot take it there — accepting it means Potter was right and her father was the villain — so she walks off with the grievance intact and chews on it for months. It is the first half of the case against her father, and Buckbeak is the second.

## Winky, and the better master *(settled)* · DIV-077, Q-098

In B4 she tells Harry that providing for his own elf is his responsibility and that he had better be a better master at it. Noblesse oblige — a lady treasures her retinue — and the only advice anyone gives all year that would actually keep an elf alive. He has never used the word in his life: not master, friend (DIV-076). They are arguing about different things and neither of them notices.

She is also lecturing him about an elf she believes he killed, and cannot say so without admitting she cared (Q-098).

**Then she watches Winky die,** in the kitchens, with Harry, Ron, Hermione and Dobby ([the-kitchens](../scenes/b4/the-kitchens.md)). No pureblood ever does this. The stories her house passed down say a dismissed elf *goes away*; what she sees is a suicide carried out over eight months in a warm room, which is far worse than the thing she was told, and which nobody told her because nobody has ever watched.

**And she stays for the burial,** which no Malfoy has ever done. Dobby asks Harry for it and Harry digs; what goes in is dust; it is the first elf grave in Britain, a thousand years after the binding (DIV-075, DIV-077).

She makes no argument of it and changes no allegiance — when she breaks with her cause it is out of fear and then love, not conviction (Q-083). But it is the one piece of evidence against her class that she gathered herself, standing up, with her own eyes, and it is why she can end up where she does (Q-100).

## Hermione's work, later *(draft)* · Q-100

The answer to all of this, when it finally comes, is a Muggle-born's, and it works by ending old houses.

From where Dracaena was raised that is every grievance she has about Mudbloods handed to her with evidence: they respect nothing, and they will take an axe to what they never troubled to understand — the same objection she first makes in the Dessendre corridor, about someone else's dead being interesting (DIV-074). Her side will hate Hermione more than they already do, and for once they will have a real point: the oldest houses in Britain, pulled down for house-elves.

The book lets them have the point and keeps the verdict. She is right, and it is worth it.

*Author's lean, post-story and not canon:* Dracaena ends up campaigning for her behind closed doors, in the rooms Hermione cannot get into (Q-096). It is earned in the Hogwarts kitchens in B4 and nowhere else. She is not persuaded by Hermione and never will be; she simply knows what the alternative looks like, because she stood in the room for it.
