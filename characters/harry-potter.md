# Harry Potter

**Role:** protagonist.
**Canon baseline:** canon Harry, harsher than the original, and in love with Dracaena Malfoy.
**Changes from canon:** DIV-004–006, DIV-009, DIV-116, DIV-117, DIV-015, DIV-017–019, DIV-021–023, DIV-026, DIV-028, DIV-029, DIV-032, DIV-037–039, DIV-041, DIV-043–052, DIV-058–063, DIV-065–069, DIV-072, DIV-073, DIV-089–092, DIV-095, DIV-096, DIV-105, DIV-106, DIV-108

The romance itself lives in [threads/harry-dracaena.md](../threads/harry-dracaena.md); this file is what he is, not what happens between them.

## Core

- **Want:** to be wanted. Not admired, not needed, not thanked for saving everyone — wanted, by someone, as himself. He could not say this out loud and does not know it about himself until very late.
- **Need:** _open._
- **Wound / fear:** that what people value him for is the scar and what comes with it, and that there is nothing underneath it worth staying for.
- **Lie he believes:** _open._

The thesis in [core/themes.md](../core/themes.md) runs through him: his found family gives him belonging, and only she gives him desire. That is why King's Cross goes the way it does (DIV-048).

## The person over the prize *(settled)* · DIV-069

The trait the B3 Quidditch final exists to show. He has just won the match of his life and his House is carrying him off, and he looks back at his rival alone on the pitch on her knees, and goes back to her. He would rather be with her than be celebrated.

- **It isn't nobility.** He's happy, and the kindness falls out of that. Conscious generosity would read as pity and she would throw it back at him ([the-quidditch-final](../scenes/b3/the-quidditch-final.md)).
- **What he actually valued** was the flying. The Cup is incidental; the best hour he has had was spent against her, and he wants another one. The offered hand is an invitation to play again, not consolation.
- **He doesn't know he's repaying anything.** It mirrors the train, where she offered her hand and he refused it, and the mirroring is hers to feel (DIV-009).

It recurs, each time at a higher price, which is what makes it character rather than a single nice beat:

| Book | What he gives up | For | Refs |
|---|---|---|---|
| B3 | His own victory lap | Going back to her | DIV-069 |
| B5 | Quidditch for life, and a Cup that means nothing to him anyway | Hitting Montague for what he said about her | DIV-067 |
| B7 | The plan, mid-raid | Finding her to apologise | DIV-049 |
| B7 | Going on, at King's Cross | Coming back for her | DIV-048 |

**B4 is the exception, and the exception is the point.** It is the one book where he goes for the prize — wanting it, in front of everyone, with her ribbon on his wrist — and the boy who would not let go of it dies for it (DIV-089). He is not taking the prize *over* her; he is taking it *for* her, which is the same axis turned round. It costs more than any of the giving does.

## Instinct, and what it costs *(settled)*

One trait, and both its faces. He is instinctive where she is technical: he duels on reflex and thrives in chaos (DIV-028), and he flies a broom that does exactly what it is asked, including what he didn't mean to ask (DIV-068).

- **It wins him the final.** He takes it on the one move where he stops minding the Firebolt and simply trusts it. The winning improvisation is precisely the one she will not make, and she names it afterwards: uncouth (DIV-069, DIV-070).
- **It maims her.** Sectumsempra is the same reflex — a spell out of a book, reached for in anger, without knowing what it does (DIV-032, DIV-043).
- **It beats a professional.** In the maze he puts down the Durmstrang champion at fourteen, because Krum knows the shape of a contest and Harry does not fight in one. The trait has no counter and no brakes, and both facts are in evidence in the same hour (DIV-089, DIV-090, Q-123).

Both are the same man. The file should never treat his instinct as simply a gift or simply a fault, because the series' worst moment and its best flying come out of it.

## Permission, and what it buys *(settled)* · DIV-096

What the Moody year leaves in him is not a syllabus. **There is no list of Dark spells**, because our magic does not work that way: Dark magic runs on intent and the fire counts spells rather than names (DIV-084). What he absorbs is the doctrine — *spells are weapons, they are not good or bad* — and from then on an ordinary spell cast wanting harm leaves the same residue as a curse.

- **What moves is the threshold, not the reflex.** He was always fast. What he has never had is an adult telling him that going first is correct, and once he has that it takes less and less to make him act. **A disposition, not a capability** — which is why nothing he does afterwards can be pointed at and called a thing Moody taught him. He is not a different duellist at the end of the year; he is a quicker-triggered one, and there is no skill anybody can name as the culprit (DIV-105).
- **Nobody teaches him to fight dirty.** He has improvised past every problem since he was eleven and has never once had the correct answer to anything. The year removes the apology, not the instinct. He is also told, sincerely, by the most decorated Auror in Britain, that he would make a good one — and a fourteen-year-old being hunted wears that like a borrowed jacket. It is never his ambition.
- **And it is handed over socially as well as professionally.** After the First Task Moody debriefs him like a colleague and pours him a drink, because his frame is the Auror office and not a school. **It is the only time all year an adult treats the dragon as an engagement he survived** rather than an ordeal he was put through — and it is the first of four, not a one-off: the drink in November, the flask thrown back at the lake in February, the stamp on the Conjunctivitis Curse in the spring where the doctrine lands, and nothing before the maze (DIV-102, DIV-097, [the-flask](../scenes/b4/the-flask.md)). **He is addressed as *junior Auror* throughout**, and wears it. **And there is a third drink after everything**, at the end of term: he finds the man packing, is told plainly why he is leaving and cannot argue with it, and is handed the flask as a prize — checked, drunk, given straight back — *full marks*. **He hears the discharge in it**, and it is the loneliest thing anybody says to him all year (DIV-106).
- **The other thing he takes out of that office is that he is safe with Moody.** It is a real lesson — the rule has exceptions, and this man is one of them — and it is true except for the five hours when Crouch is riding him, which Harry can never separate out afterwards. **The man who teaches him to verify is the one thing he never checks** (DIV-086, DIV-097).
- **There is nothing illicit to find, which is the point.** No Restricted Section, no banned book, nothing for Ron or Hermione to refuse. They drill in an empty classroom as in canon and no one present could tell the difference.
- **The one named spell is the Conjunctivitis Curse, and canon hands it over in four moves.** He does not watch Krum face the dragon — he is in the tent — he hears about it afterwards, and **Sirius says it is what he would have done.** Precedent, proof it beats a dragon, and the blessing of the one adult whose approval he wants. He learns it himself, off his own bat, and uses it on the sphinx ([the-third-task](../scenes/b4/the-third-task.md)).
- **And this is what the B6 reflex is bought with.** Not *he was taught Dark magic*: **he was taught that there is no such thing as an illegitimate move.** The DA then spends a year making fighting a reflex rather than a decision, and he has been under the Cruciatus himself. In the bathroom he is not choosing a spell; he is a boy who thinks *if she gets it off, I am finished*, and reaches (DIV-032, DIV-043).

## What can't be taken back *(settled)* · DIV-072, DIV-073

Planted in B1 and paid in B6. At Clea's landscape he is shown that magic of that order reaches past whoever worked it, outlives them, and cannot be undone — and that the creatures stranded by it can refuse the only help he has to offer. He feels it, he can do nothing, and he is eleven.

He does not become careful. That is the point: knowing a thing and acting on it with his blood up are different, and the gap is his. It comes due with the scars that don't come out and a Mirror that doesn't mend.

Nothing in the text joins the corridor to that night. Full account of the plant in [setting/painted-life.md](../setting/painted-life.md).

## Feelings ahead of understanding *(settled)* · DIV-037

His heart runs well in front of his self-knowledge, and the books use it as evidence.

- **The veela don't touch him** at the 1994 World Cup, because he is already in love. He has no idea. Fleur later uses the same rule to read him, and he can't say what she can see (DIV-038).
- **He can't account for his own happiness** at seeing her again that summer, and doesn't try very hard.
- **The lap-pillow smile.** He writes the scene off as a prank and then keeps returning to the smile and finding it pretty, which confuses him. He won't count it as a happy memory until the lake — and it is the memory that powers the Patronus that saves everyone (DIV-018).

## He doesn't read her *(settled)*

The tsundere engine from his side. Her sincerity is in front of him from B1 — the Potions servitude, the Patronus study, the trophies she has been quietly hoarding since B2 — and he reads almost none of it until the lake (DIV-017, DIV-059, Q-035). After the final the mask goes back on and he is confused and a little hurt, and nothing visibly changes for the rest of the year (Q-082).

The reader is meant to be ahead of him. He is not stupid about her; he has no framework in which a Malfoy means any of it.

**And it runs both ways, which is the engine.** He cannot read a Malfoy meaning it; she cannot believe she is wantable, and writes his decency off as the golden boy being pleasant to everyone (DIV-041, DIV-095). Two people looking straight at the evidence with the wrong filing system, which is why it takes until the Ball and why neither of them is being stupid.

## A calamity *(settled)* · DIV-092

What B4 leaves him with, and the shape his guilt takes from then on. Every other time, something tried to kill him and he came out on top. In the maze he did not manage it, and somebody else paid. From that he concludes that he is a **misfortune** — a thing that happens to the people near him.

- **The premise is false.** He settles on *if I had not been in it, Cedric would have won.* The real final was Harry and Krum, and Krum would most likely have taken the Cup (DIV-089). Nobody ever puts this to him.
- **He is not told he did nothing wrong,** because the person who answers it will not say that. She says she is glad it was him who made it out alive — not reassurance, not consolation, a flat statement that it was good that he lived, again ([the-diggorys](../scenes/b4/the-diggorys.md)).
- **And that is the only register that reaches him,** because it is his want exactly: not absolution, not gratitude, not admiration — being wanted, by someone, as himself. The found family gives him belonging; she gives him this ([themes](../core/themes.md)).

## The charm he has no idea he has *(draft)*

**When he isn't thinking about himself, he is disarming, and he never once knows he's doing it.** It only runs when he's not trying — happy, awestruck, or too absorbed to be self-conscious — so it can never be a tactic, and the moment he notices himself doing it, it's gone. He is the opposite of the girl who can work any room she chooses and cannot say one civil sentence to him ([dracaena-malfoy](dracaena-malfoy.md)): **neither of them can do on purpose what the other does without trying.**

- **Madam Malkin's is the first showing.** He asks a stranger whether she's Sleeping Beauty, goes up on tiptoe to check her eyes against the princess's, and tells her hers are pretty too. It isn't flattery and she can tell, which is exactly what makes it unanswerable — nobody has ever paid her a compliment that wasn't really about being a Malfoy (DIV-009).
- **The B3 final is the same trait at full strength.** He has just won the match of his life, he is happy, and the hand he offers falls out of that. Conscious generosity would read as pity and she would throw it back (DIV-069).
- **It isn't only for her**, and the World Cup proves it on her mother, which is the one place it has consequences. He calls Narcissa Malfoy a queen, means it, and has no idea he has said anything at all ([narcissa-malfoy](narcissa-malfoy.md), DIV-116).
- **It runs both ways with the Malfoy women, and neither side has a guard up.** Their whole arsenal is social — the curtsy, the aimed compliment, the rival held politely in reserve — and sincerity is the one thing it was never built to answer. He has nothing against them either: Narcissa holds out a hand and he kisses it because he can feel he is meant to. **Each of them is the other's blind spot.** The difference is that Narcissa is happily married and can simply enjoy it, and does, chiefly at her husband's expense (DIV-116).

### The leak *(draft)* · DIV-117

**The same thing with no charm in it: when he's absorbed enough, the thought comes out of his mouth and he is certain he only thought it.** It is almost always about Dracaena, somebody always hears, and challenged on it he asks what they are talking about and means it.

- **The first is the World Cup**, across the stands, before he has even met Narcissa: *no wonder Dracaena is that pretty, then* (DIV-116).
- **It is the only reliable leak in the series.** He cannot say what he wants on purpose and does not know he wants it, so the only evidence that ever escapes him escapes by accident — which makes the gag the instrument by which his friends work him out long before he does.
- **Ration it:** once a book at most, never in a scene carrying weight. Where it lands after B4: Q-152.

### Nobody else gets to say it *(draft)*

**A separate tell, and not the same thing as the leak:** he knows perfectly well he is speaking. He can complain about her at any length — and does — and the instant somebody else agrees with him he turns on them.

- **The first and smallest** is the robe shop in B3. He has run her down for a solid minute, Lindsay says the girl sounds like a right piece of work, and he corrects her on the spot. *She's not. She's just—* and stops (DIV-119).
- **The largest is Montague**, and it costs him Quidditch for life (DIV-067). Same reflex, two books and one enormous price apart, and the text never joins them up.
- **It is not chivalry and he would deny it was anything.** The complaint and the defence feel identical from the inside, which is why he never notices he has done two opposite things in a row.

## Harsher than canon *(draft)* · Q-024

Angrier and more willing to hurt people than the original, with his Horcrux playing a larger part in it. Visible in the Durmstrang duel, which he wins too hard (DIV-029); in Montague (DIV-067); in going cold when Ron goes through his trunk, and leaving with her without answering the accusation (DIV-063); in forcing his way past Crabbe and Goyle at the hospital wing door; and in a sixth year spent furious. How much is the Horcrux and how much is him is Q-024.

## Daydreaming, forward *(settled)* · DIV-043

He dreams as much as she does. The difference is direction, and it is the whole of why he survives the Mirror and she doesn't: he pictures a future with her, unprompted, in her room in B4, and the Mirror shows him their wedding in B6 while it shows her a dance that already happened. She retreats; he projects.

The warning aimed at him is not Aline's but Renoir's — the man who went into the painting for the best reason there is and lost years anyway (DIV-072). Being forward-facing is not protection on its own. He is told that story at eleven, by her, in the corridor (DIV-073), which makes B6 a choice rather than an escape he stumbles into: he knows exactly what he is standing in. What saves him is that he refuses to go under with her and gets her out instead (DIV-049, DIV-052).

## Magic

- **Duelling.** Instinctive, chaotic, uneven against her precision, and the two of them have read each other for years (DIV-028).
- **Patronus.** Powered by the lap-pillow memory from B3 (DIV-018). See [threads/patronus.md](../threads/patronus.md).
- **Resonance.** Blocked by the Horcrux until the forest; first flicker at the B3 lake (DIV-021, Q-028).
- **Flying.** A wild instrument suits him. He wins when he stops managing it (DIV-068).
- **Sacrifice.** His death in the forest protects nobody (DIV-026).
- **The charge, B4.** Bound by the Goblet on pain of his magic, he carries the Tournament's charge as it grows through the year — spells landing harder than he means them to, and **Dracaena notices before he does**, because she is the one who knows his magic best (DIV-087, DIV-028). It buys him nothing he wants: he is strongest, most watched and most hounded in the year he cannot make himself ask one girl a question. It is awarded to him in the graveyard, bound to a headstone with his wand out of reach, and drawn straight back out for the ritual. **It never settles, so it leaves no mark**: there is no drained Harry in B5 and nothing to convalesce from, and the book is never allowed to blame his temper on his magic (Q-122, dropped). What he keeps is knowing what it was.
- **No shape.** His edge against anyone trained, and the reason he beats Krum: three years against the best technician in the castle have made him a reader of other people's casting, and left his own unreadable. She gave him that by being perfect at him, bout after bout, since they were twelve — how often the club actually meets is deliberately uncommitted (DIV-028, DIV-089, Q-127). How it works on the page is **Q-123**: the read is hers, the finish is Moody's ground (DIV-108).
- **Interposition.** Matter in the way of what shields cannot stop (DIV-108). He gets it from the front of a classroom, as one of a hundred, and it is the one tangible thing Moody leaves him. **He has a field day with it** — it is the missing piece of his style, because he already improvises and this hands him the environment.
  - **His own variant is bouncing his own spells off placed Protegos**, which nobody taught him. **It wins him one bout against her and she loathes it** — it looks like missing on purpose, and being beaten by it is being beaten by a clown. Where it lands: Q-144.
  - **It is what finally makes him apply himself to Transfiguration**, the subject the technique is built on, and what gives McGonagall's B6 advice — keep studying with me if you want to be an Auror — something to bite on.
  - **Dracaena overcomes his first attempt**, which is why he perfects it; the mature version beats Krum (Q-123). In B5 he teaches it to the DA, with Balthier Thynne the obvious partner (DIV-082). In B7 he fights the Battle of Hogwarts with the castle.
- **What Moody taught him** was real, and some of it was Crouch's, and he can never sort which (DIV-086). The boy who was told that spells are only weapons is the boy who uses one out of a book he doesn't understand in B6 — see *Permission, and what it buys* above (DIV-096, DIV-043).
- **And in B5 two men tell him he is a grown one.** Sirius sees James, Moody sees the soldier he made, and neither of them is looking at Harry — which is how he stays the loneliest person in a house full of allies, and why the Ministry happens (DIV-099, Q-140). The same two give him the same verdict on Dracaena, a year apart and in opposite registers, and he takes Sirius's (DIV-101).

## The elves *(settled)* · DIV-075, DIV-076, DIV-078

A house-elf is needed and used and never wanted, which is his want exactly. That is why he is the only person in a thousand years to answer it, and why he could never explain what he did.

- **B2.** He catches what Lucius throws away, offers friendship instead of ownership, and has no idea that the condition is the whole point or that he qualifies only because he has no house for the curse to grip (DIV-076). He does not talk Dobby round, either: Dobby changes his own mind, because Harry has accidentally given him something to hope for.
- **He never once thinks he has freed anybody,** and he is right not to. Freedom is not what an elf wants and not what Dobby was asking for (DIV-075). What Harry offers is a person to belong to, which is the only thing on the table.
- **He gives Dobby the Burrow** — the house he wishes were his own — and thinks nothing of it.
- **And his vault.** Dobby has it, functionally without limit, and Harry never gives it a second thought: it keeps Winky all through B4 without Harry noticing he is paying for a second elf, and it is how he finally repays the Weasleys, who will not take anything from him. A small room added on for himself that nobody asked for, the structure quietly shored up, repairs on transparently silly pretexts. Molly cannot refuse a room for Harry.
- **And it is what feeds him.** Dobby has never eaten in his life and is suddenly hungry, because an unhoused elf has no house to draw on (Q-102). The vault is his board, and neither of them ever puts it that way.
- **Every one of those kindnesses is spending him.** It all comes out of a thing with no house to draw on, and Dobby is delighted to do it, because enlarging a house is the oldest thing in him.
- **It costs Dobby his life.** A person-anchored elf runs on one person and nothing else, and Harry had nothing else. Dobby dies of Harry's homelessness (DIV-050).
- **He cannot do it twice.** Kreacher is already his, completely, through Sirius's will, and being kind to him changes nothing — which is why the answer there has to be Hermione's and not his (DIV-078).
- **He also cannot undo it.** Clothes do nothing to an unhoused elf, so Harry could not free Dobby again if he tried, and Dobby could leave him any day and doesn't (DIV-075).
- **He never learns the history.** He has the remedy and no theory; Hermione has the theory and no remedy.
