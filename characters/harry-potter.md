# Harry Potter

**Role:** protagonist.
**Canon baseline:** canon Harry, harsher than the original, and in love with Dracaena Malfoy.
**Changes from canon:** DIV-004–006, DIV-009, DIV-015, DIV-017–019, DIV-021–023, DIV-026, DIV-028, DIV-029, DIV-032, DIV-037–039, DIV-041, DIV-043–052, DIV-058–063, DIV-065–069, DIV-072, DIV-073

The romance itself lives in [threads/harry-dracaena.md](../threads/harry-dracaena.md); this file is what he is, not what happens between them.

## Core

- **Want:** to be wanted. Not admired, not needed, not thanked for saving everyone — wanted, by someone, as himself. He could not say this out loud and does not know it about himself until very late.
- **Need:** _open._
- **Wound / fear:** that what people value him for is the scar and what comes with it, and that there is nothing underneath it worth staying for.
- **Lie he believes:** _open._

The thesis in [core/themes.md](../core/themes.md) runs through him: his found family gives him belonging, and only she gives him desire. That is why King's Cross goes the way it does (DIV-048).

## The person over the prize *(settled)* · DIV-069

The trait the B3 Quidditch final exists to show. He has just won the match of his life and his House is carrying him off, and he looks back at his rival alone on the pitch on her knees, and goes back to her. He would rather be with her than be celebrated.

- **It isn't nobility.** He's happy, and the kindness falls out of that. Conscious generosity would read as pity and she would throw it back at him ([the-quidditch-final](../scenes/b3/the-quidditch-final.md)).
- **What he actually valued** was the flying. The Cup is incidental; the best hour he has had was spent against her, and he wants another one. The offered hand is an invitation to play again, not consolation.
- **He doesn't know he's repaying anything.** It mirrors the train, where she offered her hand and he refused it, and the mirroring is hers to feel (DIV-009).

It recurs, each time at a higher price, which is what makes it character rather than a single nice beat:

| Book | What he gives up | For | Refs |
|---|---|---|---|
| B3 | His own victory lap | Going back to her | DIV-069 |
| B5 | Quidditch for life, and a Cup that means nothing to him anyway | Hitting Montague for what he said about her | DIV-067 |
| B7 | The plan, mid-raid | Finding her to apologise | DIV-049 |
| B7 | Going on, at King's Cross | Coming back for her | DIV-048 |

## Instinct, and what it costs *(settled)*

One trait, and both its faces. He is instinctive where she is technical: he duels on reflex and thrives in chaos (DIV-028), and he flies a broom that does exactly what it is asked, including what he didn't mean to ask (DIV-068).

- **It wins him the final.** He takes it on the one move where he stops minding the Firebolt and simply trusts it. The winning improvisation is precisely the one she will not make, and she names it afterwards: uncouth (DIV-069, DIV-070).
- **It maims her.** Sectumsempra is the same reflex — a spell out of a book, reached for in anger, without knowing what it does (DIV-032, DIV-043).

Both are the same man. The file should never treat his instinct as simply a gift or simply a fault, because the series' worst moment and its best flying come out of it.

## What can't be taken back *(settled)* · DIV-072, DIV-073

Planted in B1 and paid in B6. At Clea's landscape he is shown that magic of that order reaches past whoever worked it, outlives them, and cannot be undone — and that the creatures stranded by it can refuse the only help he has to offer. He feels it, he can do nothing, and he is eleven.

He does not become careful. That is the point: knowing a thing and acting on it with his blood up are different, and the gap is his. It comes due with the scars that don't come out and a Mirror that doesn't mend.

Nothing in the text joins the corridor to that night. Full account of the plant in [setting/painted-life.md](../setting/painted-life.md).

## Feelings ahead of understanding *(settled)* · DIV-037

His heart runs well in front of his self-knowledge, and the books use it as evidence.

- **The veela don't touch him** at the 1994 World Cup, because he is already in love. He has no idea. Fleur later uses the same rule to read him, and he can't say what she can see (DIV-038).
- **He can't account for his own happiness** at seeing her again that summer, and doesn't try very hard.
- **The lap-pillow smile.** He writes the scene off as a prank and then keeps returning to the smile and finding it pretty, which confuses him. He won't count it as a happy memory until the lake — and it is the memory that powers the Patronus that saves everyone (DIV-018).

## He doesn't read her *(settled)*

The tsundere engine from his side. Her sincerity is in front of him from B1 — the Potions servitude, the Patronus study, the trophies she has been quietly hoarding since B2 — and he reads almost none of it until the lake (DIV-017, DIV-059, Q-035). After the final the mask goes back on and he is confused and a little hurt, and nothing visibly changes for the rest of the year (Q-082).

The reader is meant to be ahead of him. He is not stupid about her; he has no framework in which a Malfoy means any of it.

## Harsher than canon *(draft)* · Q-024

Angrier and more willing to hurt people than the original, with his Horcrux playing a larger part in it. Visible in the Durmstrang duel, which he wins too hard (DIV-029); in Montague (DIV-067); in going cold when Ron goes through his trunk, and leaving with her without answering the accusation (DIV-063); in forcing his way past Crabbe and Goyle at the hospital wing door; and in a sixth year spent furious. How much is the Horcrux and how much is him is Q-024.

## Daydreaming, forward *(settled)* · DIV-043

He dreams as much as she does. The difference is direction, and it is the whole of why he survives the Mirror and she doesn't: he pictures a future with her, unprompted, in her room in B4, and the Mirror shows him their wedding in B6 while it shows her a dance that already happened. She retreats; he projects.

The warning aimed at him is not Aline's but Renoir's — the man who went into the painting for the best reason there is and lost years anyway (DIV-072). Being forward-facing is not protection on its own. He is told that story at eleven, by her, in the corridor (DIV-073), which makes B6 a choice rather than an escape he stumbles into: he knows exactly what he is standing in. What saves him is that he refuses to go under with her and gets her out instead (DIV-049, DIV-052).

## Magic

- **Duelling.** Instinctive, chaotic, uneven against her precision, and the two of them have read each other for years (DIV-028).
- **Patronus.** Powered by the lap-pillow memory from B3 (DIV-018). See [threads/patronus.md](../threads/patronus.md).
- **Resonance.** Blocked by the Horcrux until the forest; first flicker at the B3 lake (DIV-021, Q-028).
- **Flying.** A wild instrument suits him. He wins when he stops managing it (DIV-068).
- **Sacrifice.** His death in the forest protects nobody (DIV-026).
