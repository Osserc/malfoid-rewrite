# Pansy Parkinson

**Role:** Dracaena's real friend, and the one with the most data and the least appetite for using it.
**Canon baseline:** Pansy Parkinson. Anything not overridden follows canon.
**Changes from canon:** DIV-055, DIV-082, DIV-093, DIV-121, DIV-122, DIV-123, DIV-125

## Core

- **Want:** to be looked at once without being measured.
- **Need:** *(proposal — the author is unconvinced, settle it)* **to stop being the one who keeps the scoreboard.** Nobody has to maintain it for her any more; she runs it on herself when the room is empty. What she needs is to do a thing because she wants to and never check where it places her. *(Rejected: "one thing that is hers" — that is Dracaena's need, word for word (DIV-013). Also rejected: "stop measuring herself against others", which is a negation and is nearly Ron's need.)*
- **Wound / fear:** *Why can't you be more like the Greengrass girl.*
- **Lie she believes:** that she is the lesser article, and that her place is standing next to people who aren't.

## The complex *(draft)* · DIV-122

**She is a pureblood who cannot compete with purebloods.** She has grown up beside Dracaena Malfoy and Daphne Greengrass, who outclass her at manners, at knowledge and at magic without ever intending to, and the comparison is made for her at home before it is ever made at school: *Miss Malfoy can manage a perfect curtsy, are you even trying?* Nobody in her life has ever been cruel about it on purpose. It is worse for being ordinary.

So she has settled, by about eleven, for being Dracaena's friend, and expects nothing else from a school that was never going to rank her first at anything.

**Her safety in her own House is borrowed.** The Slytherin boys look down on her and the only reason they don't make a project of it is that she stands next to a Malfoy. She knows it exactly. It is why she will not go to a Ball on a Slytherin's arm — she would rather not spend an evening being looked sideways at by her own escort (DIV-123) — and it is what makes her refusal of the evacuation in B7 cost something: she has no standing of her own, so turning on her House gives up the only shelter she has ever had (DIV-055).

## Magic *(draft)* · DIV-122

**Power without skill.** She is a pureblood and she has the power that comes with it, which is not nothing ([society](../setting/society.md), DIV-008) — she is simply not very good. She has no feel for form and no patience for drilling it, and everything she casts is slightly heavier than it needs to be.

- **Which is a blow to the ideology, delivered by one of its own.** Hermione is skill without power. Pansy is the inverse, in a family that promised her the opposite. Blood matters in this world and it is demonstrably not everything, and she is the proof that was raised to believe it. DIV-008 gets a second case that costs nothing.
- **It is the same profile as Ron's** — the power and not the finesse (DIV-108) — which is why their ground has to be a game and not a duel ([ron-pansy](../threads/ron-pansy.md)).
- **It is why the Duelling Club is unbearable.** A club is where technique shows and power doesn't help, so Flitwick's piste is precisely the room where her deficiency is on display next to Dracaena's mastery.

## The two clubs *(draft)* · DIV-121

- **B2: she walks out.** Dracaena ropes her into the Duelling Club because that is what Dracaena does. Pansy goes two or three times at most, hates every minute, and tells her she isn't coming back. **She is shaking when she says it** and it costs her everything she has — it is the first preference she has ever stated out loud to anybody.
  - **And it costs Dracaena nothing to receive.** *I can't think of anything more entertaining than watching me humiliate Potter — but you do you, Pansy. I'll tell you all about it later.* She has no use for a maidservant, she never asks what Pansy means to do instead, and **she defuses the whole thing without noticing there was anything to defuse** ([dracaena-malfoy](dracaena-malfoy.md)). Pansy walks away unharmed, having braced for a disaster that was never on the table outside her own chest.
- **B2: she finds the chess club.** Working through the clubs nobody ever puts on the page, she comes across Ron Weasley playing, and winning, and being someone she has never seen in their few brief encounters. She asks whether she can watch. There is nothing romantic in it: she is interested in the game, and in the change in him. Full entry: [ron-pansy](../threads/ron-pansy.md).

## What she does well *(draft)* · DIV-123

**Clothes, and only because nobody ever graded her on it.** She has taste and she uses it herself rather than commissioning it — **not mending and not patching; a pureblood does not patch.** She restyles: strips what is awful, re-cuts, changes what can be changed, and adds her own flair. It is taste applied with a wand, which is consistent with her having no patience for drilling *combat* form while cheerfully knowing the charms she actually likes.

It is the one thing in her life nobody ever held up against anybody, which is exactly why she is good at it — and at the Yule Ball it is the first thing she has ever made that somebody was glad of, and the first time she is praised for anything.

## The jumper *(draft)*

Ron grumbles about the annual Weasley Christmas jumper and **she lights up**: *you get* ***jumpers****? Show me, show me.* She has never been given a thing that wasn't meant to make her look correct in front of somebody — **she has been dressed, not given presents** — so a hand-knitted garment with a letter on it, made by a mother for no occasion but the day, is genuinely exotic to her.

It is also what makes the Ball line safe: *imagine mother's face when she hears we went together* can only read as a prank between friends if the reader already knows the house she stopped writing to. Full entry: [ron-pansy](../threads/ron-pansy.md), Q-159.

## She notices *(settled)* · DIV-093

From B3 she is the one working out that the arithmetic between Harry and Dracaena does not add up, and the one who will not spend what she knows. At the Defence exam Lupin hands her a shaking Dracaena and explains nothing, **and she doesn't ask.** In B4 the Potter Stinks badge that Dracaena doesn't wear is the thread that finally will not go away.

She is not the leak, and nothing is ever allowed to make her one (Q-038). She does not tell Ron either ([ron-pansy](../threads/ron-pansy.md)).

## The DA *(draft)* · DIV-125

She gets in on Ron's word, alone, against the room's objection — **and she gets there before Dracaena does**, which is the only time in her life she is ahead of her. She signs the jinxed parchment knowing her family may one day make her choose, and signs anyway.

What gets her over her distaste for offensive magic is not the cause: it is that she is in danger whichever side her family lands on, and that this time she was **asked** rather than dragged. Same girl, same distaste, opposite answer, and the variable is who did the asking.

## B6 *(draft)*

Dracaena shuts her out with everybody else, somewhere around the poisoning. Pansy finally asks what is wrong — the one thing she has never done — and is refused. Crabbe and Goyle are still there, and they were never friends; by the end of that year they are guarding a hospital wing door for Snape without knowing why (DIV-043).

## Family *(draft)* · Q-159

Canon gives her no family at all: no parents named, no siblings, nothing. So this is open ground.

- **The working version:** she is the youngest of several and comfortably ignored, and the nagging is the only attention she gets, which is worse than silence. An older and more capable sibling means the comparison predates Hogwarts and was waiting for her when she got there.
- **Her B7 defection costs her nothing politically and everything personally**: she confirms what they always said about her.
- She stops writing home early, because she knows better than to expect an answer. Somebody else writes home about the Weasley boy, of course. Q-159.

## Voice

*(draft)* Quieter than canon's. She does not lead and does not perform. She is funny in a dry, late way — the remark that arrives a beat after everyone has moved on — and she is at her sharpest teasing the one person she is not frightened of.

## Appearance *(draft)* · DIV-122

**Canon's "pug face" is cut.** It is gratuitous, it reads as an inbreeding joke at a fourteen-year-old's expense, and we have no use for it. She is plain, which is all the complex needs: unremarkable beside two girls who are not.

She wears her hair in a bob. **She is not made pretty at any point.** There is no makeover arc — her injury is being *ranked*, and the valuable thing Ron offers is that he isn't ranking anybody and isn't trying not to.

## Relationships

- [Dracaena Malfoy](dracaena-malfoy.md): her oldest friend, assigned rather than chosen, and real anyway (DIV-093). She is in her shadow and knows it, and borrows her protection to live in her own House.
- [Ron Weasley](ron-weasley.md): the chess club, and then everything after it ([ron-pansy](../threads/ron-pansy.md), DIV-121).
- Daphne Greengrass: the other one she was measured against, and never a friend. Daphne has no use for her except as an audience.
- [Hermione Granger](hermione-granger.md): objects to her for three years on excellent evidence, and is never argued out of it.
- Crabbe and Goyle: present since childhood, and not friends to anybody.
