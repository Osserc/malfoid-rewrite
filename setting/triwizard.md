# The Triwizard Tournament

Canon by default. What changes is the Goblet: it is a real piece of ancient magic with a function, and that function is the only reason Voldemort's plan has to be a tournament at all (DIV-087).

## The charge *(settled)* · DIV-087

**The Tournament makes something, and the Goblet keeps it.**

Three of the finest young witches and wizards alive spend a year pouring themselves into three lethal Tasks. That expenditure would ordinarily bleed away into the air. The Goblet catches it and holds it, and the hoard grows through the year, Task by Task, reaching its height when the Cup is claimed.

- **Nothing is taken from anybody.** The losing champions go home exactly as they came, minus the bruises. The pot was never theirs to lose: it is what the contest made.
- **The prize is the pot.** The victor absorbs it and keeps it for life. Not Dumbledore — the difference between a good wizard and a great one, a lifetime's practice handed over in an afternoon. Past champions are remembered for what they went on to do, and that is why. The thousand Galleons is the part they print in the programme.
- **Which is why teenagers enter a competition with a death toll.** Not the gold and not the glory. Who would not want to be the greatest wizard of the age?
- **Lethal is the yield.** You cannot harvest a safe tournament. The danger is not tradition dragging its feet; the danger is the instrument. The 1994 committee had to find a way to get the yield without the corpses, which nobody had managed before, and they are answerable for how they went about it.
- **It is awarded raw and takes time to settle.** Until it has settled into the victor it can still be drawn out of them; afterwards it is simply part of them, and taking it would mean taking the wizard apart. That window is measured in minutes, and the whole plan is built to fit inside it.
- **It takes one claimant.** The Goblet will not split a charge and does not recognise a tie. **Claiming is not the same as touching:** the pot goes to whoever claimed the Cup, while the Portkey carries whoever happens to be in contact with it. The two come apart in the maze, and the inch between them decides which of two boys is the spare (DIV-089).

## The contract *(settled)* · DIV-087

The Goblet names champions and binds them. Canon never says what the binding consists of; ours does.

- **A bound champion owes a contribution.** One who competes pays it by competing. One who refuses cannot, so the Goblet takes it out of the body instead — **all of it.** Not a year's worth: a champion who will not commit is stripped of their magic entirely and banished from the wizarding world, no ifs and no buts.
- **It is collection, not punishment.** The Goblet is not angry and is not making an example. It is balancing a ledger, and that is worse.
- **It knows the difference between being stopped and giving up,** and it will not be tricked by a champion who goes through the motions. Which is what the officials are actually for: the judges and the scores exist to witness contribution, not to award points. A champion dragged out of the lake by grindylows has paid. One who walks away has not.
- **It is exactly what it says it is.** No secret, no Dark under-layer, no loophole Dumbledore failed to find. An impartial arbiter with no mercy and no discretion — which is precisely why it will take a fourteen-year-old's magic without blinking, and why it does not care in the slightest that he was entered falsely. Its honesty is the horror. *(Deliberately not built as legend-versus-mechanism: that shape belongs to Lustrum and to* Arceo *(DIV-080, DIV-084), and a third would be a tic.)*

## Why the plan has to be the Tournament *(settled)* · DIV-087

Voldemort's ritual needs an enemy's blood and a victor's charge to burn, **and both have to be in the same body.** The champion supplies both — whoever the champion turns out to be. **The plan is deliberately not all-in on Harry Potter.**

What he wants most is a body back, and the Goblet's hoard is too good an occasion to let pass. **The blood clause is barely a constraint, and canon says so:** blood of the enemy, taken by force, and any wizard alive who would stand against him will serve, which is very nearly all of them. Harry is wanted for one particular reason — he is the way past the protection in his own skin — and that is an upgrade, not a requirement.

- **The Cup is the only possible vector.** The charge peaks when the Cup is claimed and must be taken before it settles, so the plan needs the victor at that instant — and the Cup is the one object in the world guaranteed to be touched by him then. Snatching him off a Hogsmeade street gets Voldemort blood and nothing else.
- **Crouch's orders are one sentence: send me the Goblet's magic.** Make the Cup a Portkey before the maze is sealed, and whoever claims it arrives with the charge still loose in them. **One man can guarantee that.** No man could guarantee a fourteen-year-old winning a tournament, which is precisely why the plan never asks him to.
- **Potter is the bonus.** Entering his name costs the base plan nothing and buys a chance at the premium outcome; teaching him shortens the odds (DIV-086). If he loses, Voldemort rises anyway, out of somebody else. Vain and petty, and still not a man who stakes everything on a boy winning a footrace.
- **Nobody rigs the Tournament,** therefore. Crouch spends the year teaching rather than sabotaging, Fleur and Krum are left their dignity, and **the victory is Harry's.** The trap is that his genuine victory is the key — and that it was never the only key.
- **The charge is fuel, and the ritual burns it.** Voldemort leaves the graveyard with a body and no permanent gain. Harry leaves it stripped of the only prize that would ever have meant anything to him, and keeps the gold he does not want (DIV-062).
- **He is handed it at the moment it cannot save him.** The pot lands in him when the victory resolves: the greatest surge of power he will ever feel, arriving while he is bound to a headstone with a dead boy three feet away and his wand out of reach. Then it is drawn out of him.

## The finish *(settled)* · DIV-089

There is no shared victory, because the Goblet never gets two claimants. The maze is an elimination — Cedric beats Fleur, Harry beats Krum, and Harry and Cedric fight in front of the Cup — and the Task is designed to make them meet, because an instrument for generating contribution naturally funnels four people towards one point.

- **The 1994 committee thought it was the safe option.** No dragons, no drowning, just trained champions and wands, and nobody dies in a duel. The safest Task anyone ever designed for the Tournament is the one that got a champion killed, somewhere else, for reasons none of them could have planned for — and a wand-fight between champions is a judgement somebody made once before (*The roll of champions*, below).
- **Nobody dares yield.** Nobody knows where the Goblet draws the line between being stopped and giving up, and testing it costs everything — all of your magic, and your place in the wizarding world with it. So the champions fight long past the point of sense. That is the floor under the wanting, not a substitute for it: all four of them want the pot, and Dracaena has already told Harry he had better want it too (DIV-089).

## Harry and the contract *(settled)* · DIV-087

- **Dumbledore tells him plainly, in November.** What the contract will do to him if he refuses, and what he stands to win if he does not. There is nothing to soften, so he does not soften it. It makes Dumbledore honest and still culpable: he knowingly sends a fourteen-year-old into a thing that has killed champions, because the alternative is the boy's magic.
- **Why the threat works on Harry in particular.** Losing his magic is not an abstraction to him. It is Privet Drive for good — his standing nightmare — and now it is also never seeing Dracaena again. The Tournament is the one door that stays open.
- **It is also why the adults are helpless,** and that is the clause's real job. Dumbledore can see a trap closing around the boy all year and cannot do the one thing that would spring it, because making him lose is precisely what the Goblet forbids. **And it would not have worked anyway:** the Cup was a Portkey for whoever took it, so a losing Harry only means a different child in the graveyard. Nobody on our side ever learns this; the reader can.
- **The charge never touches the Yule Ball.** It buys him nothing socially. He is the strongest he has ever been — famous, scored, watched, hounded — and none of it reaches the one thing he wants. Sirius plants the idea and Harry asks her terrified, which is the only version worth having (🔒 DIV-004, DIV-019). *Rejected by the author: a version where the charge gives him the confidence to ask. A power-up must never cause the turning point of the series, or June takes it back.*
- **Where it shows instead is his casting.** Spells land harder than he means them to, and the first person to notice is Dracaena, because three years of the Duelling Club have made her the one who knows his magic best (DIV-028, DIV-021, Q-028).

## Watching the Tasks *(settled)* · DIV-088

Canon asks a stadium to watch the surface of a lake and the outside of a hedge. Ours relays both.

### The audience *(settled)* · Q-119

**The spectators are extra eyes, and that is all.** The Goblet has to witness a contribution before it can count it, which is what the judges and the scores are actually for; a crowd is simply a great many more judges, small ones, adding a marginal amount to what the panel already supplies.

- **It is not a battery.** Nothing is drawn out of the spectators, the same rule as the champions, and the contest is never in danger of mattering less than the people watching it. An instrument you could feed by holding a rally would be a different story and a worse one.
- **So the relay is part of the instrument** rather than a patch over canon's unwatchable Tasks (DIV-088).
- **And the 1994 committee has one honest line of defence.** They lowered the lethality and made a little of it back on the gate — which is a defensible thing to have tried and nowhere near enough, since lethal is the yield and they knew it.

## The roll of champions, and the abolition *(settled)* · Q-120

Past victors are remembered for what they did afterwards, never for having won, because the winning is only when they were handed the means. Three names carry it, and they are three answers to the same question.

- **Étienne Bouchard, Beauxbatons.** Took the pot and spent the rest of his life hunting things that had got out of hand, and is remembered for the Beast of Gévaudan — a creature that spent three years killing French peasants in front of Muggle witnesses, under a Statute of Secrecy that was barely seventy years old. The Muggle record calls it a wolf; the wizarding record has never agreed with itself about what it was. He did not merely kill a monster: he closed the worst breach of the Statute's first century. **A champion who used it in service.**
- **Loxias, Durmstrang.** Took the pot and went looking for something better, and found the Elder Wand — which in our lore passes by blood, through killing (DIV-024). The Tournament did not fail to stop him. **It built him**, and handed a lifetime's practice to a man who then wanted the one wand that would top it. Whether he is anywhere in the wand's chain by B7 is open (Q-011). **A champion who used it on everyone.**
- **Serena Passel, Hogwarts.** Took the pot and invented the Stunning Spell, which under our rules is not a charm but a moral technology: **a way to stop a man without wanting to hurt him, and therefore without staining the caster** (DIV-084). Before her, stopping somebody meant harming them, and every duel in Britain left something behind in both parties. She is the single most valuable thing anybody ever did with the prize, and she is what the Tournament's defenders cite for two centuries afterwards. What duelling looked like before her, and what that does to the culture the Duelling Club inherited: **Q-133.** **A champion who used it to change what magic is.**

### Why it stopped

- **The published reason is the death toll,** and the cockatrice is what let them close it — three dead children and a loose monster taking more. Nobody had to argue after that.
- **The real reason is Loxias,** and it could not be said aloud, because saying it means saying what the prize is. A tournament that hands a decisive wizard to one of three nations every five years will eventually hand one to the wrong person, and it did.
- **The escalation was built in.** If danger is the yield, every committee's incentive is to raise it, for ever, with no brake anywhere in the design. That is why the last century of the Tournament produced both its greatest champions and its worst casualty figures: **the golden age and the collapse are the same era and the same cause.**
- **And the 1994 committee inherits the silence along with the Tournament.** They are reviving an instrument whose stated reason for closing was never the real one, which makes their confidence about safety worth exactly what it cost them.

## The charge afterwards *(settled)* · Q-122, dropped

**It leaves no mark on Harry.** The pot never settled into him, so there is nothing missing: no flatness, no sluggish magic, nothing for Madam Pomfrey to find and nothing to convalesce from. B5's Harry is furious about what was done to him, and the book must never be able to put his temper down to his magic.

What survives is knowledge. He is the only person alive who knows what the prize actually was; he held it for about ninety seconds, tied to a headstone; and it was burned as fuel to build a body, on the same errand that killed Cedric Diggory. That is a sentence he can say once, to somebody who does not want to hear it, in a year when nobody believes him about anything.
