# The Triwizard Tournament

Canon by default. What changes is the Goblet: it is a real piece of ancient magic with a function, and that function is the only reason Voldemort's plan has to be a tournament at all (DIV-087).

## The charge *(settled)* · DIV-087

**The Tournament makes something, and the Goblet keeps it.**

Three of the finest young witches and wizards alive spend a year pouring themselves into three lethal Tasks. That expenditure would ordinarily bleed away into the air. The Goblet catches it and holds it, and the hoard grows through the year, Task by Task, reaching its height when the Cup is claimed.

- **Nothing is taken from anybody.** The losing champions go home exactly as they came, minus the bruises. The pot was never theirs to lose: it is what the contest made.
- **The prize is the pot.** The victor absorbs it and keeps it for life. Not Dumbledore — the difference between a good wizard and a great one, a lifetime's practice handed over in an afternoon. Past champions are remembered for what they went on to do, and that is why. The thousand Galleons is the part they print in the programme.
- **Which is why teenagers enter a competition with a death toll.** Not the gold and not the glory. Who would not want to be the greatest wizard of the age?
- **Lethal is the yield.** You cannot harvest a safe tournament. The danger is not tradition dragging its feet; the danger is the instrument. The 1994 committee had to find a way to get the yield without the corpses, which nobody had managed before, and they are answerable for how they went about it.
- **It is awarded raw and takes time to settle.** Until it has settled into the victor it can still be drawn out of them; afterwards it is simply part of them, and taking it would mean taking the wizard apart. That window is measured in minutes, and the whole plan is built to fit inside it.
- **It takes one claimant.** The Goblet will not split a charge and does not recognise a tie. **Claiming is not the same as touching:** the pot goes to whoever claimed the Cup, while the Portkey carries whoever happens to be in contact with it. The two come apart in the maze, and the inch between them decides which of two boys is the spare (DIV-089).

## The contract *(settled)* · DIV-087

The Goblet names champions and binds them. Canon never says what the binding consists of; ours does.

- **A bound champion owes a contribution.** One who competes pays it by competing. One who refuses cannot, so the Goblet takes it out of the body instead — **all of it.** Not a year's worth: a champion who will not commit is stripped of their magic entirely and banished from the wizarding world, no ifs and no buts.
- **It is collection, not punishment.** The Goblet is not angry and is not making an example. It is balancing a ledger, and that is worse.
- **It knows the difference between being stopped and giving up,** and it will not be tricked by a champion who goes through the motions. Which is what the officials are actually for: the judges and the scores exist to witness contribution, not to award points. A champion dragged out of the lake by grindylows has paid. One who walks away has not.
- **It is exactly what it says it is.** No secret, no Dark under-layer, no loophole Dumbledore failed to find. An impartial arbiter with no mercy and no discretion — which is precisely why it will take a fourteen-year-old's magic without blinking, and why it does not care in the slightest that he was entered falsely. Its honesty is the horror. *(Deliberately not built as legend-versus-mechanism: that shape belongs to Lustrum and to* Arceo *(DIV-080, DIV-084), and a third would be a tic.)*

## Why the plan has to be the Tournament *(settled)* · DIV-087

Voldemort's ritual needs the blood of one particular enemy and a victor's charge to burn, **and both have to be in the same body.** So Harry does not merely have to be present. He has to be made a champion, and then made a winner.

- **The Cup is the only possible vector.** The charge peaks when the Cup is claimed and must be taken before it settles, so the plan needs the victor at that instant — and the Cup is the one object in the world guaranteed to be touched by him then. Snatching him off a Hogsmeade street gets Voldemort blood and nothing else.
- **Nobody rigs the Tournament.** Voldemort does not need Harry to win *for* him; he needs Harry to win *at all*, and the cheapest way to buy that is to make the boy formidable. So Crouch spends the year teaching instead of sabotaging (DIV-086), Fleur and Krum are left their dignity, and **the victory is Harry's.** The trap is that his genuine victory is the key.
- **The charge is fuel, and the ritual burns it.** Voldemort leaves the graveyard with a body and no permanent gain. Harry leaves it stripped of the only prize that would ever have meant anything to him, and keeps the gold he does not want (DIV-062).
- **He is handed it at the moment it cannot save him.** The pot lands in him when the victory resolves: the greatest surge of power he will ever feel, arriving while he is bound to a headstone with a dead boy three feet away and his wand out of reach. Then it is drawn out of him.

## The finish *(settled)* · DIV-089

There is no shared victory, because the Goblet never gets two claimants. The maze is an elimination — Cedric beats Fleur, Harry beats Krum, and Harry and Cedric fight in front of the Cup — and the Task is designed to make them meet, because an instrument for generating contribution naturally funnels four people towards one point.

- **The 1994 committee thought it was the safe option.** No dragons, no drowning, just trained champions and wands, and nobody dies in a duel. The safest Task anyone ever designed for the Tournament is the one that got a champion killed, somewhere else, for reasons none of them could have planned for (Q-120).
- **Nobody dares yield.** Nobody knows where the Goblet draws the line between being stopped and giving up, and testing it costs everything — all of your magic, and your place in the wizarding world with it. So the champions fight long past the point of sense. That is the floor under the wanting, not a substitute for it: all four of them want the pot, and Dracaena has already told Harry he had better want it too (DIV-089).

## Harry and the contract *(settled)* · DIV-087

- **Dumbledore tells him plainly, in November.** What the contract will do to him if he refuses, and what he stands to win if he does not. There is nothing to soften, so he does not soften it. It makes Dumbledore honest and still culpable: he knowingly sends a fourteen-year-old into a thing that has killed champions, because the alternative is the boy's magic.
- **Why the threat works on Harry in particular.** Losing his magic is not an abstraction to him. It is Privet Drive for good — his standing nightmare — and now it is also never seeing Dracaena again. The Tournament is the one door that stays open.
- **It is also why the adults are helpless,** and that is the clause's real job. Dumbledore can see a trap closing around the boy all year and cannot do the one thing that would spring it, because making him lose is precisely what the Goblet forbids.
- **The charge never touches the Yule Ball.** It buys him nothing socially. He is the strongest he has ever been — famous, scored, watched, hounded — and none of it reaches the one thing he wants. Sirius plants the idea and Harry asks her terrified, which is the only version worth having (🔒 DIV-004, DIV-019). *Rejected by the author: a version where the charge gives him the confidence to ask. A power-up must never cause the turning point of the series, or June takes it back.*
- **Where it shows instead is his casting.** Spells land harder than he means them to, and the first person to notice is Dracaena, because three years of the Duelling Club have made her the one who knows his magic best (DIV-028, DIV-021, Q-028).

## Watching the Tasks *(settled)* · DIV-088

Canon asks a stadium to watch the surface of a lake and the outside of a hedge. Ours relays both. Whether the audience is part of the instrument: Q-119.
