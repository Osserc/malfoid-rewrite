# Magic

Canon by default. This file records where our magic works differently, and the rules that follow from it.

## Spellcasting: radicals 🔒 (principle) · DIV-007

Spellcasting is technical. Wand motions draw "radicals" that combine into spells.

Design still open (Q-015): which radicals exist, how they combine, what incantations add, how nonverbal and wandless casting work, and what makes a spell hard.

## Joining and separating *(direction)* · DIV-113

**The system is Empedoclean in shape.** Behind the radicals there is something that **joins what is separate** and something that **separates what is joined**. They are directions, not moral grades, and neither is on anybody's side.

**They have no names** *(settled)*. Empedocles is where the shape comes from, and *Love* and *Strife* are convenience only — never on the page, and not in the bible's final form either. What matters is what happens.

**And they may not be forces at all.** The honest version may be that there is no second thing in the world doing this, and that joining and separating are simply **how magic answers intent** — a way of categorising its reactions rather than an ontology. Deciding that is deciding how magic works at all (Q-015), so nothing below commits to the stronger claim.

Design open with the rest of the system (Q-015). What the frame already decides:

- **Dark magic is the separating force channelled.** That is *Dark magic runs on intent* (DIV-084) restated at the level it operates on: intent to harm is not a category the universe is marking, it is an alignment. **And what you channel does to you what it does to everything**, which answers Q-027 by definition — the Dark Arts crack the soul because separating things is the whole function of what you are drawing on, and you are a thing.
- **A Horcrux is the endpoint**, not an exotic ritual: that force finally applied to yourself, on purpose.
- **The Killing Curse is it concentrated to a point** and projected whole, which is also why it cannot hold together at distance (DIV-107).
- **Resonance is the inverse, and this is the keystone.** **A Horcrux is one person divided; resonance is two people joined** (DIV-021). The same physics, run in opposite directions — so Voldemort's project and Harry and Dracaena's are mechanical mirror images, and the joining force is a term in the equation rather than a sentiment bolted onto the ending (🔒 DIV-006). It is also why *only love, and friendship is not enough* stops being arbitrary.
- **The stolen edge follows** (DIV-021): she reaches for union and touches a fragment whose entire nature is division, so of course it hurts her.
- **Nobody in the story knows any of this.** The cast has folk theories — brother wands, sacrifice, prophecy, righteous fire — and they are all wrong. The reader gets the physics; the characters get superstition. **The house pattern holds** (DIV-080): the legend and the mechanism are two different things, and no character ever delivers the correct metaphysics as a lecture.
- **Neither force is a reward.** Lustrum's rule is the model and it is not negotiable: it makes no judgement of anybody, it counts spells. **That it is worth doing things right is what the book argues, not how the world works** ([themes](../core/themes.md)).

## Dementors, and Lustrum *(settled)* · DIV-080

Canon gives no way to destroy a Dementor: a Patronus drives them off and that is the limit. Ours can be killed.

### Lustrum

A cousin of Fiendfyre. The incantation is *Lustra nefas* — "purify the sacrilege" — and the name is the Roman censor's rite, the purification performed over the citizenry at the close of every census. McGonagall's signature spell in the first war, and the reason the other side called her Hellfire ([her file](../characters/minerva-mcgonagall.md)).

**The legend and the mechanism are two different things, and that gap is the whole design.** What everyone believes — including most of the few who have ever cast it — is that Lustrum is righteous fire: it weighs a person, burns what they owe, and spares the innocent. What it actually does is scorch everything Dark-touched inside the burn, in proportion, and nothing else. It makes no judgement of anybody. **It counts spells.**

- **What it burns is Dark magic present in a person, however it got there.** The Dark Arts crack the soul and let ambient magic in (Q-027), and that is what catches. Working it is the commonest route in and not the rule: a curse someone else put on you leaves the same substance behind, which is why a wound dealt by Dark magic never properly heals. Not deeds, not character, not what anyone deserves.
- **So it cannot tell a perpetrator from a victim.** It cannot tell a torturer from a man who hexed his neighbour either; it can only tell how much. An Auror who spent a career fighting Dark wizards and wears what they did to him burns hotter than a minister who has never been hit by anything in his life. **The war leaves marks on the good ones too, and the fire reads the marks.**
- **The stain is on the casting, and the spell's design decides how easy the question is.** Dark magic runs on intent (DIV-084), so a spell built only to afflict — Furnunculus, the slug-vomiting curse, Locomotor Mortis — cannot be cast any other way, and casting it at all leaves something behind. A spell with uses that are not harm — Expelliarmus, Stupefy, Protego, Petrificus Totalus, Impedimenta — can be cast clean, though it need not be. There is no fixed register of Dark spells; there is only what the caster wanted, and most spells make that obvious.
- **So nobody is clean, and small children are.** Every adult in Britain has thrown something in a temper, and so has every student above first year, which is why an ordinary person inside the burn is scorched rather than spared. A small child has had no opportunity and has had nothing done to them — not an innocent nature — and the fire finds nothing in one at all. Grass, trees and stone are untouched for the same reason, which is why the fire does not spread and nothing ordinary catches.
- **The wounded are the most flammable people on a battlefield.** Cursed scars, a Dark Mark, a fragment of somebody else's soul: all of it catches, and none of it was the carrier's doing. There is no casual use of Lustrum anywhere near casualties, in any book.
- **Dementors are nearly all of that substance,** which is why they go up like tinder, and why a powerful enough casting melts them to cinders.
- **It leaves the Dark behind.** The fire runs on what it finds and destroys the person, so what remains of anyone burned is the worst of what they ever cast, concentrated, and it scalds whatever touches it for months. Lustrum absolves nobody and settles nothing. It inflicts a punishment, which is what the ancients meant by keeping it for a last resort.
- **The caster has to be flammable too.** The fire needs something near at hand to catch on and takes it out of whoever cast it, on the spot. There is no way to cast it clean, and McGonagall's contribution is only that she stopped resenting the price and called it penance.
- **Which leaves a window, and that is the safety catch.** **Too clean and it will not light: anyone who has never worked Dark magic cannot cast Lustrum at all,** so the admission ticket is having already crossed the line. **Too dirty and it kills you,** because the nearest fuel is the caster. The spell is castable only from a narrow band in the middle, and nobody designed that in as a sermon — it simply falls out of how the fire works.
  - **The other side cannot use it.** Voldemort would be the pyre. So would Bellatrix, or Macnair, or any Death Eater worth the name. We never have to explain why they don't reach for the best area weapon in the country.
  - **And it is a clock on whoever can.** Lustrum is itself Dark, so every casting deposits more and walks the caster towards the far edge of the window. No amount of casting ever empties anyone out; it only ever fills them. The sole warning is that the backlash keeps growing.
- **The wounds are handprints.** Burns in the shape of a grip, as though a flaming hand had taken hold: a large one across the torso, smaller ones at the wrists and forearms. Enchanted dragonhide buys the first seconds and no more, which is why her jerkin is survivable and her arms are not.
- **It cannot overcome magic stronger than the caster's.** The gate is about power invested in a single piece of work, not about category. An Inferius is cheap per unit and burns readily. A Horcrux or a great Dark artefact is not: the fire catches on it perfectly well and cannot consume it, so it burns the vessel and leaves the thing inside. **You would get the man and not the thing in him.** Nobody on our side outranks Voldemort, so no Horcrux of his can be burned out of anything — while his Inferi are stopped by the fact that she cannot use the spell in a room full of her own people, which is a different problem (DIV-025).
- **It is a siege weapon, and an offensive one.** Slow, wide, telegraphed, and capable of incapacitating the caster; useless against anyone who can move or shield. It is something you drop on a position you do not occupy — which is why the war record is raids on houses and gatherings, and why a defender can almost never use it at all.
- **It does not pick sides.** Everything inside the burn is scorched, allies included, and in a war everybody's hands are dirty, so her own squad is fuel. In a corridor, in a crowd, in a battle fought among your own students, that is the reason the spell stays in its sheath.
- **Nobody knows what it is.** Not in the open stacks: Hermione finds nothing in B3, and gets at it in B4 through the Restricted Section, on a slip from Flitwick (Q-112). The vicewick she has to find for herself, elsewhere and sideways, because the few who know one was used are not telling. The Ministry regulates the incantation, which is how Fudge can call its use unauthorised.

### Where it comes from

**The legend.** Appius Februus, a Roman censor, more than two thousand years ago. The city he watched over fell to the Dark, so he cleansed it with his own hands — offering his own soul as the kindling and burning the whole citizenry along with himself. The first Lustrum left cinders everywhere and a city fit to be lived in again.

**What actually happened.** The spell is older than Rome: an Etruscan working used by haruspices, which the Romans took and dressed in their own togas. Appius was real and committed no genocide. He burned a few dozen people and scarred a good many more — and he cast so often, in so short a span, that he walked himself out of the window. Each casting had deposited more in him than the last until he was too Dark to survive his own fire, and the records, which say he nearly lost his magic, have the shape of it and not the cause. He spent the rest of his days in obscurity. They also have him writing out his own *nota censoria* afterwards — the censor's mark against a citizen's name, entered on one man's judgement and not appealable — and setting it against himself, because reaching for Lustrum at all meant he had already failed at the job he actually held.

- **The office is the argument.** A censor's defining power was to degrade a citizen on his own judgement with no appeal, and the lustrum itself was a scheduled civic purification performed every five years. The spell is named for the one magistracy in history whose entire business was unappealable verdicts on character — and it cannot deliver one.
- **The cost was never a defect, and nobody has bred it out.** The ancients held that resorting to Lustrum meant something had already gone wrong and that it ought to stain whoever used it. It also happens to be how the thing works. Moderns have been trying to file the conscience off ever since, and the best anyone has managed is the vicewick.
- **The *nota censoria* was accurate bookkeeping.** By casting as much as he had, Appius had made himself one of the people the spell is for, and he was the magistrate whose office it was to write that down. The origin story and the mechanism are the same story.
- **How much of this reaches the page, and when:** Q-112.

### The vicewick

The small sun over the castle in B3 is not the spell alone. It takes an **enhancer**: an artificial fuel source, worn on the person, which offers the fire a nearer and richer meal than the caster is. It does not make Lustrum safe. It misdirects it — the caster still pays, and pays less.

- **It is made of rendered ash.** What Lustrum leaves of the things it has burned is the one substance in the world that is nothing but Dark magic, so it is the only thing that will serve. An enhancer can therefore only exist downstream of a casting, and almost nobody alive can cast. That, rather than any regulation, is why there are no stockpiles. Dementor ash is the premium grade, being very nearly pure.
- **The name is propaganda, like the spell's.** "Vicewick" says the thing holds a man's vices, which flatters the legend. What it holds is distilled ash.
- **Flitwick is not a Dark wizard for making one.** He never practises Dark magic. He concentrates a residue and builds a vessel that presents it to somebody else's fire first — charms work, a sleight of hand rather than a corruption. His own verdict on it is that they have tamed the torture weapon. Sort of.
- **Without one the spell kills a handful at a time** — a houseful, which is exactly the shape of McGonagall's war record. Hers is the first she has ever had, and what it is made of is hers: see [her file](../characters/minerva-mcgonagall.md).
- **One is one.** McGonagall spends hers entirely at the siege and chooses not to make a second, so by B7 she casts raw (Q-113).
- **Open:** whether remorse can mend what the Dark Arts crack, which canon allows and we have not decided (Q-110).

### The Dementors

- **They can be destroyed,** and the Ministry knows it. They are a dangerous magical creature, not a fixture of the universe: huntable, the way wizards hunted the British giants to nothing. Lustrum is McGonagall's method and the one the books show, not the only one there is. The Ministry keeps them anyway, because they are useful ([azkaban](../problems/azkaban.md)).
- **They can be hurt.** A strong enough Patronus does real damage, and a wounded Dementor is a starving one. That is what turns Harry's Patronus at the lake into a siege (DIV-081) — and nothing is restraining them when it happens, because *Arceo* is not on them and never was.
- **They are not one thing** *(settled)* · DIV-083. They have factions, and the first war split them. Canon gestures at Dementors feeling fury and talking among themselves and never follows it up; we do.
  - **Some went over to Voldemort and some did not.** Azkaban kept running on a reduced contingent, which is why the prison needs no explaining away, and his side gained a real force. It also removes canon's worst piece of timing.
  - **The ones who stayed were never loyal.** Azkaban's despair is a particular vintage — concentrated, renewable, served daily — and they had acquired a taste for it. They are gourmands, which is worse than traitors, and it is the heaviest single charge against the place: the things that guard it prefer it.
  - **The defectors** were hunted. McGonagall and others like her destroyed a great many, and the remnants were put back in Azkaban, because they are an asset.
### Arceo *(settled)* · DIV-084

One spell, one incantation, and no second version. ***Arceo*** — first person singular, like *Accio* and *Protego* — means *to ward off, to keep at a distance*, and equally *to shut in, to enclose*. One verb for both halves of its life, which is the whole design: what it does is decided by what the caster wants while casting it (*Dark Magic runs on intent*, below).

- **It was made as a defence.** A Malfoy ancestor's charm for getting under a Dementor's skin so it wants to be elsewhere — pushing them off a house, off a road, off a child. Clean magic. Macnair's discovery, generations later, was only that wanting the opposite makes it say *come here*.
- **It is upkeep, not ownership.** A continuous influence and nothing more. There is no casting it once and keeping the creature; stop, and it leaves when it likes.
  - **A place can be warded permanently**, because the enchantment sits in the stone. That is how Azkaban's human guards survive, how Ministry buildings stay habitable, and what the B3 garrison was supposed to be held off the students with. This is the ordinary, sanctioned, openly used half.
  - **A creature cannot be owned.** Commanding has to be sustained, by a caster, for as long as it is wanted.
- **So the Dementors are not under anyone's control.** They stay because the terms are good. Macnair and the Death Eaters treated them well, and they learned that taking orders feeds them better than wandering does. They would leave for a better offer, and in time they get one. Nobody at the Ministry has understood this in fourteen years.
- **Naming.** The registers say *the Arceo Charm*; in departmental use it is just warding. **The commanding use has no name**, because naming it would be admitting to it — so there is nothing written down anywhere, which is exactly why it keeps being rediscovered (DIV-085). "The leash" and "the collar" are bible shorthand and must never reach the page.

Who traded it, and the consequences: [azkaban](../problems/azkaban.md), DIV-084.

## Dark Magic runs on intent *(draft)* · DIV-084

Canon's own rule, never stated as one: the curse that tortures does not work on righteous anger, only on genuinely wanting to cause pain. We make it general. **Dark Magic is powered by what the caster wants, not by what the caster knows.** Technique helps; appetite is the requirement. *(What it is underneath: Strife channelled — DIV-113.)*

- **It explains Macnair.** A brute with no scholarship takes a defensive spell and makes it a yoke, because the only qualification was wanting it that way. It is also why nobody decent ever found the trick: you have to want it first, and most people never do.
- **It explains Umbridge** doing the same thing twelve years later with no more training than he had (DIV-085).
- **It is not a licence.** Power still runs in lines ([society](society.md)) and the Dark Arts still crack the seal and let ambient magic in, at a price (Q-027). Intent decides whether dark magic *takes*, not how strong you are.
- **It decides what Lustrum can see.** The fire burns Dark magic present in a person, so the intent rule is what sorts a curse from a martial spell at the moment of casting — and what makes almost everybody flammable, since a curse you took leaves the same residue as one you threw (*Lustrum*, above).

## The Killing Curse *(settled)* · DIV-107

**It is not a spell the caster throws. It is the caster, thrown.** Avada Kedavra projects the caster's entire magic out of the body, which then has to come back. Every other property follows from that.

- **Nothing stops it, because it is not scaled like a spell.** Shields are built against castings; this is a whole person arriving at once. It goes through Protego and through any ward ever devised, and it does not need to strike anywhere in particular — it envelops rather than hits.
- **No travel time inside its reach, and nothing whatever outside it.** Wand, word, green light, and the man drops. Past its range the projection cannot hold itself together and dissipates entirely: no falloff, no wounding at the edge, nothing arrives. **Duelling range, not battlefield range** (Q-143).
- **It needs an unobstructed path through matter, not an unobstructed view.** Seeing is targeting; matter is the wall. A pane of conjured glass stops it dead and still lets the caster watch you through it. **The pinnacle of the Dark Arts goes through every shield ever devised and is stopped by a plank** — alien, not weak.
- **It is a wave with a small field, not a point.** It pushes forward and spreads a little, which is why it only has to reach you *somewhere* and why it has never cared about clothing. **So it does not see thin material at all** — cloth, paper, a leaf. **Stopping it takes mass**, and some materials take the load better than others. **Nobody survives this by wrapping up in a blanket**: the defence is a Transfiguration problem, not a prop (DIV-108).
- **So the enemy has to close.** That is the staging of every confrontation with Voldemort in seven books: a man walking towards you through the spells being thrown at him, because the only way to use the best thing he has is to be near. He cannot snipe and never could.
- **The caster is empty while it is out.** No magic to cast with and none to defend with, for as long as the projection takes to go and return, longer the further it travels. Casting it means voluntarily being nothing for a moment, and almost everyone experiences that as a small death. **That, and not conscience, is why nobody spams it.**
- **Which makes it an ambush weapon or a group weapon, never a solo one.** Somebody has to hold the line while you are hollow. It is one of the reasons Voldemort keeps followers — and the one time he went alone, it ruined him (DIV-110).
- **He is the fastest and surest caster of it alive, and the aptitude is the damage.** A man already in pieces is long used to not being whole, so the hollow moment does not frighten him as it frightens everybody else. Inside his reach, anyone who drops their guard is finished. **His being best at it and his losing are the same fact.**
- **There is no clean casting, ever, by anyone.** The spell has no second use, so under DIV-084 there is no other way to cast it: you launder your whole being through malice and not all of it comes back. **One casting marks a person permanently** — the heaviest single residue there is, and what makes any Death Eater worth the name a pyre under Lustrum (DIV-080).
- **And it cannot be cast in a flinch.** The longest, least forgiving radical string in the Dark Arts (DIV-007), so it is a decision held visibly for seconds in front of whoever is watching. **Nobody has ever killed with it by accident.**

### The graveyard, and the red herring *(settled)* · DIV-111

Canon locks the wands because they share a core and calls it Priori Incantatem. **Ours has nothing to do with the wands.** Voldemort casts the foulest spell in existence at a piece of himself — the most mutilated soul there is — lodged inside a boy who at that moment can think of nothing but the girl he will not see again. It is Godric's Hollow's little brother, with the same cause: **he is firing his whole self into his own worst work.**

- **What actually happens.** Harry does not know what he is, so the fragment is not being used — **it is lashing out blindly at being fired on**, and the recoil runs back along both wands. **Two pieces of the same soul repelling each other.** That one event locks the wands, forces the procession out, and stops the curse.
- **The twin cores are the conduit, not the cause.** The connection is visibly between the two wands and the shared core is real, so the wrong answer is irresistible and nobody checks.
- **It is the stain that holds, not the love.** Harry holds on to the wand for her; what actually resists is the fragment. **That ordering is load-bearing** — if love beats the Killing Curse here, the locked ending is a repeat with better lighting (🔒 DIV-006). In B7 the fragment is gone, only the holding-on is left, and it is enough because there are two of them.
- **The twin cores stay in the story as the wrong answer.** They are real, Ollivander says so in B1, and the explanation is authoritative and entirely mistaken. **Voldemort blames the instrument** — which is exactly what canon's Voldemort does, so his wand-swapping and the Elder Wand hunt survive as his error rather than as machinery (DIV-024). He spends two books chasing the wrong variable.
- **Dumbledore believes it too.** The darkest wizard of the age and the greatest misdiagnose the same event the same way, because neither can conceive that the answer is what Voldemort did to himself.
- **And he never learns Harry is a Horcrux.** Not in B5, not in B7, not at the end. **So the forest is the same mistake twice**: he destroys his own Horcrux by accident, for the second time, for the same reason — because he insisted on doing it personally.
- **Prior Incantato is untouched.** The forensic charm that reads a wand's last castings is a different thing sharing a name in canon, and we keep it (DIV-086, Q-114).
- What the procession shows, and whose shades come out: **Q-145**.

### The forest *(settled)* · DIV-114

**The graveyard again, on purpose.** By B7 Harry knows what he is, and he takes hold of the fragment and holds it in front of himself — not physically, functionally — forcing it to stay where it is and take the curse.

- **How he does it is open** (Q-147). *Lean: will, not a spell* — canon already runs on will for throwing off the Imperius and for Occlumency, and a named spell would make it teachable, which it must not be.
- **It is not safe.** He has to get a grip on a piece of Voldemort's soul that does not want to be held, while being fired on, and he is betting on a theory. Good odds, not a sure thing.
- **The connection keeps the aftermath.** He is joined to the thing that takes the curse, so he goes down with it — unconscious, and at King's Cross (DIV-048, Q-061).
- **Voldemort is doing him the favour**, and never knows it (DIV-111). **Harry's death still protects nobody** (DIV-026).

## Interposition *(settled)* · DIV-108

**You move, and you use cover.** Matter answers what shields cannot, the Killing Curse included. An advanced technique, not a reflex anyone can reach for.

- **It takes real Transfiguration, at speed, under fire, in the right place.** **Mass is the requirement** — the curse ignores cloth, paper or a leaf and needs heft to stop (DIV-107) — and some materials take the load better than others.
- **So the ceiling is Transfiguration.** It is what finally makes Harry apply himself to the subject, and what puts something behind McGonagall's B6 advice that Aurors use it.
- **It is not an upgrade to Protego and does not compete with it.** A shield is faster, cheaper and better against everything a shield works on, and **if you know the answer a counter or a shield is the stronger move** — you can afford not to defend, and keep the other man answering you. Interposition is for the things with no answer, and for when you do not know it.
- **Its limits are real.** Fire that snakes around cover does exactly that: you trap it with geometry, which is a very high ceiling, or you move and accept being singed.
- **It costs almost no power, so it is technique rather than strength** — which is why Hermione can do it, and why it is the first fighting skill in the series she is built for.
- **How Moody arrives at it and how he teaches it:** [alastor-moody](../characters/alastor-moody.md), DIV-108. **Where it lands on the page:** Q-144. **Mobility, the other half, is not designed:** Q-149.


## Love as magic

🔒 The payoff (DIV-006): in B7, love rebounds the Killing Curse.

### Sacrifice *(settled)* · DIV-026

Harry's death in the forest protects no one. Unlike canon, it doesn't stop Voldemort's spells from taking hold on the defenders. It opens the way to Voldemort's destruction, but everyone still has to play their part (see [themes](../core/themes.md)).

Why her sacrifice worked and his doesn't is no longer a question about sacrifice at all: see *Godric's Hollow* below (DIV-110). How resonance relates to either is still open: Q-014.

### Godric's Hollow *(settled)* · DIV-110

**A configuration, not a reward.** Nothing judged anybody and nobody was protected for being good. Three quantities met at one point and the result was a reaction.

- **The filthiest caster alive**, projecting his entire self (DIV-107), carrying more accumulated Strife than anyone in Britain and a soul already deliberately broken into pieces.
- **A perfect null.** A small child has worked no magic and has had none worked on it — not an innocent nature, simply nothing there. **Our magic already treats this as a quantity:** it is why Lustrum finds nothing at all in a small child (DIV-080), and it is what the old stories about unicorns were always describing.
- **A body in the path.** Not a shield and not a bargain: the projection has to pass through whatever is between, and she was between.
- **The reaction maimed him and handed part of him to the boy.** His whole magic was outside his body and in contact with Harry when it went wrong. **A piece staying behind is not an accident that needed arranging; it would have been remarkable if none had.** This is where Harry's Horcrux comes from.
- **Every mother alive could have done what Lily did.** None ever got the chance, because Voldemort does not do his own killing — and a follower is merely a murderer, not a lifetime's sediment of Dark magic with a soul in pieces. **The one time he walked up to a small child personally, this happened.** Nobody is insulted and nothing is unrepeatable in principle.
- **It is not what happens in B7.** Godric's Hollow is a chemical accident; the final duel is two people doing something on purpose (🔒 DIV-006), and the locked ending stays singular because of it.
- **Why he was in the room at all:** the prophecy, which did that one thing and nothing else (DIV-112).

### Resonance *(settled)* · DIV-021

A wand resonates when two people wield it in total sync, and their magic becomes exponentially stronger. It's literally the power of love.

- **Only love.** Friendship isn't enough, or resonance would be more common. Harry and Hermione could never do it. It's obscure, extremely rare and extremely hard to pull off.
- **Sync.** Both draw the same radicals at the same instant, with no leader. That's almost impossible between two people. Harry and Dracaena can do it because of years of feuding and duelling, spent learning each other's every move, much of it at the Duelling Club (DIV-028).
- **Volatile.** No instant win. The state shows cracks, but it holds.
- **Occlumency.** In resonance, Dracaena wraps Harry's mind in hers. Voldemort's Legilimency finds nothing but her love for Harry.
- **The stained soul.** Until the forest, Harry's Horcrux blocks it. They come close a few times, and each time she's hurt by the stained, mutilated soul she senses in him, without either of them knowing what it is. Full resonance only becomes possible once the Killing Curse in the forest destroys that fragment.
- *(draft)* **The stolen edge.** By her line, Dracaena is the more powerful ([society](society.md)). Harry evens it with instinct (DIV-028) and with the piece of Voldemort in him, which gives him an edge (how: Q-027). The irony: part of his power comes from his enemy, and it's the same piece that hurts her in the near-misses. In the forest he loses it. Alone he's weaker, but full resonance opens, and together they come out more powerful than ever.
- **How it builds** across the books is kept vague until a dedicated session (Q-028). So far: a first flicker at the B3 lake (DIV-018), and one wand in two hands in the final duel (🔒 DIV-006).
- **Nobody knows.** In school no one knows what resonance is. Dracaena works out the rule herself, by research, and tells no one.
- **Never disarmed.** When they're together they're each other's strength. Harry doesn't need a wand of his own, because hers is theirs.
- *(draft)* **The link.** Drawing the same radicals with no leader opens a link between the two minds. The link is what multiplies their power, what lets her extend her Occlumency over him, what brings her into contact with the stained soul, and what can crack.

Where the near-misses happen across B1–B6, and how she finds the rule: Q-028.

### The veela charm *(settled)* · DIV-037

A veela's charm has no hold on someone who's already in love. Canon never says either way.

- **Harry** is immune by the 1994 World Cup. Fresh off the B3 lake, where she saved his godfather, he sees Dracaena again and can't explain to himself why he's happy to. He's in love before he knows it ([harry-dracaena](../threads/harry-dracaena.md)).
- **Who knows.** It's veela lore. Fleur knows it, and so does Dracaena. Harry doesn't.
- **Dracaena notices** at the World Cup that he's unmoved, so she knows he's in love. She has no way of knowing it's with her, and assumes it's Hermione, who is sitting with him and who has never been anywhere else (DIV-041).
- **The married men** in the Top Box: Arthur Weasley and Lucius Malfoy are unmoved too. Who else notices: Q-050.
- **Fleur's test.** In B4 she turns her charm on Harry to find out whether his heart is taken (DIV-038).
- **Separate from resonance.** It takes no wand and no sync: it's a limit of the charm, not something two people do.

### Harry's dance *(settled)* · DIV-054

From B7, Harry Apparates Dracaena by dancing with her. She can't Apparate ([apparate-dancing](../scenes/b7/apparate-dancing.md)).

- **Side-Along** is canon: anyone who can Apparate can take along someone who can't, hauled along, squeezed, with a crack.
- **The turn.** Apparition is a turn on the spot (canon), and a waltz is a chain of turns. Each turn of the dance is the Apparition turn. Neither of them is dragged: it feels like a pull, a slide across the floor, not a squeeze.
- **The wish.** What drives it is that he wants to go somewhere *with her*: "I wished we could go somewhere together." That's why it only works with her.
- **The sound.** A *swoosh*, not canon's crack: the tell that his technique is different. On his own, Harry still cracks.
- **No wand.** Apparition takes none, so his hands are free for her.
- **A feat.** Chaining Apparitions on the beat, both of them, without a Splinch, is very hard. Ron and Hermione doubt many wizards alive could do it.
- **Not resonance.** It's all in Harry's mind: he casts alone and leads. There's no shared wand and no link between them, so the stained soul never comes into it and it doesn't hurt her (see *Resonance* above).

## Brooms amplify the rider *(settled)* · DIV-068

Canon treats brooms as a ladder: a better broom is faster and better at everything, and the best broom in the world is simply the best. Ours ties power to obedience, so a broom transmits its rider instead of carrying them.

- **Cheap brooms are forgiving.** They're stable, placid and slow, and they quietly ignore half of what they're told, which is exactly what makes them safe to learn on.
- **Top brooms are responsive to the point of wildness.** They do precisely what they're asked, including what the rider didn't mean to ask. Power and obedience arrive together, and there's no version that is fast and docile.
- **Assistance and obedience are different things,** and the two great lines sit at opposite ends of it. A Nimbus is *opinionated*: its flying charm manages the small corrections — angling into a turn, holding a line through a gust — so the rider can spend attention on scouting and duelling instead, and it will quietly decline an instruction it reads as a mistake. A Firebolt does nothing for you and refuses nothing. It simply obeys, stray thoughts included.
- **So the better broom costs more to fly.** A Firebolt is a standing tax on attention: everything you think, the broom does. What you buy with that is that nothing is off the table, including things the charm on a Nimbus would have talked you out of.
- **So money buys amplification, not performance.** A great broom magnifies whatever the rider already is, in both directions. A rich mediocrity is worse off on a Firebolt than on a Cleansweep, and knows it.
- **Temperament, not just skill.** A wild broom suits Harry, who flies the way he duels, on instinct and improvisation (DIV-028). It's the wrong instrument for Dracaena, who is precise and controlled. She could buy a Firebolt tomorrow and be slower on it than on her Nimbus, and she knows it: a broom that manages the details for her is exactly what a cerebral flier wants, and she is the better technician of the two.
- **What it looks like on the page.** The B3 final runs the whole rule twice (DIV-069). Harry spends the match minding his Firebolt and is nearly spent by the end, while Dracaena never fights hers for a second. He catches a falling hair tie because the broom gives him the reach he never asked for out loud, and he wins the Cup riding it upside down, which is the same trick at the scale that matters. The only thing it costs her is that she would never have tried it.
- **The gap at the ceiling is small.** A Firebolt and a Nimbus 2001 are both top racing brooms, and the Firebolt's margin only shows at the very edge of what either can do — a place almost nobody flies. Reaching that edge is the rider's doing.
- **It explains canon.** Lucius buys seven top-of-the-line brooms and his team loses to Gryffindor on older ones anyway, repeatedly, Dracaena included (B2). Canon plays it as a joke about money not buying skill. We make it the rule, and the reader has watched it work twice before the B3 final.
- **Why it matters.** Quidditch stops being decided by chequebooks without anyone having to regulate equipment, so canon's Firebolt subplot survives intact, and the B3 final is hers to lose (DIV-069).

## Sloughsilk *(draft)* · DIV-064

The Lullasp is a snake said to straddle the waking and the dreaming realms. Its sloughed skin, sloughsilk, has peculiar magical properties, the useful one being that it takes growing charms extraordinarily well: a sloughsilk garment adapts to its wearer as they sleep and grows with them, so a set made for a newborn still fits at fifteen.

- **A Black tradition.** A sloughsilk set for a new baby is a Black family custom. Narcissa had one made for Dracaena — pyjamas, a snake-headed hood, and snake slippers, which are not traditional at all. Sirius had one as a child, which Harry can ask him about.
- **It does nothing for sleep.** The dreaming-realm lore is where the creature's name comes from, not a property of the cloth. Harry's nightmares are not solved by what anyone is wearing (DIV-061).

## Legilimency and Occlumency *(settled)* · DIV-020

- **Not mind reading.** Legilimency perceives thoughts, feelings and memories (canon). What it perceives can be shaped.
- **Passive Legilimency.** Voldemort can sense the people in front of him without casting with a wand. With his own followers he usually has no reason to do more.
- **Tweaking.** A skilled Occlumens can tweak what passive Legilimency perceives. Lying outright to Voldemort's face takes far more: Snape can, Lucius can't. Voldemort's arrogance helps both of them.
- **The Malfoys.** No one is born an Occlumens, but the discipline the Malfoys instil lends itself to learning it.
- **Dracaena** learns it from Snape in summer 1995. Against Voldemort in summer 1996 her walls break with ease (DIV-015). In the final duel she doesn't wall him out: she wraps Harry's mind in hers (DIV-021).

## Riding an Imperius *(settled)* · DIV-086

Canon's Imperius Curse is unchanged: compliance, contentment, and no reliable way for anyone to detect it from outside. What we add is one refinement, invented by Voldemort and given to exactly one follower.

- **A caster can wear the cursed, not only steer them.** It is possession — his own speciality, the diary and Nagini and what he does to Harry in B5 — fitted onto somebody else's Imperius, which serves as the road. The curse is Crouch's; the technique is the master's; nothing else was supplied.
- **Holding and wearing are the same grip.** The caster can hold the cursed down or wear them, not both at full strength, and wearing always loosens the hold. Every take-over is therefore a risk, and an excited or careless one is how the cursed gets a chance to throw the caster out (DIV-086).
- **It is upkeep.** A strong will surfaces, and only a caster who is present, undistracted and relentless keeps it under. That is the whole reason Crouch spends the year in a trunk a few feet from Moody rather than anywhere safer.
- **Nobody else has it,** and it goes down with Crouch. It is not a technique in circulation.

## Wands *(settled)* · DIV-024

- **No allegiance.** Wands have no allegiance that passes to whoever defeats their owner. Canon's lore about winning a wand by disarming or overpowering is purged, and nothing in the final duel turns on it.
- **The Elder Wand** is the exception, with lore of its own: it passes by blood, through killing. Voldemort killing Snape is the correct move, and pointless. Only the idea is set. The canon chain doesn't fit it (Grindelwald and Dumbledore never killed for it), and who masters it at the end is open: Q-011.
- **Two casters, one wand.** Harry's hand over hers on her wand, in resonance (DIV-021, DIV-023).
- **Wand rules** in general, including what "the wand chooses the wizard" means now: Q-027.
