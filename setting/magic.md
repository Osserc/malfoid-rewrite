# Magic

Canon by default. This file records where our magic works differently, and the rules that follow from it.

## Spellcasting: radicals 🔒 (principle) · DIV-007

Spellcasting is technical. Wand motions draw "radicals" that combine into spells.

Design still open (Q-015): which radicals exist, how they combine, what incantations add, how nonverbal and wandless casting work, and what makes a spell hard.

## Time 🔒 · DIV-002

Time-Turners do not exist. Whether any other time magic exists: Q-004.

## Painted life *(settled)* · DIV-072

Canon's portraits are unchanged: echoes with manners, not people. The one exception is the extinct Dessendre line, whose ancestral gift made paintings that were genuinely alive. Written out in [painted-life](painted-life.md).

## Dementors, and Lustrum *(settled)* · DIV-080

Canon gives no way to destroy a Dementor: a Patronus drives them off and that is the limit. Ours can be killed.

### Lustrum

A cousin of Fiendfyre. Where Fiendfyre destroys everything, **Lustrum burns what a person owes.** The incantation is *Lustra nefas* — "purify the sacrilege". McGonagall's signature spell in the first war, and the reason the other side called her Hellfire ([her file](../characters/minerva-mcgonagall.md)).

- **It judges, and it is proportional.** It weighs an account and burns in proportion to it. A man with a great deal to answer for goes up as ash; someone with little is singed. Grass, trees and stone are untouched — the fire does not spread and nothing ordinary catches.
- **Dementors are almost nothing but account,** which is why they go up like tinder, and why a powerful enough casting melts them to cinders. Their remains burn anything that touches them for a long time afterwards.
- **It always burns the caster, too.** The fire weighs whoever casts it and takes its price out of them on the spot. There is no way to cast it clean. That is the catch, and it is intrinsic — McGonagall's contribution is only that she stopped resenting it and called it penance.
- **It is not a duelling spell.** Slow, wide, telegraphed, and it can backfire badly enough to incapacitate the caster. Against a wizard who can move or shield it is close to useless. It is something you drop on a position — which is why her war record is raids on houses and gatherings rather than duels.
- **It does not pick sides.** Everything inside the burn is weighed, including whoever is standing with the caster. Against Dementors on an empty lawn that costs nothing; in a corridor, in a crowd, in a battle fought among your own students, it is the reason the spell stays in its sheath. Nobody who casts it can promise what it will do to the person beside them.
- **Nobody knows what it is.** Extremely rare, and not in the library: Hermione can find nothing on it. The Ministry regulates the incantation, which is how Fudge can call its use unauthorised.
- **The enhancer.** The small sun over the castle in B3 is not the spell alone. It takes a charmed trinket, custom-made and extremely expensive, which McGonagall had Flitwick make at the start of that year. Without it the spell kills a handful at a time — which is also why the thing is dangerous to own, and why she surrenders it to him in December rather than keep carrying it.
- **What sets the price:** Q-104. **Whether it touches things that are spells rather than selves** — Horcruxes, Inferi — is explicitly **not settled**: Q-103.

### The Dementors

- **They can be destroyed,** and the Ministry knows it. They are a dangerous magical creature, not a fixture of the universe: huntable, the way wizards hunted the British giants to nothing. Lustrum is McGonagall's method and the one the books show, not the only one there is. The Ministry keeps them anyway, because they are useful ([azkaban](../problems/azkaban.md)).
- **They can be hurt.** A strong enough Patronus does real damage, and a wounded Dementor is a starving one. That is what turns Harry's Patronus at the lake into a siege (DIV-081).
- **They are not one thing** *(settled)* · DIV-083. They have factions, and the first war split them. Canon gestures at Dementors feeling fury and talking among themselves and never follows it up; we do.
  - **Some went over to Voldemort and some did not.** Azkaban kept running on a reduced contingent, which is why the prison needs no explaining away, and his side gained a real force. It also removes canon's worst piece of timing.
  - **The ones who stayed were never loyal.** Azkaban's despair is a particular vintage — concentrated, renewable, served daily — and they had acquired a taste for it. They are gourmands, which is worse than traitors, and it is the heaviest single charge against the place: the things that guard it prefer it.
  - **The defectors** were hunted. McGonagall and others like her destroyed a great many, and the remnants were put back in Azkaban, because they are an asset.
- **The leash** *(settled)* · DIV-084. What put them back, and what holds them now, had three lives.
  1. **A Malfoy ancestor's defence.** Ancient, clean, and kind: it gets under a Dementor's skin and makes it want to be elsewhere. A way to push them off a house, off a road, off a child.
  2. **Macnair's collar.** In the first war Lucius brings the find to Voldemort and Macnair turns it round — the same magic saying *come here* instead of *go away*. He led the horde with it.
  3. **The Ministry's two purchases.** The clean original from Lucius, as the price of the Imperius story being believed, which it now uses openly and extensively — human guards at Azkaban, habitable Ministry buildings, and in B3 the thing the Hogwarts garrison was meant to be held off the students with. And Macnair himself, as the only defector who can work the cursed version.
  So the Ministry's hold is one employee, and Fudge's B5 line is wrong rather than false (DIV-085). Policy in [azkaban](../problems/azkaban.md).

## Dark Magic runs on intent *(draft)* · DIV-084

Canon's own rule, never stated as one: the curse that tortures does not work on righteous anger, only on genuinely wanting to cause pain. We make it general. **Dark Magic is powered by what the caster wants, not by what the caster knows.** Technique helps; appetite is the requirement.

- **It explains Macnair.** A brute with no scholarship takes a defensive spell and makes it a yoke, because the only qualification was wanting it that way. It is also why nobody decent ever found the trick: you have to want it first, and most people never do.
- **It explains Umbridge** doing the same thing twelve years later with no more training than he had (DIV-085).
- **It is not a licence.** Power still runs in lines ([society](society.md)) and the Dark Arts still crack the seal and let ambient magic in, at a price (Q-027). Intent decides whether dark magic *takes*, not how strong you are.

## Love as magic

🔒 The payoff (DIV-006): in B7, love rebounds the Killing Curse.

### Sacrifice *(settled)* · DIV-026

Harry's death in the forest protects no one. Unlike canon, it doesn't stop Voldemort's spells from taking hold on the defenders. It opens the way to Voldemort's destruction, but everyone still has to play their part (see [themes](../core/themes.md)).

Lily's protection is canon. Why her sacrifice protects and his doesn't, and how resonance relates to both, is open: Q-014.

### Resonance *(settled)* · DIV-021

A wand resonates when two people wield it in total sync, and their magic becomes exponentially stronger. It's literally the power of love.

- **Only love.** Friendship isn't enough, or resonance would be more common. Harry and Hermione could never do it. It's obscure, extremely rare and extremely hard to pull off.
- **Sync.** Both draw the same radicals at the same instant, with no leader. That's almost impossible between two people. Harry and Dracaena can do it because of years of feuding and duelling, spent learning each other's every move, much of it at the Duelling Club (DIV-028).
- **Volatile.** No instant win. The state shows cracks, but it holds.
- **Occlumency.** In resonance, Dracaena wraps Harry's mind in hers. Voldemort's Legilimency finds nothing but her love for Harry.
- **The stained soul.** Until the forest, Harry's Horcrux blocks it. They come close a few times, and each time she's hurt by the stained, mutilated soul she senses in him, without either of them knowing what it is. Full resonance only becomes possible once the Killing Curse in the forest destroys that fragment.
- *(draft)* **The stolen edge.** By her line, Dracaena is the more powerful ([society](society.md)). Harry evens it with instinct (DIV-028) and with the piece of Voldemort in him, which gives him an edge (how: Q-027). The irony: part of his power comes from his enemy, and it's the same piece that hurts her in the near-misses. In the forest he loses it. Alone he's weaker, but full resonance opens, and together they come out more powerful than ever.
- **How it builds** across the books is kept vague until a dedicated session (Q-028). So far: a first flicker at the B3 lake (DIV-018), and one wand in two hands in the final duel (🔒 DIV-006).
- **Nobody knows.** In school no one knows what resonance is. Dracaena works out the rule herself, by research, and tells no one.
- **Never disarmed.** When they're together they're each other's strength. Harry doesn't need a wand of his own, because hers is theirs.
- *(draft)* **The link.** Drawing the same radicals with no leader opens a link between the two minds. The link is what multiplies their power, what lets her extend her Occlumency over him, what brings her into contact with the stained soul, and what can crack.

Where the near-misses happen across B1–B6, and how she finds the rule: Q-028.

### The veela charm *(settled)* · DIV-037

A veela's charm has no hold on someone who's already in love. Canon never says either way.

- **Harry** is immune by the 1994 World Cup. Fresh off the B3 lake, where she saved his godfather, he sees Dracaena again and can't explain to himself why he's happy to. He's in love before he knows it ([harry-dracaena](../threads/harry-dracaena.md)).
- **Who knows.** It's veela lore. Fleur knows it, and so does Dracaena. Harry doesn't.
- **Dracaena notices** at the World Cup that he's unmoved, so she knows he's in love. She has no way of knowing it's with her, and assumes it's Cho (DIV-041).
- **The married men** in the Top Box: Arthur Weasley and Lucius Malfoy are unmoved too. Who else notices: Q-050.
- **Fleur's test.** In B4 she turns her charm on Harry to find out whether his heart is taken (DIV-038).
- **Separate from resonance.** It takes no wand and no sync: it's a limit of the charm, not something two people do.

### Harry's dance *(settled)* · DIV-054

From B7, Harry Apparates Dracaena by dancing with her. She can't Apparate ([apparate-dancing](../scenes/b7/apparate-dancing.md)).

- **Side-Along** is canon: anyone who can Apparate can take along someone who can't, hauled along, squeezed, with a crack.
- **The turn.** Apparition is a turn on the spot (canon), and a waltz is a chain of turns. Each turn of the dance is the Apparition turn. Neither of them is dragged: it feels like a pull, a slide across the floor, not a squeeze.
- **The wish.** What drives it is that he wants to go somewhere *with her*: "I wished we could go somewhere together." That's why it only works with her.
- **The sound.** A *swoosh*, not canon's crack: the tell that his technique is different. On his own, Harry still cracks.
- **No wand.** Apparition takes none, so his hands are free for her.
- **A feat.** Chaining Apparitions on the beat, both of them, without a Splinch, is very hard. Ron and Hermione doubt many wizards alive could do it.
- **Not resonance.** It's all in Harry's mind: he casts alone and leads. There's no shared wand and no link between them, so the stained soul never comes into it and it doesn't hurt her (see *Resonance* above).

## Brooms amplify the rider *(settled)* · DIV-068

Canon treats brooms as a ladder: a better broom is faster and better at everything, and the best broom in the world is simply the best. Ours ties power to obedience, so a broom transmits its rider instead of carrying them.

- **Cheap brooms are forgiving.** They're stable, placid and slow, and they quietly ignore half of what they're told, which is exactly what makes them safe to learn on.
- **Top brooms are responsive to the point of wildness.** They do precisely what they're asked, including what the rider didn't mean to ask. Power and obedience arrive together, and there's no version that is fast and docile.
- **Assistance and obedience are different things,** and the two great lines sit at opposite ends of it. A Nimbus is *opinionated*: its flying charm manages the small corrections — angling into a turn, holding a line through a gust — so the rider can spend attention on scouting and duelling instead, and it will quietly decline an instruction it reads as a mistake. A Firebolt does nothing for you and refuses nothing. It simply obeys, stray thoughts included.
- **So the better broom costs more to fly.** A Firebolt is a standing tax on attention: everything you think, the broom does. What you buy with that is that nothing is off the table, including things the charm on a Nimbus would have talked you out of.
- **So money buys amplification, not performance.** A great broom magnifies whatever the rider already is, in both directions. A rich mediocrity is worse off on a Firebolt than on a Cleansweep, and knows it.
- **Temperament, not just skill.** A wild broom suits Harry, who flies the way he duels, on instinct and improvisation (DIV-028). It's the wrong instrument for Dracaena, who is precise and controlled. She could buy a Firebolt tomorrow and be slower on it than on her Nimbus, and she knows it: a broom that manages the details for her is exactly what a cerebral flier wants, and she is the better technician of the two.
- **What it looks like on the page.** The B3 final runs the whole rule twice (DIV-069). Harry spends the match minding his Firebolt and is nearly spent by the end, while Dracaena never fights hers for a second. He catches a falling hair tie because the broom gives him the reach he never asked for out loud, and he wins the Cup riding it upside down, which is the same trick at the scale that matters. The only thing it costs her is that she would never have tried it.
- **The gap at the ceiling is small.** A Firebolt and a Nimbus 2001 are both top racing brooms, and the Firebolt's margin only shows at the very edge of what either can do — a place almost nobody flies. Reaching that edge is the rider's doing.
- **It explains canon.** Lucius buys seven top-of-the-line brooms and his team loses to Gryffindor on older ones anyway, repeatedly, Dracaena included (B2). Canon plays it as a joke about money not buying skill. We make it the rule, and the reader has watched it work twice before the B3 final.
- **Why it matters.** Quidditch stops being decided by chequebooks without anyone having to regulate equipment, so canon's Firebolt subplot survives intact, and the B3 final is hers to lose (DIV-069).

## Sloughsilk *(draft)* · DIV-064

The Lullasp is a snake said to straddle the waking and the dreaming realms. Its sloughed skin, sloughsilk, has peculiar magical properties, the useful one being that it takes growing charms extraordinarily well: a sloughsilk garment adapts to its wearer as they sleep and grows with them, so a set made for a newborn still fits at fifteen.

- **A Black tradition.** A sloughsilk set for a new baby is a Black family custom. Narcissa had one made for Dracaena — pyjamas, a snake-headed hood, and snake slippers, which are not traditional at all. Sirius had one as a child, which Harry can ask him about.
- **It does nothing for sleep.** The dreaming-realm lore is where the creature's name comes from, not a property of the cloth. Harry's nightmares are not solved by what anyone is wearing (DIV-061).

## Legilimency and Occlumency *(settled)* · DIV-020

- **Not mind reading.** Legilimency perceives thoughts, feelings and memories (canon). What it perceives can be shaped.
- **Passive Legilimency.** Voldemort can sense the people in front of him without casting with a wand. With his own followers he usually has no reason to do more.
- **Tweaking.** A skilled Occlumens can tweak what passive Legilimency perceives. Lying outright to Voldemort's face takes far more: Snape can, Lucius can't. Voldemort's arrogance helps both of them.
- **The Malfoys.** No one is born an Occlumens, but the discipline the Malfoys instil lends itself to learning it.
- **Dracaena** learns it from Snape in summer 1995. Against Voldemort in summer 1996 her walls break with ease (DIV-015). In the final duel she doesn't wall him out: she wraps Harry's mind in hers (DIV-021).

## Wands *(settled)* · DIV-024

- **No allegiance.** Wands have no allegiance that passes to whoever defeats their owner. Canon's lore about winning a wand by disarming or overpowering is purged, and nothing in the final duel turns on it.
- **The Elder Wand** is the exception, with lore of its own: it passes by blood, through killing. Voldemort killing Snape is the correct move, and pointless. Only the idea is set. The canon chain doesn't fit it (Grindelwald and Dumbledore never killed for it), and who masters it at the end is open: Q-011.
- **Two casters, one wand.** Harry's hand over hers on her wand, in resonance (DIV-021, DIV-023).
- **Wand rules** in general, including what "the wand chooses the wizard" means now: Q-027.
