# Wizarding Society

Canon by default. This file records where society works differently.

## Pureblood ideology 🔒 (principle) · DIV-008

The ideologues have an actual point. What it is, and where it turns into Voldemort's ideology: Q-016. House-elves are the hardest test case: [problems/house-elves.md](../problems/house-elves.md).

*(draft)* **Lead from DIV-035.** Wizarding Britain really is disappearing, and it really does live off the Muggles. The self-sufficient pure-blood estate, owing Muggles nothing, is a dream of independence. That gives Voldemort's promise to take it all back real credence, though his war, by killing so many, made the decline worse. The turn into his ideology is the blame: Muggle-borns are the only inflow slowing the decline, and the ideologues attack them.

*(draft)* **Lead: power runs in lines.** This is what the pure-bloods are right about. As a rule, the less magical your ancestry, the weaker your magic. What counts is the line, not the fraction: one prominent magical line on one side can carry a half-blood (Harry through the Potters), and Muggle-borns have none. Voldemort and Snape have something darker on top: the Dark Arts (Q-027). Practice raises power and endurance somewhat, but not much without the aptitude.
- **Not worth, only power.** A Muggle-born can still be an excellent witch. Hermione is the proof: immense technical skill, and excellent at charms and enchantments that need precision rather than power. She's the brains of the operation and a support, not the magical muscle ([hermione-granger](../characters/hermione-granger.md)).
- **On the page early.** At the Duelling Club, Dracaena wipes the floor with Hermione ([duelling-club](../threads/duelling-club.md)).
- **The dilemma.** Muggle-borns are the only inflow slowing the decline, and they're weaker on average. Where the ideologues' answer turns suicidal: Q-048.

## Population and economy *(draft)* · DIV-035

Wizarding Britain is small and shrinking. The figures are illustrative: something like seventy pupils a year in peacetime, and lifespans of well over a century, give roughly ten thousand people. Canon's single institutions (one school, one bank, one hospital, one newspaper, one prison, one wandmaker) are what a community that size looks like: a market town scattered across a country, with Diagon Alley as its high street. Ollivander making about a hundred wands a year is right for forty to eighty eleven-year-olds.

- **The long decline.** The castle was built for a far larger magical population. Since the Statute of Secrecy, people have married out and drifted into the Muggle world, and the pure-blood lines have thinned. Wizarding Britain lives in the ruins of a once-thriving world ([themes](../core/themes.md)).
- **The war dip.** The first war (c. 1970–1981) hollowed out the cohorts born in it. Whole families were murdered, young adults died, and people put off having children until it was over. The real-world precedent: French births roughly halved during the First World War, and those hollow cohorts (*classes creuses*) showed up twenty years later as a shortage of conscripts. The Potters, Longbottoms and Weasleys having children anyway was an act of defiance.
- **The Muggle-born share.** Muggle-born births don't dip, because the war happens out of Muggle sight. So the war years are smaller and proportionally more Muggle-born.
- **The boom.** Births recover after 1981, and the post-war cohorts reach Hogwarts from about 1993 ([hogwarts](hogwarts.md)).
- **Magic multiplies labour.** Cooking, cleaning, building, repairs and transport take a wand or Apparition, so a few thousand witches and wizards can run what would take a much larger Muggle town. Most of the economy is crafts, services and government.
- **The Ministry is sized for the Muggles.** Obliviators, Muggle Liaison, Misuse of Muggle Artefacts, Accidental Magic Reversal: most departments police the border with tens of millions of Muggles, not ten thousand wizards. That's why the bureaucracy looks oversized.
- **It lives off the Muggle economy.** Food can't be conjured (Gamp's Law, B7). Bread, meat, cloth and paper come from Muggles, bought with Muggle money that Gringotts gets by quietly selling gold. Exports, such as brooms, potions and wands sold abroad, make up the rest.
- **Not the elves.** House-elf labour doesn't quietly make the economy work. That would make pure-blood society indefensible and undercut DIV-008. Their fate: [problems/house-elves.md](../problems/house-elves.md).
- **The way out** of the decline, after the war: Q-048.
