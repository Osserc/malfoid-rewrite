# Hogwarts

Canon by default. This file records where the school works differently.

## Size · DIV-035

Why wizarding Britain is small and shrinking: [society](society.md). What that means for the school:

- **A third full.** The castle was built for about a thousand pupils, the figure Rowling has given. In the 1990s it holds perhaps a third of that. Hence canon's endless disused classrooms and empty corridors.
- **About forty a year.** Every pupil at Hogwarts during the series was born during the first war, and Harry's year is about forty. The war years are proportionally more Muggle-born.
- **The boom.** Children born after the war start arriving from about 1993, and each Sorting runs a little longer than the last. It's a quiet sign of peace, and in B7 the castle is fuller of young children than it has been in years.
- **On the page,** one line is enough: Hermione, who has read *Hogwarts: A History*, notices the castle was built for far more pupils than it has. After that the prose keeps crowds modest. The Great Hall is never full.
- **Still the only school.** Hogwarts stays the only wizarding school in Britain (canon). Why we didn't add more: [problems/scale.md](../problems/scale.md).

## Classes · DIV-034

- **The whole year together.** Each year is taught as one class, all four Houses at once: about forty pupils, a big class rather than a crowd. One teacher per subject (canon) can cover it.
- **Electives** are subsets of the year, timetabled in blocks. Two of Hermione's choices clash in B3, which gives her the excuse to drop Muggle Studies (DIV-012, Q-002).
- **The clumps.** The classroom starts out in four clumps by House, the Great Hall's tables in miniature. The Houses stay fiercely tribal everywhere else: tables, dormitories, points, Quidditch. How far the clumps have dissolved by B5 is the visible measure of the unity theme ([themes](../core/themes.md)). Teacher favouritism, like Snape awarding Slytherin points in front of three other Houses, keeps the resentment alive.
- **Pairs.** The practical part of a lesson is done in pairs. Left to themselves, pupils pair within their House at first, and the clumps have dissolved when they start crossing the lines on their own. Teachers can force specific pairings, and each has their own habits: one pairs to punish, another puts a weak pupil with a bright one, another pairs the best together to push them further. Forced pairings give "among hostiles" on demand: Ron stuck with a Slytherin, Hermione with Pansy.
  - **Snape** pairs Harry with Dracaena, thinking he's handing her a chew toy. In time it gives them space together instead, and the Potions servant routine grows out of it (DIV-017). What he makes of his own handiwork: Q-043.
  - **Flitwick** pairs them at the Duelling Club because iron sharpens iron ([duelling-club](../threads/duelling-club.md)).
- **Lupin's Boggart lesson** goes House by House, so no House sees another's fears ([boggart-lesson](../scenes/b3/boggart-lesson.md), DIV-042).
- **Norm or reform:** Q-047.

## Common rooms and the holidays · DIV-034

- **In term,** each common room is a haven for its own House, away from the competition. No guests.
- **Over the holidays,** with the castle nearly empty, the few who stay can mix and bunk together, by long tradition. Canon's B3 Christmas, with everyone at one table in the Great Hall, is the precedent. That keeps the Christmas mornings: in B1 Harry opens the Cloak with Ron there.
