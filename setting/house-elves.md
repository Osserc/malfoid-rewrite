# House-Elves

What house-elves are, where they came from, and why nobody remembers. This is the home of the rules; the beats that use them live in their scene files.

The canon problem this answers: [problems/house-elves.md](../problems/house-elves.md). Ledger: DIV-075 (what they are), DIV-076 (B2), DIV-077 (Winky), DIV-078 (Kreacher), DIV-050 (Dobby's death).

## What they are *(settled)* · DIV-075

A house-elf is not a magical creature. It is a house.

Where one family line has lived in one house long enough, the house takes on the household's temperament and character and puts it into a body. The elf is what the building has become from being lived in: its upkeep, its warmth and its memory, walking about. It is made of the accumulated line, and the living household tilts it — which is why an elf is a fair portrait of the family that made it, in character and in looks both. A Malfoy elf is fair-haired and grey-eyed. A Black elf is dark.

- **They do not reproduce.** There are no young elves, no old ones, no mating, no habitat, no wild population. There never were.
- **They do not die in the ordinary way.** A body can be destroyed; the house makes another. The spirit is the house and persists as long as the house and the line do.
- **One house, one elf.** A house may have many bodies walking about it, but there is only ever one of it. Bodies scale with the demesne: a manor supports one, a castle holding a thousand children supports a hundred.
- **So there are very few.** Only old houses with unbroken lines have them, which is a few dozen in Britain, plus the castle's many. They are not and never could be an economic base ([society](society.md), DIV-035).
- **None has been made since the binding** *(settled)* · Q-097. Making a whole elf is the work of an unbroken line in one house over a span nobody has managed since, and the binding may have broken the capacity outright. Nobody knows, because nobody knows elves are made at all. Every elf alive is one of the originals.
- **So they are dwindling and cannot be replaced.** As houses empty and lines thin, elves lose bodies and are never added to. Hogwarts has fewer than it had. Nobody has noticed, because nobody counts them.

## The demesne *(settled)* · DIV-075

Their power is enormous and almost entirely local.

- **Within the house** they can do very nearly anything to it and to the things in it: build, repair, clean, move, hide, heat, extend. Given material, an elf can enlarge its own house. It cannot conjure from nothing.
- **They always know when the household calls.** Anywhere in the demesne, and at any distance, if the call comes from one of their own people.
- **Anchored, not confined.** They can go out, and the further they go the weaker they get. Projecting beyond the house costs them. This is why elf errands in the wider world are small and brief, and why a travelling elf is a feeble thing compared to the same elf at home.

## What sustains them *(settled)* · DIV-075, Q-102

The house feeds the elf. **A housed elf eats nothing, and never has.**

- **The body is real.** Flesh, not a ghost's semblance — and the house maintains it the way it maintains its own roof. Nothing goes in, and nothing is needed. The castle's elf has cooked for a thousand years and has never tasted anything it made.
- **So no elf has ever been hungry, and no elf has ever been paid,** and those are the same fact. Wages buy food and lodging; the house is both. Money offered to a housed elf is a thing it has no use for, which is most of why the offer has never been made and why nobody has ever had to think about it. **It stays that way.** Nobody is ever employed, by anyone, including Dobby: canon's wage from Dumbledore is cut (DIV-077). The only elf in Britain who is kept costs money to keep, and the person paying has never once thought of it as paying.
- **An unhoused elf has to eat.** With no house behind it, the body falls back on matter like anything else's. Every dismissed elf is unhoused for the few weeks it takes to come apart, and none of them has ever cared. Winky is unhoused for eight months and refuses. Dobby is unhoused for five years, and is the only elf in a thousand years who has had to feed himself and wanted to.
- **Food sustains the body, not the spirit.** It keeps an unhoused elf from starving and does nothing whatever for the thing that is actually running out. Dobby can be the best-fed creature in Britain and still be dying on schedule (DIV-050).

## The keeping *(settled)* · DIV-075

In their original form the relation had nothing to do with ownership. The elf **kept** the house and the people in it — upkeep and protection both — because that is what it was for and what it wanted. The people are **the household**, never masters.

Our terms: *the household*, *keeping*, *its people*. On the page an elf says **family**. "Masters" is the vocabulary of the binding, and every elf alive uses it, which is the point.

## The binding *(settled)* · DIV-075

A long time ago — before Hogwarts — wizards used dark magic to bind the elves: so that they could not refuse an order, and could not leave. The keeping became service. The household became masters.

- **It took the obedience and never touched the will.** This is the cruelty of it. The elf still wants to help, still loves its people, and now also cannot do otherwise. Everything that looks like devotion in a bound elf is real, and is also a chain, and the elf cannot tell which is which.
- **What is left of the will lives in the gaps between orders.** An elf can do anything it was not told not to do, at the price of punishing itself for it. That is the whole of Dobby's conduct in B2, and the whole of the kindness any bound elf manages.
- **They degraded.** Over centuries the binding dwindled them. They were once near-human, tall and fair; they are now the small, bulging, rag-wearing things everyone knows. A bound elf is a cheap thing to run. The true shape is expensive, and needs a whole house to sustain it.
- **The house is what grips.** The chain has three links — elf, house, household — and the house holds it.
- **There is nothing to break.** The binding was cast once, long ago, and what it sits on now is the elves. There is no seat of it: no artefact, no vault, no circle, nothing anyone could find and destroy. The only instrument still standing is the house itself — so when anyone speaks of the seat of an elf's binding they mean a *building*, and Grimmauld Place is the seat of Kreacher's in that sense and in no other. It is why force was never going to undo this, and why the answer, when it comes, has to be procedural (Q-100).
- **The knowledge is gone.** Nobody now living knows how it was done, which means nobody knows how to undo it, and nobody knows that an elf could be *transferred* rather than freed. It was once possible to hand one on, still bound, to a new house.

### The exit, and the glitch *(settled)* · DIV-075

**A dismissed elf can live.** It needs a new household to take it — normally a witch or wizard with a house, which re-anchors the elf and binds it properly again. That is not a secret and not forbidden; it is simply never done (see *The two locks*). It may have happened a handful of times in a thousand years, and nothing records it, because both parties are disgraced by it: the elf begged, and the family admitted it could not make its own. Which is another reason Hermione finds nothing.

**Harry is not that.** He is a master with **no house**, which the curse has no provision for, because nobody casting it imagined such a thing. So the hold catches, and catches wrong.

- **A partial bind.** Dobby is anchored to a person and to no demesne. He has an owner and no house to draw on, which is why he is feeble everywhere, why he can lodge at the Burrow without belonging to it, and why the Manor's claim on him never quite lapsed.
- **It is killing him, slowly.** A proper bind would have held him indefinitely, small and cheap and alive. The partial one lets him revert towards his true shape, and the true shape needs a house he has not got. The five-year unravelling is the gap between those two facts (DIV-050).
- **Clothes do nothing to him now.** Dismissal works through the house, and there is no house. Harry could not free him a second time if he tried, and Dobby could walk away any day he chose. He doesn't, which makes Harry a shelter rather than an owner and makes every day Dobby stays a choice he renews.
- **It is a bug, not a loophole.** An oversight by the original casters, a thousand years old, sitting in the Burrow eating toast. **And a bug means the thing has seams** — which is the first real evidence anyone has ever had that this magic can be worked at all, and the ground Hermione's method is built on (Q-100).

## The forgetting, and what everyone "knows" *(settled)* · DIV-075

Both sides forgot. The elves have no history of themselves; the wizards have a kindness story in place of one. The noble houses buried their own shame so thoroughly that no one alive knows there was ever anything to bury, and the cover-up itself has been forgotten along with the crime. Every account of elves anyone can find begins with elves already in service, already like this, as though they had always been furniture.

What everyone does know, as folk knowledge with no explanation attached: **a dismissed elf does away with itself.**

- **What actually happens.** Freed from the house, an elf has nothing to be, and comes apart. **How long it takes depends entirely on how much it still wants.** Nothing is counting down. The elf is letting go, and the rate of dissolution is the rate at which it lets go.
  - **Dobby goes in minutes,** because he had wanted to go for decades and could do nothing about it while he was bound. The sock did not kill him; it opened a door he had been standing at for a very long time, and he went through it at once. What Harry does in that corridor is talk an elf off a ledge, not pull one out of an accident (DIV-076).
  - **Winky takes eight months,** and that is the ordinary way. She does not want to live either, but she has not finished *wanting* — her master back, the verdict overturned, to be worth housing — so she does not release. She erodes, conscious of it, the whole time (DIV-077).
  - **So the quick death is the merciful one,** and almost nobody gets it. The reader meets the fast version first and takes it for what dismissal is; B4 shows them the real thing, which is far worse and is what Crouch actually ordered.
- **And nothing marks them.** A thousand years and not one elf grave in Britain, because housed elves do not die and nobody has ever minded the ones that did. The nearest thing to an elf memorial in the country is the row of severed heads on the stairs at Grimmauld Place: a house's own discarded bodies, mounted by the house itself, and called an honour ([kreacher-and-the-manor](../scenes/b7/kreacher-and-the-manor.md)). **The first real one is Winky's,** at Dobby's asking, in 1995 (DIV-077).
- **Why it looks like suicide.** It is near enough to one. A dismissed elf *wants* to go, because being dismissed is being told by the thing you are made of that you are not worth housing. And the one remedy — asking some other household to take you — is so shameful that an elf will choose dissolution over it, every time but one.
- **So the wizards' version is cruel and roughly true.** Crouch dismissing Winky is not a man disposing of a witness. It is a man telling his elf to go and kill itself, in a society where that is a known and unremarkable thing to say.
- **But knowing is not the same as having seen.** Nobody watches. The elf goes off and does it somewhere else, and what is left is dust, and so the version told in good houses — the one passed down to children — is simply that the elf *goes away*. Both accounts are current at once: the adults know what it means, the nursery version is soft, and the softness is load-bearing. It is part of what makes Crouch's dismissal of Winky shocking even to people who find the fact unremarkable: he said it out loud, in front of witnesses, and made everyone present look at it.
- **And everybody is complicit.** They are not ignorant. They know what dismissal does. They have simply never once asked why.

## The two locks *(settled)* · DIV-075

The way out exists and is available to every elf in Britain, and nobody takes it, because there are two locks on it and both have to come off.

1. **A dismissed elf does not want to live.** Being dismissed is the thing you are made of telling you that you are not worth housing, and the elf agrees with it. This is the real lock. Winky never gets past it.
2. **And it would not ask even if it did.** An elf does not beg. The shame of asking a strange household to take a cast-off is past bearing, and the household would refuse anyway.

What takes the first lock off is not hope offered but **grief witnessed**: somebody minding that you are going. Nobody has ever minded. The second comes off on its own once the first does, because an elf with something to live for will debase itself for it.

It happens once, in a corridor in B2 ([the-sock](../scenes/b2/the-sock.md), DIV-076), and what does it is a twelve-year-old crying.

That is the answer to the problem, and it is deliberately not a solution. Nothing is undone, nothing generalises, and nobody who could act on it ever understands what happened — except that it leaves a working anomaly in plain sight for anyone clever enough to study it.

The *beginning* of a real solution is Hermione's, late and partial and procedural: not a spell that frees an elf but a way for one to recover its natural state over time, in a household that has decided never to use what it holds. It is her S.P.E.W. questline and it is not designed yet (Q-100).

What it is **not** is a repeat of the corridor. Harry's accident worked once, on one elf, because the two of them happened to be the right people in the right week standing on the right seam, and Hermione is clever enough to see that it cannot be extended. She takes the anomaly as proof that the magic has seams, and then goes and finds a different one.

## Dobby *(settled)*

Full beats: [the-sock](../scenes/b2/the-sock.md) (B2) · [malfoy-manor](../scenes/b7/malfoy-manor.md) (his death).

- **Ur-Dobby is Malfoy Manor** and has been for as long as there have been Malfoys in it. Nobody knows this, him least of all. The Dobby anyone meets is one manifestation of it.
- **He was a coward for centuries** — fearful, cringing, bound to be helpful. What he is now came from Narcissa: she married in, she had Dracaena, she was cursed and could have no more children, and the house took the shape of her wanting. His kindness is hers, and neither of them knows it. His dream of a great house full of a happy, kind family is her dream, worn by the house she lives in.
- **He has a soft spot for Dracaena** and smuggles her sweets against Lucius's wishes — which he can do because no order ever forbade it.
- **Lucius knew what the sock meant,** and discarded it anyway. Not malice and not temper: he takes a filthy Muggle sock off the diary and flings it aside as rubbish, and Dobby catches it. The agency is Dobby's and the indifference is Lucius's, and that is the whole indictment. His parting line to Harry — *I hope you know what you have just done, Mr Potter* — is the only warning anyone gives, and Harry can't read it ([the-sock](../scenes/b2/the-sock.md)).
- **He is the only unhoused elf there has ever been**, anchored to a person instead of a building, and the configuration only works because Harry has no house for the curse to grip. Neither Privet Drive nor the Burrow is his: one he hates, one is borrowed.
- **He lives at the Burrow** from B2 — lodging, not a demesne. He is weak everywhere.
- **He is hungry, for the first time in his existence.** As far back as he can remember he never ate anything: he was a spirit in a house, and spirits do not eat. Unhoused, the body the binding gave him behaves like any other body and starts asking (Q-102). Nobody tells him what the feeling is, because nobody alive knows there is anything to tell.
- **He never asks anyone for work or wages.** He has a person, which is the whole of what he has ever wanted, and being kept by Harry is not a humiliation to be worked off — it is the arrangement. Wanting to be independent of the one who feeds you is a wizard's instinct and he has no access to it (DIV-077).
- **The first meal is Molly's cooking,** at the Burrow, in the summer of 1993. Not a house's output: a woman standing at a stove, working, by hand, for him. He is the first elf in a thousand years to be fed by somebody, and he bawls his eyes out. **Harry is not there** — he spends that summer in Diagon Alley — so Dobby tells him about it instead, at length and at speed, and Harry cannot work out why it matters so much.
- **The false dawn.** Out of the yoke and fed for the first time, he feels better than he has ever felt, and anyone looking at him agrees. Then it thins: across the next four years his powers go by degrees while he eats perfectly well, and the last of them goes at Malfoy Manor. Feeding him never buys him a day (DIV-050).
- **He has Harry's money.** Functionally complete access to the vault: not that he could walk into Gringotts, but the funds are his to call on and Harry never thinks about it twice. It is how he supports Winky, who will not take wages from anyone (DIV-077).
- **And it is how Harry pays the Weasleys.** They will not accept anything from him, so he does it sideways and absurdly: a small room added on for himself that nobody asked for, the structure quietly shored up, a repair here and there, all of it on the most transparent pretexts and all of it paid for. Dobby is already lending Molly a hand; this lets him be the architect he has always wanted to be, which is the oldest thing in him — an elf's first power is enlarging the house it keeps.
- **Which means Harry is spending him.** Every improvement comes out of a thing with no house to draw on. Harry's generosity and Harry's homelessness are killing him together, and neither of them knows it.
- **Except the Manor,** where the residual claim still holds: he is still something of a Malfoy geist, which is why his Apparition works there at all, and works repeatedly, until it doesn't.
- **He unravels, slowly, for five years.** Freed of the house that shrank him, he reverts towards the true shape — and the true shape needs a house to sustain, and he has not got one. So the reversion *is* the dying. He grows taller by degrees across the books and everyone who notices assumes freedom agrees with him. Harry asks him in B3 whether he has got taller. That is the first sign he is dying, and the reader only learns it in B7.
- **His death is real** because he is unhoused: there is no house to make him again, and the last of what he was made of goes with him ([malfoy-manor](../scenes/b7/malfoy-manor.md), DIV-050).

## The Manor *(settled)*

Lucius did not dismiss a servant in B2; he cut his house's spirit loose and did not notice. The claim is residual, not severed — Dobby is still partly the Manor's, which is what makes him strong there in B7 — but the Manor has not had him in it since 1992.

- **It goes cold.** Upkeep, warmth, the sense of a house that is looked after: all of it slowly stops. It becomes a very large building that has to be heated and cleaned like any other.
- **Nobody understands why**, and nobody connects it to an elf. Lucius blames the war when the war finally arrives. It started five years before it.
- **Narcissa feels it most**, and cannot name it.
- **The Manor dies properly when Dobby does**, at Christmas 1997, and **it never makes another** — nothing has been made since the binding (Q-097, answered). Dobby is gone for good. What stands afterwards is a house with nothing in it, and what fills it is a foreign elf: Kreacher colonises the Manor ([kreacher-and-the-manor](../scenes/b7/kreacher-and-the-manor.md), DIV-078).

## Hogwarts *(settled)*

**Helga Hufflepuff brought her own elf.** She took a bound thing out of a house and settled it into a castle, to give it somewhere safe and warm to work, and it took root and has kept the school ever since. (Canon's hook for this is Rowling's supplementary writing, not the books — soft source, used as a hook.)

- **The curse predates Hogwarts,** which is fine and useful: the school is a late chapter of it, not its origin.
- **It explains the headstart.** Nothing had to coalesce. The elf arrived centuries old.
- **It explains the hundred.** A thousand years in the largest inhabited building in magical Britain is an enormous demesne, and bodies scale with the demesne.
- **It explains the character.** The castle's elf feeds people and keeps them warm and is never seen doing it, because it was made in a house that did that.
- **And it is the cover story in miniature.** The school remembers Hufflepuff's kindness. The kindest of the four took a cursed thing and moved it somewhere nicer, and that is the whole of what anyone knows. Every elf story Hermione digs through has this shape: a kindness laid over a crime by people who meant well and never asked.
- **Hogwarts is not a refuge.** It takes in no freed elves and knows nothing the rest of the world doesn't. Dobby and Winky are in the kitchens in B4 as refugees, not as beneficiaries of a policy, and canon never names a single elf native to the castle — the only two with names came from somewhere else.

## Kreacher *(draft)*

Full beats: [kreacher-and-the-manor](../scenes/b7/kreacher-and-the-manor.md) (DIV-078). Summary only here.

- **Ur-Kreacher is Grimmauld Place.** Distinguish the spirit from its manifestations: the lineage of bodies is not a lineage, it is one thing wearing out and being replaced.
- **The heads on the wall are his own.** When a body gets too old, the Blacks order it beheaded; the house takes the body back and mounts a head. Whether a blade was ever actually involved is unclear even to the house — *Ur-Kreacher may have disappeared the bodies himself and set the heads on the plaques*, which is worse and is the preferred version.
- **He mounted every one of them** and does not remember doing it, and dusts them daily, and is proud of the row, and looks forward to joining it.
- **Memory lives in the house, not the body.** He inherits the Blacks' temperament, not their episodes. He does not know he was beheaded; he knows the house thought it was proper. This is the rule that keeps "not even he knows" true.
- **He can see what no human can.** Kreacher is the only character in the series who knows Dobby is dying. He says so, repeatedly, as an insult, and everyone hears spite and ignores him, because he is Kreacher.
- **He is already Harry's**, through Sirius's will, bound to Grimmauld in the ordinary way. So nothing about his ending can turn on being *given* to Harry; he has been Harry's all along, and that is exactly the problem (DIV-078).
- **What gets him out is Hermione's**, not Harry's — a method rather than a kindness, and the beginning of an answer for every elf in Britain. Undesigned: Q-100.

## What this is for

- **DIV-008.** The ideologues are not running a slave economy; they are the custodians of a forgotten atrocity they did not commit and have never examined. That is defensible in the way the rest of DIV-008 needs, and damning in a way that does not require anyone to be a monster.
- **DIV-035.** Elves index the decline. Fewer houses, thinner lines, fewer elves, fewer bodies. They are a symptom, never the engine ([society](society.md)).
- **The spine.** A house-elf is needed and used and never wanted. That is Harry's want exactly ([harry-potter](../characters/harry-potter.md)), which is why he is the only person in the world who knows how to answer it, and why he cannot explain what he did.
- **Hermione gets the history and no remedy. Harry has the remedy, once, and no history.** They never put it together.
